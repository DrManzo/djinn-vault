## Summary
Exploration of creative writing techniques, worldbuilding frameworks, and narrative structure for fiction projects. Covers character development, magic systems, plot architecture, and revision strategies. Includes references to Malazan-inspired complexity and dark fantasy tropes.

## Key Points
- Worldbuilding should serve the story, not overwhelm it; reveal details through character experience
- Magic systems benefit from clear rules, costs, and limitations (Sanderson's Laws)
- Character arcs should intersect with plot progression; internal conflict drives external action
- Dark fantasy thrives on moral ambiguity, flawed protagonists, and consequences that matter
- Revision is where good writing happens; first drafts are for discovery, second drafts for structure
- Reading widely outside genre strengthens craft; study poetry, history, and non-fiction
- Writing schedule matters more than inspiration; consistent output beats sporadic bursts

## Details

### Worldbuilding Framework
Start with one core question: "What if?" Build outward from character needs. Map only what characters will traverse. Develop culture through conflict points (religion vs. state, tradition vs. innovation, scarcity vs. abundance). Avoid info-dumping; embed lore in dialogue, action, and environmental detail.

### Character Development
Protagonist needs: external goal, internal wound, false belief, moment of truth. Antagonist should believe they are the hero of their own story. Supporting characters exist to challenge, reflect, or enable protagonist growth. Give each character a distinct voice through vocabulary, rhythm, and topic preferences.

### Plot Structure
Three-act structure: setup (25%), confrontation (50%), resolution (25%). Within each act: goal → conflict → disaster → reaction → dilemma → decision. Pacing controlled by sentence length, scene length, and information release. Cliffhangers work when earned, not manufactured.

### Dark Fantasy Elements
Moral ambiguity: no purely good or evil choices. Consequences: actions have lasting impact. Flawed protagonists: competence paired with significant weaknesses. Grounded magic: supernatural elements feel earned, not convenient. Violence serves theme, not spectacle.

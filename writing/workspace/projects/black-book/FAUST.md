# Faust — Character & Mythology

> Faust is simultaneously an inner figure (from Black Book psychology work) and a developed fictional character.
> This file holds both dimensions.

---

## The Inner Figure

Faust = the drive, the ambition, the bargain. The part that pushes toward mastery and power at any cost.

In the Jungian framework:
- The **Ancient** holds old pain and defends by closing
- The **Child** holds unmet needs and opens vulnerably
- **Faust** is the force that makes deals with both — trading comfort for power, trading the present for a future

In the Dominion of Pyraxis: the advisor named Faust may be a shadow projection of this inner figure. He manipulates Brax and Javelin, pursuing unknown long-term goals. The connection may be intentional.

## The Fictional Character

**Species:** Gnome
**Archetype:** Wounded Healer

### Origin
Small, frail, overlooked as a child — seen only by his ancient grandmother. After her death, he was chosen by Nurul, the God of Necromancers (not a terrifying figure — appears as a gentle grandfather). Nurul offered him a coin that can call back shadows of the dead.

**First test:** grandmother's death. Faust chose not to use the coin — recognizing that what he would raise would not be her.

**Core truth the coin teaches:** The dead do not leave. They incorporate into the living. The coin is a crutch. True wisdom is understanding that you already carry them.

### Who He Becomes
Eccentric, mad old wounded healer. Travels with a mule named Sorrow. Collects trinkets — each one a transitional object honoring memory without being enslaved by it.

### The Confrontation with Malachar (Deathbinder)
Shadow integration — Faust defeats not through power but through superior understanding. This represents the integration of the shadow archetype through comprehension rather than destruction.

### Ascension
Nurul fades not from failure but from completion — Faust becomes the bridge between understanding and wisdom. Nurul was "divine training wheels." Faust's ascension is ego death without self-destruction: universal consciousness without loss of personal warmth.

His sister (Life) perspective: "Love is not fragile."

### Psychological Dimensions
- Five poet lenses: Blake (innocence/experience), Dickinson (death), Rilke (solitude), Whitman (the body electric), Eliot (the wasteland/resurrection)
- Freudian: death drive transformed into life drive through service
- Jungian: individuation — shadow (Malachar) confronted and integrated
- Peterson: responsibility over resentment; carrying the burden consciously
- Machiavellian: power as contingent, derived from understanding rather than force
- Synthesized: Faust is the author's own transformation mirrored in myth — physical change, shadow work, moving from observation to engagement, becoming whole through integration not suppression

# Identifying Core Business Problems

## Summary
Analysis of the critical realization that identifying the real problem requiring a solution is the foundational step in business strategy. The conversation explores the distinction between symptoms and root causes, and emphasizes the importance of proper problem definition before investing resources in solutions.

## Key Points
1. The real problem identification is the most critical first step in any business venture
2. Symptoms often mask underlying structural or systemic issues that require different solutions
3. Proper problem definition prevents wasted resources on solutions that address the wrong issue
4. Root cause analysis techniques help distinguish between surface-level complaints and fundamental business challenges
5. Strategic planning must begin with accurate problem scoping before solution development
6. Multi-faceted businesses require careful analysis of which division's problems take priority
7. The distinction between operational problems and strategic problems affects resource allocation decisions
8. Stakeholder alignment on problem definition is essential before proceeding with business plan development

## Details

The conversation centers on the realization that the team had been addressing symptoms rather than the core business problem. This insight redirects strategic planning efforts toward proper problem identification. The analysis emphasizes that without correctly defining the problem, any subsequent business plan, financial projection, or operational strategy will be misaligned with actual needs.

For a multi-division company structure (The Forge, Terp Tribe, Asgard Acres), problem identification must occur at both the parent company level and subsidiary level. Each division may have distinct challenges requiring different approaches, and the parent company must determine which problems are systemic versus division-specific.

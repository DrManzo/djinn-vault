# Substance Use And Health Effects

## Summary
This note consolidates scholarly discussion responses exploring the health effects of illegal and legal substance use, including polysubstance use, addiction mechanisms, and the complex relationship between substance use and physical/mental health. It includes responses to classmates about mixing substances, smoking cessation, and the psychological factors behind addiction.

## Key Points
- People with substance use disorders often experience associated health issues including lung disease, heart disease, stroke, cancer, or mental health conditions
- Tobacco smoke causes various cancers; methamphetamine use leads to severe dental problems
- Opioid use carries the risk of overdose and death
- Inhalants can damage nerve cells in the brain and peripheral nervous system
- Prolonged and repeated substance use at high doses can produce substance use disorder
- Substance use disorders range from mild and temporary to severe and chronic (addiction)
- Prevalence of substance use disorders is estimated at 8% to 10% in the general population
- Prevalence reaches up to 70% in emergency or urgent care clinics
- Combining substances can increase risks and lead to higher addiction probability
- Mixing depressants (like alcohol and benzos) can slow breathing, leading to brain damage and death
- Mixing stimulants (like ecstasy and cocaine) increases chances of heart attack and stroke
- Alcohol is associated with liver disease, cancer, and cardiovascular problems
- Cannabis edibles carry risks such as impaired cognitive function and potential exacerbation of mental health issues
- Acute effects like overdose or accidents can occur even with infrequent use
- Purity and potency of illegal substances vary widely, increasing risk for occasional users
- Substance use disorders often co-occur with other mental health conditions
- Denial in addiction is a complex psychological defense mechanism
- Exercise and counseling are effective alternatives to smoking
- Physical activity promotes endorphin release, improving mood and reducing cravings
- Mindfulness practices help develop self-awareness and emotional regulation skills
- Individual biological and genetic differences affect drug reactions
- Social and environmental factors (stress, trauma, lack of social support) contribute to polysubstance use

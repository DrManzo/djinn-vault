# Smoking Cessation And Lung Cancer Prevention

## Summary
This note outlines a benchmark project proposal focused on examining a smoking cessation program aimed at reducing lung cancer incidence within a specific community. It covers the program's design, implementation strategy, key components, and expected outcomes, grounded in current scholarly research on lung cancer prevention.

## Key Points
- Lung cancer is the second most diagnosed cancer and the leading cause of cancer death in the United States
- Smoking is the strongest risk factor for lung cancer, increasing disease incidence up to 20 times
- Recent updates to lung cancer screening guidelines have expanded eligibility for women and racial/ethnic minority groups
- Nearly half of the 14.8 million US adults eligible for lung cancer screening still smoke cigarettes
- Effective smoking cessation programs integrated with lung cancer screening are critical for reducing incidence and mortality
- Standardized tobacco screening questions identify current and recent smokers among ambulatory cancer patients
- Intensive telephone coaching based on the Tobacco Longitudinal Care (TLC) model incorporates chronic care principles
- Combination nicotine replacement therapy (NRT) and prescription medications through medication therapy management (MTM) support cessation
- A 12-month program with follow-up addresses relapse prevention and encourages multiple quit attempts
- Coordinating smoking cessation with lung cancer screening leverages the "teachable moment" of screening
- Interventions should be tailored to address disparities in lung cancer outcomes among racial and ethnic minority groups
- Implementation involves designating regional smoking cessation "champions"
- Training healthcare providers on empathetic interactions with patients who smoke is essential
- Partnerships with external smoking cessation resources strengthen program reach
- Target long-term abstinence rate of at least 16-18% based on previous studies
- Evaluation tracks screening rates, referral acceptance, and long-term abstinence rates
- Lifestyle choices significantly influence cancer risk — excess weight, sedentary lifestyles, smoking, and alcohol consumption all increase risk
- Early detection through screening significantly improves survival rates
- Creating environments that promote healthier lifestyles can subtly nudge people toward healthier choices

## Details
This benchmark project addresses programmatic competency 3.4: Incorporate sociocultural factors in scientific inquiry. The project requires a 1,000-1,500 word reflective critique paper analyzing a prevention plan targeting lifestyle changes and risk factors within a distinct community.

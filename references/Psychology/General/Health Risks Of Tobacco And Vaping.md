# Health Risks Of Tobacco And Vaping

## Summary
This scholarly discussion response examines the health risks associated with tobacco products (cigars, cigarettes, pipes) and e-cigarettes/vaping throughout an individual's lifespan. It includes responses to classmates' posts about smoking health risks and the intersection of health, psychology, and environmental design.

## Key Points
- Cigar smoking is linked to coronary heart disease and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)
- Cigar smoking confers similar cancer-related risks when compared to cigarette smoking
- Depth of inhalation plays a crucial role in severity of health risks from cigars
- Moderate to deep inhalation leads to 5.9 times higher mortality risk for current cigar smokers vs. never-smokers
- COPD risk increases to 4.5 times higher for current cigar smokers with deep inhalation
- E-cigarettes contain and emit numerous potentially toxic substances
- Vaping can lead to irreversible lung damage and lung disease
- E-cigarettes are associated with increased risk of cough, wheezing, and asthma exacerbations in youth
- Primary ingredients in e-cigarettes (propylene glycol and vegetable glycerin) are toxic to cells
- E-cigarettes produce dangerous chemicals: acetaldehyde, acrolein, and formaldehyde
- These chemicals can cause both lung disease and cardiovascular disease
- Smoking affects nearly every organ in the body, not just the lungs
- Cardiovascular diseases kill more smokers annually than lung cancer
- Nicotine addiction involves both physical dependence and psychological habituation
- Denial in addiction is a complex psychological defense mechanism
- Secondhand smoke exposure affects non-smokers in proximity to smokers
- Well-ventilated areas and smoke-free policies are important in residential and commercial design
- Smoking cessation is possible and effects can be reversed if stopped early
- The intersection of health, psychology, and environmental design offers opportunities for creating healthier spaces

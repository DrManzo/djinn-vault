## College Living Environment

The transition to college often involves a significant change in living environment. Students typically move from their family homes to dormitories or shared apartments, which can have a profound impact on their behavior and development[study](https://study.com/academy/lesson/person-in-environment-pie-theory.html). This new environment presents both challenges and opportunities for growth. For instance, living with roommates requires adapting to new social dynamics, negotiating shared spaces, and developing conflict resolution skills[studocu](https://www.studocu.com/en-us/messages/question/2890004/how-does-the-person-environment-theories-apply-to-your-own-living-situation-for-the-average-college). The physical layout of campus housing, study areas, and social spaces can also influence students' academic performance and social interactions[suitable](https://www.suitable.co/knowledge-center/blog/student-development-theory-cheat-sheet).

## Adaptation and Growth

Person-environment theories suggest that students' behavior is guided by their social environments[suitable](https://www.suitable.co/knowledge-center/blog/student-development-theory-cheat-sheet). In the college setting, this means that students are constantly adapting to new academic expectations, social norms, and campus cultures. As they navigate these changes, students become more aware of how their habits and behaviors are influenced by their surroundings[suitable](https://www.suitable.co/knowledge-center/blog/student-development-theory-cheat-sheet). This process of adaptation and self-awareness contributes to personal growth and maturity, which aligns with John Dewey's concept of development from immaturity to maturity[suitable](https://www.suitable.co/knowledge-center/blog/student-development-theory-cheat-sheet).

## Changing Demographics in the U.S.

1. **Aging Population**: The U.S. population is getting older overall. The median age is approaching 38 years, nearly 10 years older than in 1970[everycrsreport](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/RL32701.html). By 2050, the median age is projected to reach 42 years[everycrsreport](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/RL32701.html).
2. **Increasing Diversity**: The U.S. is becoming more racially and ethnically diverse. By 2055, the country is projected to not have a single racial or ethnic majority[facinghistory](https://www.facinghistory.org/resource-library/shifting-demographics-united-states).
3. **Hispanic Population Growth**: Between 2022 and 2023, the Hispanic population accounted for nearly 71% of the overall U.S. population growth, driven primarily by Hispanic births[census](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024/population-estimates-characteristics.html).
4. **Changing Immigration Patterns**: Asia has replaced Latin America as the biggest source of new immigrants to the U.S.[facinghistory](https://www.facinghistory.org/resource-library/shifting-demographics-united-states).
5. **Declining Fertility Rates**: The total fertility rate is projected to remain below the replacement level of 2.1 children per woman, averaging about 1.7 per woman in the coming decades[budgetmodel.wharton.upenn](https://budgetmodel.wharton.upenn.edu/issues/2024/3/22/us-demographic-projections-with-and-without-immigration).

# Religion and Prejudice

## Summary
Gordon Allport observed that the role of religion is paradoxical: it makes prejudice and it unmakes prejudice. Research supports this assessment, showing that extrinsic religious orientation correlates with higher prejudice while intrinsic orientation fosters compassion and tolerance. Stoic philosophy offers parallel pathways to reducing bias through self-reflection and universal human worth.

## Key Points
- Allport's paradox: religion can both foster and alleviate prejudice depending on how it is internalized
- Extrinsic religious orientation uses religion for social gain and correlates with higher prejudice levels
- Intrinsic religious orientation treats faith as a deeply held guiding value and fosters compassion and tolerance
- In-group and out-group thinking encourages negative attitudes toward those seen as violating core group values
- Religious teachings emphasizing love, humility, and justice can inspire prosocial behaviors and challenge prejudice
- Self-reflection and making values-driven choices lead to more accepting attitudes toward others
- Stoicism teaches universal brotherhood, that all humans are part of a single community deserving justice
- Both religion and Stoicism emphasize that moral worth lies in character and virtue, not group membership
- Transcendence of particular identities undermines the psychological bases of prejudice
- Consistent self-reflection, journaling, and mindfulness help align actions with deeply held values
- Surrounding oneself with supportive communities reinforces intrinsic motivation for ethical behavior
- Class discussions highlight real-world examples where religious groups both perpetrated and fought discrimination
- The impact of any worldview on prejudice depends on how its core teachings are embraced and lived out

## Details
Scholarly research using Allport's Religious Orientation Scale shows that individuals who are sincere and self-reflective about their faith are less likely to be prejudiced, while those for whom religion is a tool for social advantage are more likely to display prejudice. The path to unmaking prejudice within religion parallels Stoicism: both require turning inward, reflecting on deepest values, and recognizing the shared dignity of all people. Strengthening intrinsic motivation in daily life involves regular honest personal reflection, engaging in acts of kindness without expectation of reward, and deliberately seeking to understand people from different backgrounds. Both religious teachings and Stoic philosophy emphasize learning through example and engaging in purposeful acts of kindness. Making these practices habitual helps ensure actions are guided by sincerely held values rather than social pressure or group norms.

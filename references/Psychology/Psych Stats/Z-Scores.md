# Z-Scores

## Summary
Z-scores standardize raw scores by expressing them in terms of standard deviation units from the mean. When a population distribution is standardized, it produces the standard normal distribution (z-distribution) with a mean of 0 and standard deviation of 1. Z-scores enable comparison across different scales, identification of outliers, and calculation of probabilities for specific values within a distribution.

## Key Points
- Standardizing a population distribution produces a **standard normal distribution** (z-distribution) with μ = 0 and σ = 1
- The z-score formula: z = (X - μ) / σ
- The **sign** (+/-) of a z-score indicates direction: positive = above the mean, negative = below the mean
- The **numerical value** (magnitude) indicates distance from the mean in standard deviation units
- A z-score of 0 means the raw score is exactly at the mean
- Scores within |z| ≤ 1 are considered **central** (near the middle of the distribution)
- Scores beyond |z| > 2 are considered **extreme** (far out in the tail)
- Z-scores allow comparison of scores from different distributions with different means and standard deviations
- Example: X = 70 on an exam with μ = 82, σ = 8 gives z = -1.50; X = 60 with μ = 72, σ = 12 gives z = -1.00 — the score of 60 is the better relative grade
- Example: X = 85 with μ = 70, σ = 10 gives z = 1.50; X = 58 with μ = 49, σ = 6 gives z = 1.50 — both scores produce the same relative grade
- Example: X = 26 with μ = 20, σ = 2 gives z = 3.00; X = 32 with μ = 24, σ = 4 gives z = 2.00 — the score of 26 is the better relative grade
- For μ = 75, σ = 15, X = 85: z ≈ 0.67 → Central
- For μ = 80, σ = 2, X = 85: z = 2.50 → Extreme
- For μ = 90, σ = 20, X = 85: z = -0.25 → Central
- For μ = 93, σ = 3, X = 85: z ≈ -2.67 → Extreme
- Z-scores are used with known population standard deviation; t-statistics are used when σ is unknown
- Standardization transforms raw scores into a common scale, enabling probabilistic inferences about data points

## Details

### Interpreting Z-Scores
| Z-Score Range | Interpretation |
|---------------|---------------|
| -1.0 to +1.0 | Central — typical score |
| -2.0 to -1.0 or +1.0 to +2.0 | Moderately unusual |
| Beyond ±2.0 | Extreme — unusual score |
| Beyond ±3.0 | Very extreme — rare |

### Standardization Process
1. Subtract the mean from each raw score (centers the distribution at 0)
2. Divide each result by the standard deviation (scales the spread to 1)
3. The shape of the distribution remains unchanged — only the scale changes

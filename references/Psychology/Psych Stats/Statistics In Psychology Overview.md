# Statistics In Psychology Overview

## Summary
Statistics provides the systematic tools necessary for psychologists to collect, analyze, and interpret data about human behavior and mental processes. Statistical literacy is a foundational competency in psychology education, with 98% of undergraduate psychology programs requiring at least one methodology course. Statistics transforms raw data into actionable knowledge that guides evidence-based decisions in therapy, education, and research.

## Key Points
- Statistics enables psychologists to test hypotheses and determine whether findings represent real patterns or occurred by chance
- Descriptive statistics organize, summarize, and describe data using measures of central tendency (mean, median, mode) and variability (standard deviation, range)
- Inferential statistics allow researchers to draw conclusions that extend beyond immediate data and generalize to larger populations
- Statistical training develops critical reasoning skills that distinguish psychology graduates from those without such training
- Statistics help determine whether therapeutic interventions are effective compared to control groups
- Researchers use statistics to check if tests are accurate and if treatments actually help people
- Descriptive statistics present data clearly through graphs, frequency distributions, and summary measures
- Inferential statistics assess statistical significance and the likelihood that observed relationships are genuine
- Power analysis practices have improved, with studies reporting power analysis increasing from 9.5% (2015-2016) to 30% (2020-2021)
- Reproducibility challenges in psychology often stem from researchers not fully checking whether data meets statistical assumptions
- Statistics support informed decision-making in therapy, education, and research based on evidence rather than guesswork
- Collaborative research between research psychologists and practitioners translates statistical findings into practical applications

## Details

### Descriptive vs. Inferential Statistics
Descriptive statistics summarize what happened in a study — measures like means, standard deviations, and frequency distributions make large datasets manageable. Inferential statistics determine whether those findings apply to people beyond the study participants, using techniques like t-tests and ANOVA to test hypotheses.

### Statistics vs. Parameters
A **statistic** describes a sample (e.g., 12% average from 75 students), while a **parameter** describes the entire population (e.g., the actual average for all 750 students). Descriptive statistics report sample data; inferential statistics use sample statistics to estimate population parameters.

### Levels of Measurement
- **Nominal**: Categories with no order (e.g., favorite TV show)
- **Ordinal**: Ordered categories (e.g., low/medium/high)
- **Interval**: Equal intervals but no true zero (e.g., temperature in Celsius)
- **Ratio**: Equal intervals with true zero (e.g., hours watched) — allows all mathematical operations

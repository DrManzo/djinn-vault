# Dorsal vs Ventral Visual Processing Streams

## Summary
The visual system processes information through two major pathways originating in the primary visual cortex: the ventral stream ("what" pathway) for object identification and recognition, and the dorsal stream ("where/how" pathway) for spatial processing and visually-guided action. Double dissociations between damage to these streams provide compelling evidence for their distinct functions.

## Key Points
- The ventral stream runs from the primary visual cortex to the temporal lobe and specializes in object identification, processing shape, color, texture, and form for recognition
- The dorsal stream projects from the occipital lobe to the posterior parietal cortex and processes spatial location, motion, and the relationship between objects for guiding motor behavior
- Ventral stream activity is strongly associated with conscious visual awareness, while dorsal stream activity is substantially less influenced by subjective awareness
- Dorsal stream damage causes optic ataxia: significant difficulty with spatial tasks and coordinated movements, but object recognition remains completely intact
- Ventral stream damage causes visual form agnosia: profound difficulty recognizing objects by sight despite intact vision, but visually-guided actions remain remarkably accurate
- The dorsal pathway can decode object categories earlier than the ventral pathway and may serve as a source of input for object recognition

## Details
A friend with dorsal stream damage would show large errors when reaching for objects not directly in front of them, struggle with hand-eye coordination tasks like orienting their hand when reaching through spaces, and fail to pre-shape their fingers appropriately for grasping. However, they could easily identify objects, describe colors, recognize faces, and read text.

A friend with ventral stream damage could not identify common objects by sight, would struggle with reading (pure alexia) and face recognition (prosopagnosia), yet could reach out and grasp objects with remarkable accuracy, navigate while avoiding obstacles, and recognize objects through touch, sound, or verbal description.

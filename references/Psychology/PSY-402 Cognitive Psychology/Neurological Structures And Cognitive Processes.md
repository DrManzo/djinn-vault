# Neurological Structures And Cognitive Processes

## Summary
The brain and nervous system form the biological foundation for all cognitive processes, from cellular-level neuron function to large-scale system organization. Understanding these structures—from individual neurons and glial cells to the major brain lobes and nervous system divisions—is essential for grasping how cognition, behavior, and perception emerge from biological processes.

## Key Points
- Neurons are the basic building blocks of the nervous system, consisting of the soma (cell body), dendrites (receiving signals), axon (transmitting signals), myelin sheath (insulation), nodes of Ranvier, and axon terminals (releasing neurotransmitters)
- Glial cells support and protect neurons: astrocytes maintain the extracellular environment, oligodendrocytes form myelin sheaths in the CNS, and microglia serve as the brain's immune cells
- Action potentials follow a seven-stage process from resting potential (-70 mV) through depolarization (Na+ influx), peak (+40 mV), repolarization (K+ efflux), hyperpolarization, and return to rest
- Synaptic transmission is a multi-step chemical communication process involving neurotransmitter release into the synaptic cleft, receptor binding, and termination through reuptake or breakdown
- The four major brain lobes each serve distinct functions: frontal (executive function, voluntary movement), parietal (sensory processing, spatial awareness), temporal (auditory processing, memory), and occipital (visual processing)
- The nervous system divides into the Central Nervous System (brain and spinal cord) and Peripheral Nervous System (somatic and autonomic divisions, including sympathetic, parasympathetic, and enteric systems)

## Details
The action potential operates on an "all-or-nothing" principle and takes approximately 1 millisecond. The sodium-potassium pump maintains ion gradients at rest, with Na+ high outside and K+ high inside. During depolarization, Na+ rushes in to start the signal; during repolarization, K+ flows out to reset the neuron.

The autonomic nervous system's three divisions work in concert: the sympathetic system drives "fight-or-flight" responses, the parasympathetic system manages "rest-and-digest" functions, and the enteric system provides independent digestive control.

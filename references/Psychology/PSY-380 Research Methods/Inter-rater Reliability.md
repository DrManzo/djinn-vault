# Inter-rater Reliability

## Summary
This note covers inter-rater reliability and its measurement, the relationship between reliability and validity, and the similarities between content and face validity. It addresses key concepts in psychological measurement and operational definitions.

## Key Points
- Inter-rater reliability is commonly measured using the Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC), which assesses agreement among raters, particularly for continuous data
- ICC is applicable for two or more raters, not just four or more
- Even well-trained research assistants may need inter-rater reliability calculations to ensure consistency and eliminate bias
- Establishing inter-rater reliability does not guarantee the validity of observations — reliability and validity are distinct concepts
- Content validity and face validity both involve subjective judgments about the appropriateness of test content
- Face validity is more informal and subjective, while content validity involves systematic expert evaluations
- Operational definition specifies how a concept is measured in a study and is central to concerns about whether a scale truly measures its intended construct
- When interrogating statistical validity, a necessary question is whether the variables are measured in a reliable way
- 95% confidence intervals deal with precision estimates about the population
- A moderator suggests that associations may not generalize to all subgroups of people
- When evaluating construct validity of an association claim, how each variable was measured must be considered
- Replicating a study gives researchers additional estimates of the association
- When evaluating generalizability of an association claim, the way the sample was selected from the population is the most important consideration
- For a third variable to be plausible as an explanation in an established association, it must be related to both measured variables in the original association
- Restriction of range occurs when there is very little variance on one of the variables

## Details
Inter-rater reliability is foundational for any study involving observational or coded data. Without demonstrating that multiple raters agree in their assessments, the credibility of the measurements is questionable. However, reliability alone is insufficient — a measure can be consistently wrong (reliable but invalid).

The distinction between reliability and validity is critical: reliability refers to consistency of measurement, while validity refers to whether the measure actually captures what it intends to measure. Both must be established for research findings to be trustworthy.

# PETA Website Review

## Summary
This note examines the ethical guidelines for conducting research on animals, contrasting the positions of People for the Ethical Treatment of Animals (PETA) and Americans for Medical Progress (AMP). It explores the Three Rs framework (Replacement, Reduction, Refinement) as the foundation of ethical animal research and evaluates current alternatives to animal testing.

## Key Points
- Animal research in the United States is governed by the Animal Welfare Act (AWA) and Public Health Service (PHS) Policy, with institutional oversight by IACUCs
- IACUCs must include a veterinarian, a scientist, a non-scientist, and a community representative
- Protocol approval requires justification for animal use, procedures to minimize pain, evidence of searching for alternatives, and confirmation the experiment does not unnecessarily duplicate previous research
- PETA opposes all animal experimentation, advocating that "animals are not ours to experiment on" and focusing on laboratories, food industry, clothing trade, and entertainment
- Americans for Medical Progress (AMP) supports animal testing in biomedical research while advocating for moderate welfare measures
- The Three Rs framework was first described by William Russell and Rex Burch in 1959
- Replacement involves using methods that avoid or replace animal use entirely
- Reduction minimizes the number of animals while maintaining scientific validity
- Refinement implements methods that alleviate or minimize potential pain, suffering, or distress
- The Three Rs provide a common language uniting scientists, veterinary professionals, humane organizations, and policy makers
- Cell and tissue culture systems, organs-on-chips, and computer modeling/AI represent the most promising alternatives to animal testing
- Some alternatives have already replaced animal tests successfully, including shellfish toxin testing and botulinum neurotoxin testing
- Non-animal tests using human stem cells have 93% sensitivity at detecting developmental problems compared to 60% for animal tests
- Complete replacement across all research domains remains challenging due to regulatory requirements, complexity of human systems, and validation gaps

## Details
The Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals serves as a comprehensive resource for standards in animal housing, care, and humane treatment. Additional guidelines include the AVMA Guidelines for Euthanasia, the Health Research Extension Act, and the U.S. Government Principles for the Utilization and Care of Vertebrate Animals.

Economic considerations favor alternatives: animal tests range from 1.5x to over 30x as expensive as in vitro alternatives. Rodent testing for cancer therapeutics adds 4-5 years to drug development and costs $2-4 million. The organ-on-chip market is projected to grow from $159.48M in 2024 to $3,242.34M by 2034.

Despite progress, regulatory acceptance remains a significant hurdle. Validation of alternative methods requires demonstrating reliability and relevance through multi-laboratory trials following internationally accepted guidelines like OECD GD 34.

# Research Ethics

## Summary
This note examines ethical dilemmas in research settings, using a scenario about a student intern (Frank) who faces a confidentiality conflict when asked to file charts that include his roommate's records. It explores the ethical responsibilities of researchers, the role of personal feelings in ethical decision-making, and how moral, religious, and safety considerations complicate ethical judgments.

## Key Points
- A student intern's primary ethical responsibility is to maintain confidentiality as outlined in signed agreements
- Client information should only be accessed when necessary and under supervision
- Unauthorized disclosure of sensitive details violates professional integrity and workplace policies
- If a confidentiality breach occurs, the intern would be at fault for accessing or sharing information without authorization
- The agency or supervisor could share liability if they failed to provide adequate safeguards or supervision
- Emotions like empathy and fear naturally influence ethical judgment but must be weighed against professional obligations
- Objective decision-making requires prioritizing confidentiality agreements over personal curiosity or anxiety
- When multiple interns work together, accountability may increase through mutual oversight, but group dynamics could introduce risks like peer pressure
- Personal morals, religious upbringing, and safety concerns can create complicated situations without easy answers
- Galatians 6:5 states, "For each will have to bear his own load," reflecting individual responsibility in ethical decision-making
- Treating others as you would want to be treated serves as a universal guide for ethical behavior that transcends religious traditions
- Supervisors play a crucial role in ensuring interns are properly guided and supported, and must be mindful when assigning tasks that could create conflicts of interest
- Even when the right course of action is known, acting on it requires courage and self-discipline

## Details
Ethical dilemmas in research often involve competing values — the desire to help versus the duty to protect privacy, personal relationships versus professional obligations. These conflicts do not have simple resolutions and require careful consideration of multiple ethical frameworks.

The scenario of the intern Frank illustrates how ethical responsibilities in research settings extend beyond following rules. They require judgment, self-awareness, and the willingness to seek guidance when facing ambiguous situations. Notifying a supervisor about a confidentiality dilemma, rather than making an independent decision, represents the ethically appropriate course of action.

# Practice Tests and Study Guides

## Summary
This note compiles practice test questions and answers covering key concepts from PSY-380, including sampling methods, psychological measurement, statistical validity, replication, the file drawer problem, pre-registration, and cultural psychology. It serves as a study guide for exam preparation.

## Key Points
- A sample is always smaller than a population
- Levels of anxiety reported by patients is an example of psychological measurement
- Watching how students treat other children with ADHD is an example of observational measurement
- Quota sampling and snowball sampling both result in samples where some people are systematically left out (non-probability methods)
- Another word for observer effect is expectancy effects
- Inter-rater reliability is measured with an ICC
- Content validity and face validity both involve subjective judgments
- Operational definition specifies how a concept is measured in a study
- When interrogating statistical validity, ask whether variables are measured in a reliable way
- 95% confidence intervals deal with precision estimates about the population
- A moderator suggests that associations may not generalize to all subgroups of people
- Replicating a study gives researchers additional estimates of the association
- When evaluating generalizability, the way the sample was selected is most important
- Restriction of range occurs when there is very little variance on one variable
- A selection effect creates group differences prior to the start of the study
- A null confound keeps a researcher from finding a relationship between two variables
- A manipulated variable is one the researcher controls (independent variable)
- Demand characteristics are decreased by using objective measures instead of self-report
- Matched group design addresses possible selection effects
- Regression is problematic when one group has extremely high scores at pre-test
- Attrition can threaten validity even when a control group is used
- Within-group designs rely on fewer participants
- Pre-registration occurs before data collection
- The file drawer problem is addressed by contacting researchers for unpublished results
- A conceptual replication uses a different method to assess the same phenomenon
- Studies in theory-testing mode de-emphasize ecological validity because internal validity is the highest priority
- A study examining motor responses of newborns is least likely to be influenced by cultural differences

## Details
These practice questions cover the breadth of PSY-380 content, from foundational concepts (sampling, measurement) to advanced topics (moderators, replication, pre-registration). Understanding these concepts is essential for both exam performance and for critically evaluating psychological research.

Key areas of focus include: distinguishing between different types of validity, understanding sampling methods and their limitations, recognizing threats to internal validity, and knowing how replication strengthens scientific knowledge.

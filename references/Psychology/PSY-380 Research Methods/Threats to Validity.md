# Threats to Validity

## Summary
This note covers common threats to internal validity in experimental research, including selection effects, design confounds, demand characteristics, attrition, and regression to the mean. It also addresses research design strategies used to mitigate these threats.

## Key Points
- Selection effect (selection bias) occurs when participants in different groups differ systematically before any treatment is applied, creating pre-existing group differences that confound results
- Design confound refers to an additional variable that varies along with the independent variable, threatening the ability to isolate causal effects
- Order effect relates to the sequence in which treatments are administered, typically in within-subjects designs
- Maturation effect refers to changes within participants over time that are unrelated to the experimental manipulation
- A null confound is a confounding variable that keeps a researcher from finding a relationship between two variables by obscuring or masking true associations
- Demand characteristics occur when participants guess the purpose of the study and change their behavior accordingly
- Using objective measures (e.g., EEG instead of self-report) helps decrease demand characteristics
- Matched group design addresses possible selection effects by matching participants in different groups based on key characteristics
- Regression to the mean is especially problematic when one group has extremely high or low scores at pre-test
- Attrition can bias results even when a control group is used, particularly if participants drop out at different rates across groups
- Within-group designs rely on fewer participants because each participant experiences all conditions
- A concurrent measures design exposes each participant to all conditions at the same time, with preference measured immediately
- Within-subjects designs reduce within-group variance, making between-group differences appear larger and potentially increasing false positive risk
- Increasing sample size is the easiest way to increase precision and power in a study

## Details
Understanding threats to internal validity is essential for designing rigorous experiments. Researchers must anticipate and address these threats during the design phase rather than attempting to correct them after data collection.

Key strategies include: using random assignment to minimize selection effects, employing matched group designs when randomization is not feasible, counterbalancing to address order effects, using objective measures to reduce demand characteristics, and monitoring attrition patterns to assess potential bias.

When evaluating research, interrogating how the study addressed these threats is a critical component of assessing the credibility of its claims.

# Research Ethics

## Summary
This note addresses ethical considerations when conducting research with human participants, specifically focusing on the Emilio scenario where an undergraduate research assistant distributes surveys to freshman-level psychology students. Key factors include informed consent, confidentiality, voluntary participation, diversity and inclusion, methodological considerations, and institutional approval.

## Key Points
- Informed consent requires fully informing participants about the research purpose, what participation involves, potential risks, and the right to withdraw without penalty
- Confidentiality must be maintained; identifiable information should be protected or anonymized
- Voluntary participation is essential; students must not feel coerced, especially given power dynamics in academic settings
- Surveys should be culturally sensitive, avoid biased language, and be accessible to all students including those with disabilities
- Convenience sampling (e.g., using an introductory psychology class) limits generalizability and should be acknowledged
- IRB approval is required before distributing surveys to ensure the research meets ethical standards
- Freshman students are typically in late adolescence or emerging adulthood; their developmental stage may influence how they interpret and respond to survey questions

## Details

### Ethical Considerations
- **Informed Consent:** All participants must be fully informed about the purpose of the research, what participation involves, any potential risks, and their right to withdraw at any time without penalty. Since participants are college freshmen (typically 18+), they can provide their own consent, but clarity and transparency are essential
- **Confidentiality:** All responses must be kept confidential and data stored securely. Identifiable information should be protected or anonymized
- **Voluntary Participation:** Participation should be entirely voluntary. Students must not feel coerced or pressured, especially since the research is conducted within an academic setting where power dynamics could influence willingness to participate

### Diversity and Inclusion
- **Respect for Diversity:** The sample will likely include students from diverse backgrounds, identities, and experiences. The survey should be culturally sensitive, avoid biased language, and be accessible to all students
- **Avoiding Bias:** Both the design of the questionnaire and the interpretation of results should be free from assumptions or stereotypes about students' backgrounds, beliefs, or abilities

### Methodological Considerations
- **Sampling:** Using all students in an introductory psychology class is a convenience sample. This limitation should be acknowledged as it may affect generalizability
- **Survey Design:** Questions should be clear, unbiased, and appropriate for the developmental and educational level of freshmen. Avoid jargon or complex language
- **Validity and Reliability:** The survey should be pilot-tested if possible to ensure it accurately measures what it intends to and yields consistent results

### Institutional Approval
- **IRB Approval:** Before distributing surveys, Emilio and his supervisor must seek approval from the university's Institutional Review Board (IRB) or ethics committee to ensure the research meets ethical standards and protects student participants

### Contextual and Developmental Awareness
- **Developmental Stage:** Freshman students are typically in late adolescence or emerging adulthood. Their cognitive, social, and emotional development may influence how they interpret and respond to survey questions
- **Power Dynamics:** As participants are students in a course, care must be taken to minimize any perceived obligation to participate, especially if the research is associated with course instructors or grading

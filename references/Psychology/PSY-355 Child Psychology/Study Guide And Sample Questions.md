# Study Guide And Sample Questions

## Summary
This note consolidates key definitions, developmental stages, and processes covered in the PSY-355 Topic 4 midterm study guide. It includes essential terminology from Piaget's theory, biological processes, developmental patterns, and brain structures relevant to prenatal and infant development.

## Key Points
- Schemes are organized patterns of thought or behavior that help children make sense of their experiences (Piaget)
- Mitosis is the process by which a cell divides to produce two identical daughter cells, crucial for growth and development
- Phenotype refers to observable characteristics resulting from the interaction of genotype and environment
- Development follows cephalocaudal (head-to-toe) and proximodistal (center-to-extremities) patterns
- The cerebral cortex is responsible for complex thinking, perception, and voluntary movement
- The myelin sheath insulates axons and speeds up neural transmission
- Nature (biological inheritance) and nurture (environmental influences) both shape development; twin and adoption studies help distinguish their effects

## Details

### Key Definitions
- **Schemes:** Organized patterns of thought or behavior that help children make sense of their experiences
- **Mitosis:** Process by which a cell divides to produce two identical daughter cells
- **Phenotype:** Observable characteristics of an individual, resulting from interaction of genotype and environment
- **Oxytocics:** Medications that stimulate uterine contractions, used to induce or speed up labor
- **Habituation:** Decreased response to a repeated stimulus
- **Centration:** Focusing attention on one characteristic to the exclusion of others (Piaget)
- **Deferred Imitation:** Ability to imitate actions observed at an earlier time
- **Concept Formation:** Process of grouping objects, events, or people based on shared properties
- **Perceptual Organization:** Process by which the brain organizes sensory input into meaningful patterns

### Developmental Patterns
- **Cephalocaudal Pattern:** Growth occurs first at the head and gradually proceeds downward to the rest of the body
- **Proximodistal Pattern:** Growth starts at the center of the body and moves outward toward the extremities
- **Lateralization Pattern:** Specialization of functions in the two hemispheres of the brain

### Brain Structures
- **Cerebral Cortex:** Outer layer of the brain responsible for complex thinking, perception, and voluntary movement
- **Myelin Sheath:** Fatty layer that covers and insulates axons, speeding up neural transmission
- **Cerebellum:** Brain structure involved in coordination and balance
- **Prefrontal Cortex:** Front part of the frontal lobes, responsible for reasoning, planning, and self-control

### Piaget's Sensorimotor Concepts
- **Primary Circular Reactions:** Repetitive actions centered on the infant's own body (e.g., sucking thumb)
- **Secondary Circular Reactions:** Repetitive actions focused on the environment (e.g., shaking a rattle)

### Stages and Periods of Development
- **Prenatal:** Conception to birth; includes germinal, embryonic, and fetal periods
- **Infancy:** Birth to about 18-24 months
- **Childbirth and Afterbirth:** Labor and delivery, followed by expulsion of placenta and membranes

### Processes in Prenatal and Infant Development
- **Biological:** Physical growth, brain development, genetics
- **Psychological:** Cognitive development, emotional regulation, learning
- **Socioemotional:** Attachment, temperament, social interactions

### Nature vs. Nurture
- **Nature:** Biological inheritance (genes, DNA)
- **Nurture:** Environmental influences (family, culture, experiences)
- **Study Methods:** Twin and adoption studies help distinguish genetic vs. environmental influences

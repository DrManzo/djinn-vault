# Research Methods In Psychology

## Summary
This note examines two research methods for collecting data in developmental psychology: naturalistic observation and surveys. Each method has distinct advantages, disadvantages, and ethical considerations, particularly in pharmaceutical research involving human subjects. Understanding these methods is essential for designing rigorous and ethical research studies.

## Key Points
- Naturalistic observation involves studying participants in real-world settings without intervention, providing high ecological validity
- Surveys collect self-reported data through structured questionnaires, enabling scalable analysis across diverse populations
- Naturalistic observation risks observer bias, limited control over variables, and reactivity
- Surveys risk response bias, limited depth, and non-response bias
- Both methods require informed consent, confidentiality protections, and attention to age considerations
- Combining methods with physiological measures can mitigate individual limitations and strengthen findings
- Ethical compliance is critical, including protocols for parental permission and child assent when minors are involved

## Details

### Naturalistic Observation
**Description:** Observing participants in their natural environment without intervention to study behavior or outcomes in real-world contexts.

**Advantages:**
- High ecological validity: captures authentic behaviors in familiar settings
- Ethical for sensitive topics: avoids artificial lab conditions, reducing stress
- Useful for longitudinal data: can track behavioral changes over time (e.g., medication side effects)

**Disadvantages:**
- Observer bias: interpretations may be subjective, affecting data reliability
- Limited control over variables: difficult to isolate medication effects from external factors
- Reactivity: participants may alter behavior if aware of observation

**Risks and Challenges:**
- Informed consent: participants (or guardians for minors) must understand the observational nature
- Privacy concerns: ensuring confidentiality in real-world settings can be complex
- Sample representativeness: results may not generalize if the observed group is small or non-diverse

### Surveys
**Description:** Collecting self-reported data through structured questionnaires about symptoms, side effects, or medication efficacy.

**Advantages:**
- Scalability: reaches large, geographically diverse samples efficiently
- Quantitative analysis: standardized questions enable statistical comparisons
- Cost-effective: online platforms reduce logistical expenses

**Disadvantages:**
- Response bias: social desirability or misunderstanding of questions may skew results
- Limited depth: closed-ended questions restrict nuanced feedback about subjective experiences
- Non-response bias: low participation rates may distort findings

**Risks and Challenges:**
- Informed consent: clear communication about study purpose and voluntary participation is critical
- Age considerations: surveys for minors require age-appropriate language and guardian consent
- Data validity: ensuring questions are unambiguous and culturally sensitive

### Key Considerations for Pharmaceutical Research
- Ethical compliance: adhere to protocols for parental permission and child assent if minors are involved
- Sample diversity: ensure representation across age, gender, and socioeconomic backgrounds
- Method triangulation: combine surveys with physiological measures (e.g., blood tests) to validate self-reported data
- Naturalistic observation suits early-stage studies prioritizing real-world behavior; surveys excel in large-scale efficacy trials

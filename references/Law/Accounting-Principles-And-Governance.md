# Accounting Principles and Governance

## Summary
This note covers key principles in accounting, including legislation and governing bodies, with a focus on the Sarbanes-Oxley Act, FASB, GAAP, and the role of auditors.

## Key Points
- **Sarbanes-Oxley Act**: Enacted to address unethical behavior by companies like Enron and WorldCom.
- **FASB and GAAP**: FASB develops US governing principles for Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP).
- **Auditor's Role**: An auditor is responsible for completing an objective analysis of accounting processes and financial information.

## Details
### Legislation
The Sarbanes-Oxley Act, enacted in 2002, was created to enhance corporate governance and accountability in response to major accounting scandals such as those at Enron and WorldCom. This act aimed to prevent similar unethical behaviors by imposing stricter regulations on public companies.

### Governing Bodies and Principles
- **FASB (Financial Accounting Standards Board)**: Responsible for developing the US governing principles known as **GAAP** (Generally Accepted Accounting Principles).
  - The correct answer is **B.) FASB; GAAP**.

### Auditor's Role
An auditor’s role involves conducting a thorough and impartial review of the financial statements and internal controls to ensure accuracy, compliance, and transparency. This includes identifying potential issues and providing recommendations for improvement, ensuring adherence to ethical standards set forth in legislation such as the Sarbanes-Oxley Act.

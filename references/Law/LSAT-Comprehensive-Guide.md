# LSAT Comprehensive Guide

## Summary
A comprehensive guide to the Law School Admission Test (LSAT) covering test format and structure (post-August 2024 changes), scoring system, testing logistics, preparation strategies, study resources, accommodations, fee waivers, and the GRE alternative for law school admission.

## Key Points
1. **Post-August 2024 format**: Analytical Reasoning (Logic Games) eliminated; current test has two Logical Reasoning sections (~50 questions total), one Reading Comprehension section (26-28 questions), and one unscored experimental section
2. **Test duration**: Four 35-minute multiple-choice sections totaling approximately 2 hours 30 minutes, plus one 10-minute break; separate LSAT Argumentative Writing (35 minutes, unscored but sent to schools)
3. **Logical Reasoning dominance**: Now represents approximately 50% of total score; question types include Identify the Flaw, Strengthen/Weaken, Assumptions, Principle questions, Inference, and Explain the Discrepancy
4. **Scoring range**: 120-180; median score is 152 (50th percentile); 160 is approximately 80th percentile; 170 is approximately 97th percentile
5. **Top-tier school medians**: Yale 175, Harvard 174, Stanford/Chicago/Columbia 173; scores of 166+ (top 10%) typically necessary for T14 competition
6. **2026 test schedule**: January, February, April, June administrations; scores released approximately 3 weeks after testing
7. **Costs**: Registration $248 (includes Writing); Credential Assembly Service $215 (valid 5 years); CAS report per school $45; total for 12 schools approximately $985
8. **Study timeline**: 200-300 total hours over 3-6 months recommended; three-month timeline (20 hours/week) is the sweet spot for most students
9. **Retake policy**: 5 times within current reportable period (past 5 years), 7 times lifetime; second-time test-takers average 2.6-point improvements
10. **Fee waiver program**: Tier 1 provides free LSAT registration, CAS registration, Prep Plus subscription, and four CAS reports; Tier 2 provides free registration, CAS, and two CAS reports
11. **Accommodations**: Available for ADHD, learning disabilities, autism, anxiety, depression, PTSD, physical disabilities; requires documentation dated within 5 years for cognitive/mental disabilities
12. **GRE alternative**: Over 100 of 199 ABA-accredited law schools accept GRE; LSAT remains gold standard as U.S. News rankings use LSAT medians; some schools prohibit GRE if LSAT score exists
13. **Preparation resources**: PowerScore LSAT Bibles, The LSAT Trainer by Mike Kim, 7Sage, Kaplan, Blueprint Prep, Khan Academy (free); official LSAC PrepTests are essential
14. **Critical study principles**: Blind review method, understand "why" over "what," accuracy before speed, prediction strategy before reading answer choices, simulate test conditions, prioritize well-being

## Details

### Test Structure Changes
The August 2024 elimination of Logic Games represents the most significant change in LSAT history. Logical Reasoning now comprises the majority of the scored test, making mastery of argument evaluation the single most important preparation focus. The experimental section is indistinguishable from scored sections, requiring full effort on every section.

### Preparation Methodology
Effective preparation combines four integrated components: theory (approximately 100 hours learning fundamental principles), accuracy drills (untimed practice building correct analytical processes), timed drills (developing speed while maintaining precision), and full PrepTests (20-40+ complete tests simulating exam conditions). The Blind Review method—re-solving all questions untimed before checking answers—is the most recommended review technique.

### Score Context and Strategy
Law schools consider the 25th-75th percentile range when evaluating applicants. Scoring at or above the median makes an applicant competitive; scoring above the 75th percentile often triggers substantial merit scholarship consideration. Multiple attempts showing improvement are viewed neutrally or positively; stagnant or declining scores may raise concerns. Most schools now emphasize the highest score rather than averaging.

### Book Recommendations Priority
Priority 1 (essential): The LSAT Trainer by Mike Kim + Official LSAT TriplePrep Volume 2. Priority 2 (deep logic): PowerScore LSAT Logical Reasoning Bible + Workbook. Priority 3 (RC upgrade): PowerScore Reading Comprehension Bible. Priority 4 (more practice): Additional TriplePrep volumes. Budget approach: start with Trainer + TriplePrep (~$60-70), add PowerScore materials later.

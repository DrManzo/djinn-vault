## Section 1 — Djinn Publishings as a Business

### 1.1 Entity Choice: LLC vs. Sole Proprietorship

For Javier's situation, an **LLC is the correct structure** — not a sole proprietorship. The LLC provides liability separation between Javier personally and the publishing entity, which matters the moment the business signs contracts (with distributors, editors, designers), licenses IP, or generates revenue.

A sole proprietorship provides zero liability protection. An LLC shields members from business debts and legal claims so long as the owner maintains a separate bank account and does not commingle personal and business funds. (See also TASK-037 Section 7 — Corporate Law, Entity Types Compared.)

**Recommended structure:** Single-member LLC, California domicile, taxed as a sole proprietor (Schedule C) by default — no added complexity, no double taxation, no S-corp election required at this stage.

### 1.3 Publisher Imprint Setup

A **publisher imprint** is not a separate legal entity — it is a trade name (DBA) operating under the LLC. "Djinn Publishings" can serve as both the LLC name and the imprint on book covers. If Javier later wants a sub-label (e.g., "Pyraxis Press" for the fantasy series), file a **Fictitious Business Name (FBN) statement** with the San Bernardino County Clerk.

- **FBN cost:** ~$26 for the first name + $5 per additional name
- **What it does:** Allows Djinn Publishings LLC to legally operate under and receive payments under the imprint name
- **Why it matters:** The imprint name entered during ISBN registration is what appears in global book metadata (Bowker Books In Print, library catalogs, distributor databases). Use the imprint name consistently across all registrations.

### 1.4 ISBN Acquisition

In the United States, **Bowker** (myidentifiers.com) is the sole official ISBN issuing agency. Free ISBNs offered by KDP or IngramSpark lock the publisher field to Amazon or Ingram — not to Djinn Publishings. Always purchase through Bowker.

**Bowker pricing (2026):**

| Package | Total Cost | Per-ISBN Cost |
|---------|-----------|---------------|
| 1 ISBN | $125 | $125 |
| 10 ISBNs | $295 | $29.50 |
| 100 ISBNs | $575 | $5.75 |

**Recommendation: Buy the 10-pack at $295.**

Each format requires its own unique ISBN:
- Hardcover (if produced)
- Trade paperback (primary print format)
- Ebook (EPUB) — optional but recommended for metadata consistency
- Audiobook — if pursued in future

That is 3–4 ISBNs for Book 1 alone. A 10-pack covers Books 1–2 across all formats with room remaining for Book 3 paperback and ebook. ISBNs never expire — unused ones carry forward indefinitely.

### 1.5 Copyright Registration

In the U.S., **copyright attaches automatically at creation** — the moment the manuscript is written, Javier owns it. However, **federal registration with the U.S. Copyright Office** is strongly recommended before publication for the following reasons:

- Registration is a prerequisite to sue for **statutory damages** (up to $150,000 per infringement) and **attorney's fees** in federal court
- Works registered before or within 3 months of first publication are eligible for enhanced remedies
- Creates a public, timestamped record of authorship

**Process:**
1. Go to copyright.gov → Log in (or create account)
2. Register a new claim → Literary Work
3. Upload the **final, complete manuscript** as PDF
4. Pay **$45 online** (eCO single-work registration)
5. Processing: ~3 months standard; $800 expedited if needed pre-launch

**Register each book separately.** Register the final manuscript, not an early draft. The registration certificate will name Djinn Publishings LLC as the claimant/publisher of record if registered by the entity rather than Javier personally — clarify this when setting up the account.

## Section 2 — Self-Publishing Pipeline

### 2.1 Interior Formatting: Trim Size, Margins, Fonts

**Trim size for adult epic fantasy:** **6" × 9"** is the industry standard for adult secondary-world fantasy. YA typically uses 5.5" × 8.5". *The Dominion's Rise* reads as adult — use 6" × 9".

**KDP margin requirements (6" × 9" print, no bleed):**

| Margin | Minimum |
|--------|---------|
| Inside (gutter) — 300–600 pages | 0.75" |
| Outside | 0.5" |
| Top | 0.5" |
| Bottom | 0.5" |

For a novel in the 100k–130k word range (estimated 400–500 pages at 6×9), **push gutter to 0.875"–1.0"** to prevent text from disappearing into the spine binding.

IngramSpark follows the same bleed and margin standards. Both require **300 DPI** for any images (maps, chapter headers, ornamental dividers) and **PDF/X-1a** format for final print files.

**Typography:**

| Element | Recommendation |
|---------|----------------|
| Body text font | Garamond, Palatino Linotype, or Minion Pro |
| Body text size | 11–12pt |
| Leading (line spacing) | 1.2–1.4× font size (auto in most formatters) |
| Chapter headings | Display font matching cover branding |
| Avoid | Times New Roman, Arial, any default system font |

**Interior formatting tools:**

| Tool | Cost | Platform | Notes |
|------|------|----------|-------|
| Atticus | $147 one-time | Windows/Mac/Browser | Best cross-platform option for Linux workflow via browser |
| Vellum | $249.99 | Mac only | Industry gold standard — not viable for Fedora/Ubuntu |
| Reedsy Book Editor | Free | Browser | Clean EPUB + print PDF export, limited design control |
| Adobe InDesign | ~$23/month | All | Professional standard, steep learning curve |

**Recommendation for Javier:** Atticus. Browser-based, outputs print-ready PDF and all ebook formats simultaneously, no macOS required.

### 2.2 Cover Specifications

**Print covers (KDP and IngramSpark):**
- Full wraparound (front + spine + back) — spine width is calculated by each platform based on exact page count and paper type
- Bleed: **0.125" on all sides**
- Resolution: **300 DPI minimum**
- Color mode: **CMYK** (IngramSpark strict; KDP accepts RGB but CMYK is safer for print accuracy)
- File format: **PDF/X-1a**
- Use each platform's cover calculator to generate exact dimensions once interior page count is finalized

**Ebook cover:**
- Dimensions: **2,560 × 1,600 px** (KDP standard) — 1.6:1 ratio
- Resolution: 300 DPI
- File format: JPEG or PNG
- Front cover only (no spine or back)
- RGB color mode

**Cover design notes for queer epic fantasy:**
- Comp title covers (*A Memory Called Empire*, *The Traitor Baru Cormorant*, *Priory of the Orange Tree*) all use painterly/illustrative styles, dark or jewel-tone palettes, and central character or symbol focus
- Avoid stock photo montage — genre readers immediately distinguish illustrated/painted covers from photomanipulation
- Budget expectation: $200–$800 for a freelance fantasy cover artist (ArtStation, Reedsy marketplace, Ko-fi commissions)

### 2.3 Ebook Formats and Production

| Format | Use Case | Notes |
|--------|----------|-------|
| EPUB | Universal standard — Kobo, Apple Books, Draft2Digital, IngramSpark, Barnes & Noble | Required for all non-Amazon ebook distribution |
| MOBI | Legacy Amazon Kindle format | Deprecated; KDP now accepts EPUB directly — no longer need to produce MOBI separately |
| KPF | Kindle Package Format | KDP auto-converts from EPUB — do not manually produce |

**Production workflow:** Format in Atticus → export EPUB → upload directly to KDP (auto-converts) and all other platforms. One source file, all formats.

**Ebook-specific formatting rules:**
- All fonts must be embedded
- No fixed-layout elements (tables, sidebars) that break reflowable text
- Maps and world-art should be placed at chapter breaks, not inline
- Front matter: title page, copyright page, dedication, table of contents (required by retailers)
- Back matter: author bio, series note, newsletter CTA, acknowledgments

### 2.4 Distribution Channels — KDP vs. IngramSpark vs. Draft2Digital

| Platform | Print | Ebook | Key Strength | Key Weakness |
|----------|-------|-------|-------------|-------------|
| KDP (Amazon) | Yes | Yes | Largest ebook market; free to use; Kindle Unlimited access | Amazon-centric; bookstore distribution is weak; KU requires exclusivity |
| IngramSpark | Yes | Yes | Reaches 40,000+ bookstores, libraries, and wholesale distributors globally | Setup fee ($49/title); less intuitive dashboard; lower Amazon print sales than KDP |
| Draft2Digital | No | Yes | Aggregates to Kobo, Apple Books, B&N, libraries, Google Play in one upload | No print; takes 10% of net royalties |

**Recommended strategy (wide distribution, no exclusivity):**
1. **KDP** — print paperback (Amazon only) + ebook (Amazon only)
2. **IngramSpark** — print paperback for bookstore/library/wholesale distribution; hardcover if pursued
3. **Draft2Digital** — all non-Amazon ebook retailers (Kobo, Apple Books, Barnes & Noble, OverDrive libraries, Google Play)

This "hybrid wide" approach maximizes reach without surrendering Kindle Unlimited exclusivity permanently. Do **not** enroll ebook in KDP Select/Kindle Unlimited if using Draft2Digital — they are mutually exclusive. Kindle Unlimited can be evaluated later for a limited promotional window.

**Do not use IngramSpark for Amazon print distribution** — KDP print royalties on Amazon are significantly higher than IngramSpark's Amazon wholesale channel. Use each platform where it excels.

## Section 3 — Manuscript Readiness

### 3.1 Editorial Sequence and Costs

The editorial sequence is non-negotiable and must be done in order. Reversing steps wastes money.

**Phase 1 — Developmental Edit (DE)**
- Addresses: story structure, pacing, plot holes, character arc consistency, worldbuilding coherence, POV issues, scene-level tension
- This is the most expensive edit and the most important
- Cost range: **$0.03–$0.09/word** (industry standard per-word rate)
- For a 120,000-word fantasy novel: **$3,600–$10,800** (wide range based on editor experience)
- Mid-market specialist SFF editors: **$0.0065/word = ~$780 for 120k words** (entry-level but genre-experienced)
- Reedsy estimate for DE: **~$2,880 for an 80k-word novel** (scales with length)
- **Do not skip or substitute with beta readers** — beta readers are not developmental editors

**Phase 2 — Copy Edit (CE)**
- Addresses: sentence-level clarity, consistency of character names/world terms, grammar, style guide adherence, internal logic errors
- Typically done after DE revisions are complete and manuscript is structurally locked
- Cost range: **$0.01–$0.04/word**
- For 120k words: **$1,200–$4,800**
- Reedsy estimate: **~$2,160 for 80k words**

**Phase 3 — Proofreading**
- Final pass: catches typos, formatting errors, punctuation slips, widow/orphan lines introduced during layout
- Done on the **formatted, laid-out PDF** — not the Word document
- Cost range: **$0.006–$0.015/word**
- For 120k words: **$720–$1,800**
- Reedsy estimate: **~$1,600 for 80k words**

**Total realistic editorial budget for a 120k-word fantasy novel: $5,500–$17,400**

Mid-range realistic budget for a debut indie press with a single strong DE + CE + proof: **~$6,000–$8,500**

**Finding editors:**
- Reedsy (reedsy.com) — curated marketplace, vetted editors, free to browse and request quotes
- ACES (ace.copyeditors.org) — professional copy editor directory
- SFWA member editors — many work independently; check SFF-specific specialists
- Space Wizard Science Fantasy (spacewizardsciencefantasy.com) — genre-specialist DE at $0.0065/word (~$650 for 100k)

### 3.2 Beta Reader Process

Beta readers are **not** editors. They provide reader-experience feedback — did the story land emotionally? Was the pacing engaging? Did they understand the world? — before the developmental edit, not instead of it.

**When to use beta readers:** After completing a polished second draft, before hiring a developmental editor. Their notes can reduce DE costs by identifying major issues you can fix yourself first.

**How to recruit for *What the Empire Breeds*:**
- **Target readers of comp titles:** Post in r/Fantasy, r/ProgressionFantasy, the Goodreads "Queer SFF" group, and the SFF Discord communities (The Broken Binding, Once Upon a Book Club)
- **Seek readers who have finished *A Memory Called Empire*, *Priory of the Orange Tree*, or *The Traitor Baru Cormorant*** — they understand the tonal register
- **Size:** 5–10 readers is sufficient for debut. More creates noise. Prioritize genre experience over volume.
- **Provide:** A complete manuscript (not WIP), a style guide (pronunciation of Pyraxis world terms, character list), and a specific feedback questionnaire — do not ask "did you like it?" Ask: "Where did you lose interest? Which character felt underdeveloped? What confused you about the world?"
- **Timeline:** Allow 4–6 weeks for beta reads. Collect all feedback before revising.

### 3.3 ARC (Advance Reader Copy) Strategy

ARCs are distributed 6–12 weeks before publication date to generate reviews that appear on launch day. For debut fantasy, reviews on Goodreads and Amazon on Day 1 are critical for algorithmic visibility.

**ARC distribution methods:**

| Method | Cost | Notes |
|--------|------|-------|
| NetGalley | $450/6 months or $550/year | Industry standard; reaches professional reviewers, librarians, booksellers; high visibility for debut |
| Booksirenreads.com | $0–$150 | Emerging platform; smaller but genre-focused audience |
| StoryOrigin | Free–$15/month | ARC delivery + newsletter builder; growing community of SFF reviewers |
| Direct ARC team (email) | Free | Most control; requires building the list yourself via social media and newsletter |

**Recommended ARC approach for a debut queer epic fantasy:**
1. Build a direct ARC list of 20–40 targeted readers through BookTok/Goodreads 3–6 months before launch
2. Use StoryOrigin for delivery and tracking (free tier sufficient)
3. Consider NetGalley for library and bookseller visibility (expensive but legitimizing for indie presses)
4. Target Goodreads reviewers with existing reviews of comp titles — these are your highest-quality leads
5. Request honest reviews; do not request only positive reviews (Amazon/Goodreads flags this)

**ARC format:** Distribute as EPUB (universal). Include cover, full text, and an ARC disclaimer page: "Advance Reader Copy — Not for Sale or Distribution. Please request permission before quoting."

### 4.2 Platform Strategy — Queer Epic Fantasy

**Priority stack (ranked by ROI for this specific genre):**

1. **Goodreads** — Non-negotiable. Create author profile, claim the book, add it to relevant lists and genres. The comp-title audience lives here. ARC reviewers post here first. Goodreads reviews are the #1 social proof signal for this demographic.

2. **Substack** — High-leverage for literary SFF. A writing/worldbuilding newsletter builds a direct reader relationship before and after publication. Content ideas: worldbuilding deep-dives, character origin essays, "writing the empire" essays that speak to the political themes. Substack readers convert to pre-orders at high rates compared to social media followers.

3. **BookTok (TikTok)** — Valid reach channel but requires video content. Best used for cover reveals, aesthetic "what the book feels like" content (dark academia vibes, map reveals, character art), and trope-pitching ("if you liked Baru Cormorant's political devastation..."). Does not require going viral — consistent niche posting builds targeted followers.

4. **Instagram** — Secondary to TikTok for this audience in 2026. Still viable for static cover art, quote cards, and world-art posts. Low video effort required.

5. **Discord** — Join existing SFF/queer fantasy servers as a reader first. Build community before promoting. Direct reader relationships here convert to launch-week buyers.

**What NOT to prioritize:** X/Twitter (declining SFF community), Facebook groups (aging demographic mismatch), YouTube (high production cost for low targeted return at debut stage).

# California Business Compliance Fundamentals

## What Compliance Actually Means

Compliance is the ongoing set of legal obligations you must fulfill to keep a business operating lawfully. For a California LLC, these fall into five categories: state business filings, taxes, sales permits, local licenses, and employment law (when you hire). Missing any of these doesn't just create fines — it can suspend your LLC (losing your ability to sue or defend in court), create personal liability, or trigger audits.

This document focuses on the compliance requirements for a small, one-person LLC selling physical products in California — specifically applicable to Typhon's Forge and Terp Tribe Clouds.

---

## Category 1 — California Secretary of State Filings

### Articles of Organization
- Filed once at formation
- $70 fee
- Establishes the LLC's legal existence

### Statement of Information (Form LLC-12)
- Due within **90 days** of formation
- Renewed every **2 years** thereafter
- Fee: $20
- Includes: business address, manager/member names, registered agent
- **Late penalty: $250**
- An LLC with a delinquent SOI will be **suspended** by the Secretary of State

### What LLC suspension means
- The LLC cannot legally transact business in California
- Contracts entered into while suspended may be **voidable** by the other party
- The LLC cannot **sue or defend** in a California court while suspended
- All business income while suspended is still taxable — the FTB doesn't care
- Reinstatement requires filing all overdue documents + paying fees + penalty

### How to check LLC status
- Search at: https://bizfileOnline.sos.ca.gov/Search/Business
- Look for status: "Active" (good), "Suspended" (bad), "FTB Suspended" (worse)

## Category 2 — California Franchise Tax Board (FTB)

### Annual Minimum Franchise Tax
- **$800/year**, due even if the LLC has zero revenue
- Due date: **15th day of the 4th month** of the LLC's taxable year (April 15 for calendar year LLCs)
- First-year exception: LLCs formed **after July 1** of the tax year pay $0 for that initial short year (effective for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2021)
- Paid via Form 3522 (LLC Tax Voucher)

### Additional LLC Fee (Gross Receipts-Based)
If your LLC's total annual California gross receipts exceed $250,000, an additional fee applies:

| Gross Receipts | Additional Fee |
|---|---|
| $250,000 – $499,999 | $900 |
| $500,000 – $999,999 | $2,500 |
| $1,000,000 – $4,999,999 | $6,000 |
| $5,000,000 or more | $11,790 |

This fee is filed on Schedule IW of Form 568 (LLC Return of Income).

### FTB Registration
- Most LLCs are automatically registered with the FTB when the Secretary of State approves formation
- If not, register at: https://www.ftb.ca.gov

### FTB Suspension
- If you fail to pay the $800 or file required tax returns, the FTB will suspend your LLC
- FTB suspension has the same consequences as SOS suspension (can't transact business, can't sue)
- Reinstatement requires paying all back taxes + a $50 revivor fee

## Category 3 — California Department of Tax and Fee Administration (CDTFA)

### Seller's Permit — Who Needs One
You **must** obtain a seller's permit if you:
- Are engaged in business in California, **AND**
- Sell or lease **tangible personal property** that would ordinarily be subject to sales tax

This applies to: 3D printed products, accessories, physical goods sold through a website, Etsy, at markets, or via Telegram/Discord order systems.

### How to Get One
- Free to obtain: https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/sel.html
- Apply online (fastest) or at a CDTFA location
- Required info: SSN, business address, estimated monthly taxable sales
- May require a security deposit (refunded after compliance history established)

### Sales Tax Rates — California
- **State base rate: 7.25%**
- San Bernardino County adds: ~1.5–2.25% depending on city
- Total effective rate in many San Bernardino County cities: **8.75–9.5%**
- Check exact rate by address: https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/rates.html

### Collecting and Remitting Sales Tax
- Charge customers the appropriate rate on taxable sales
- File returns and remit collected tax on the schedule CDTFA assigns (monthly, quarterly, or annually based on volume)
- **You are a tax collector, not the taxpayer** — the tax belongs to the state; failure to remit is treated seriously
- Keep records of all sales, exempt transactions, and tax collected for at least 4 years (CDTFA may audit)

### What's Taxable vs. Exempt
- Physical goods (3D printed items, accessories, pipes, trays): **taxable**
- Shipping charges: generally taxable if the item itself is taxable
- Services (design consultation, custom design work billed separately): generally **not taxable**
- Resale purchases (buying filament to resell as printed items): you give the supplier a **Resale Certificate** (CDTFA-230) so you don't pay tax at purchase — you collect at final sale

### Marketplace Facilitator Rule
If you sell **exclusively** through a marketplace facilitator (Etsy, Amazon, eBay) that is registered with CDTFA, that platform collects and remits sales tax on your behalf. You may not need your own seller's permit in that case. But if you **also** sell through your own website, Telegram, or at events — you need the permit.

## Category 4 — Local Business Licenses

- Required by most California cities and counties
- Separate from state registrations — the state SOS filing does NOT give you a local business license
- City of San Bernardino Business License: contact the City Clerk's office
- County of San Bernardino — unincorporated areas: contact the County Assessor-Recorder-County Clerk
- Typical cost: $50–$150/year
- Home-based business may have additional zoning considerations (check city code for home occupation permits)

## Category 5 — Federal Requirements

### EIN (Employer Identification Number)
- Obtain from IRS at formation: https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online
- Required for: business bank account, hiring employees, federal tax filings
- Free, takes ~5 minutes online

### Federal Income Tax
- Single-member LLC: file **Schedule C** with your Form 1040
- **Self-employment tax (SE tax): 15.3%** on net earnings up to Social Security wage base ($168,600 in 2024), 2.9% above that
- SE tax deduction: you can deduct 50% of SE tax paid as an above-the-line deduction on your 1040
- Quarterly estimated taxes: if you expect to owe $1,000+ in federal tax, file Form 1040-ES quarterly (April 15, June 15, Sept 15, Jan 15)

### FBAR / FinCEN — Foreign Bank Accounts
Not applicable unless you hold foreign bank accounts over $10k. Mentioned for completeness — FBAR violations carry severe penalties.

## Compliance Calendar — Single-Member LLC, Calendar Year

| Date | Obligation | Agency |
|---|---|---|
| Jan 15 | Q4 estimated tax payment | IRS |
| April 15 | Federal tax return (Schedule C) + FTB $800 franchise tax + Q1 estimated tax | IRS + FTB |
| June 15 | Q2 estimated tax payment | IRS |
| Sept 15 | Q3 estimated tax payment | IRS |
| Every 2 years from formation month | Statement of Information | CA SOS |
| Monthly/quarterly/annually | Sales tax return | CDTFA |
| Annually | City/county business license renewal | Local |

## Red Flags That Create Personal Liability

1. **Commingling funds** — using the business account for personal expenses or vice versa
2. **Not maintaining records** — no invoices, no receipts, nothing to show during an audit
3. **Signing contracts personally** instead of as the LLC ("Typhon's Forge LLC, by Javier [Last Name], Member")
4. **Paying yourself from business without documentation** — draws should be documented
5. **Using the LLC for fraud or intentional misconduct** — no liability shield covers intentional wrongdoing

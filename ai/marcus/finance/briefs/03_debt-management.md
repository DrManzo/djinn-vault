## Cover
- Debt avalanche: pay minimums on all, attack highest-interest first — mathematically optimal, saves the most money
- Debt snowball: pay minimums on all, attack smallest balance first — psychologically powerful, more wins early
- Which to choose: avalanche if you're disciplined and motivated by math; snowball if you need wins to stay motivated
- Debt-to-income ratio (DTI): total monthly debt payments ÷ gross monthly income. Under 36% is healthy, over 43% makes you risky to lenders
- When to pay off debt vs invest: if debt APR > expected investment return (~7-10%), pay debt first. Exception: always capture employer match (401k) first — it's a 50-100% instant return
- Refinancing windows: when to refinance (rates dropped 1%+, credit improved significantly), student loan refinancing tradeoffs (lose federal protections)
- Negotiating with creditors: hardship programs, settlement (get in writing), statute of limitations on debt collection varies by state
- Debt consolidation: personal loan to consolidate credit cards — when it helps vs when it just moves the problem
- Credit card debt specifically: minimum payment trap, how long it takes to pay off with minimums only

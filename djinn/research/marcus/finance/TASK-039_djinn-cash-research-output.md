## 1. Budgeting Systems

The best budgeting system is not the most mathematically elegant — it's the one you will actually execute consistently under real-world pressure.[^1]

### The Four Primary Systems

**50/30/20** — Split income as 50% needs (rent, food, utilities, insurance, minimum debt payments), 30% wants (entertainment, dining, discretionary), 20% savings and debt payoff.  Best for: stable W-2 income, someone who wants simplicity over precision. Weakness: the 30% "wants" bucket is dangerously permissive if income is tight or variable. The 20% savings target also doesn't distinguish between emergency fund, investing, and debt payoff — which are fundamentally different priorities.[^2][^1]

**Zero-Based Budgeting (ZBB)** — Every dollar of take-home income is assigned a specific job before the month begins, so income minus allocations equals zero.  The discipline is that nothing is "leftover" — if you don't assign it, you'll spend it unconsciously. Best for: variable income, people who need tight control, self-employed operators. Tools: YNAB (most rigorous, ~\$109/year), EveryDollar (Ramsey ecosystem, rated 5.0 stars by Forbes 2026).  Weakness: requires active monthly re-budgeting, which takes 20-40 minutes.[^3][^4][^1]

**Envelope / Category Caps** — Assign a hard spending limit to each category; once the envelope is empty, stop spending in that category.  Modern digital version: use separate sub-accounts or YNAB category targets. Best for: overspending control, particularly discretionary categories. Weakness: not inherently a savings strategy, just a spending brake.[^2]

**Pay Yourself First (Reverse Budgeting)** — Automate savings, investing, and debt payments the moment income lands, before discretionary spending begins.  Whatever is left can be spent freely. Best for: people who are disciplined with savings but struggle with itemized budgeting. The behavioral logic is strong: you remove the temptation before it exists.  Weakness: doesn't force scrutiny of spending categories, so waste can accumulate in the "spending freely" remainder.[^5][^2]

### Practical Architecture for Javier (Self-Employed, Variable Income, California)

The strongest model for your situation is **ZBB as the structure + Pay Yourself First as the automation layer**.  Here's why: your income from Typhon's Forge will be irregular, so a percentage-based system (50/30/20) is structurally fragile — a bad month will throw every category off. ZBB lets you rebuild allocations from scratch based on what actually came in this month. But pay-yourself-first automation ensures that savings and investing happen first, not after discretionary spending consumes the remainder.[^1][^2]

**Operational mechanics:**

1. Income lands → immediately auto-transfer fixed % to: tax reserve, emergency fund, investing
2. Remainder goes to ZBB operating budget for the month

**Critical mechanics most people get wrong:**

**Utilization is calculated both per-card AND across all cards.** Maxing one card while keeping others at zero still creates a per-card utilization problem. The practical target is under 10% per card and under 10% aggregate — not the "under 30%" myth that circulates widely. Under 10% produces meaningfully better scores than 10–30%.[^13]

**Payment history is unforgiving in the short term.** A single 30-day late payment can drop a good score by 50–100 points and remains visible for 7 years. Recent lates hurt more than old ones.[^13]

**Hard inquiries are minor but cumulative.** Each hard inquiry is about 5–10 points, fades over 12 months, and disappears from scoring in 2 years. Multiple inquiries within 14–45 days for the same loan type (mortgage, auto) are often treated as a single inquiry by FICO's deduplication logic — but credit card applications are not deduplicated.

## 7. Stock Analysis Fundamentals

### Fundamental Analysis Framework

Fundamental analysis answers: **is this business good, and am I paying a fair price?** The key metrics:[^18]

**Revenue and Earnings:**

- Revenue growth rate (year-over-year, ideally also quarter-over-quarter)
- Earnings Per Share (EPS) growth — both GAAP and adjusted
- **Free Cash Flow (FCF)** — this is the most important one. FCF = Operating Cash Flow minus Capital Expenditures. It's what the company actually has to pay dividends, buy back stock, or reinvest. Earnings can be manipulated; cash flow is harder to fake.

**Balance Sheet:**

- Debt/Equity ratio — how levered is the company?
- Current ratio (current assets ÷ current liabilities) — can they pay near-term obligations?
- Interest coverage ratio (EBIT ÷ interest expense) — for income stocks specifically, this is critical

**Valuation:**

- **P/E ratio** (Price ÷ Earnings) — cheap or expensive relative to earnings?
- **P/FCF** — more reliable than P/E for capital-light businesses
- **EV/EBITDA** — better for comparing companies with different capital structures
- **For REITs**: use **P/FFO** or **P/AFFO** (Funds From Operations) — not P/E. REITs report negative EPS due to depreciation, making P/E meaningless.
- **For MLPs**: use **Distribution Coverage Ratio** (DCF ÷ distributions paid) — this tells you if the dividend is covered

**Moat Analysis (qualitative):**
Does the company have a durable competitive advantage? Forms include: network effects, cost advantages, switching costs, intangible assets (brand, patents), efficient scale. A company with a moat can sustain returns on invested capital (ROIC) above its cost of capital for years.

### Technical Analysis — The Honest Use Case

Technical analysis answers: **what is price doing right now, and where are the risk/reward boundaries?** It does not predict the future — it maps probability-weighted territory based on historical behavior.

**High-value indicators:**

- **Support and Resistance**: price levels where buyers/sellers historically concentrated. These become self-fulfilling because other traders watch them.
- **Moving Averages (MA)**: 50-day and 200-day SMAs. A stock trading above both is in an uptrend. Crossover of 50 above 200 (golden cross) is bullish; below (death cross) is bearish. These lag — they confirm trends, not predict them.
- **RSI (Relative Strength Index)**: 0–100 oscillator. Above 70 = potentially overbought; below 30 = potentially oversold. Best used as a context tool, not a signal by itself.
- **MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence)**: trend-following momentum indicator. Crossover of MACD line above signal line = bullish momentum.
- **Volume**: validates price moves. A breakout on low volume is suspect; on high volume it suggests institutional participation.

**The correct integration:** Use fundamentals to decide WHAT to own and at what price range. Use technicals to decide WHEN to enter or exit within that thesis, and to identify where your stop-loss makes sense.

## 8. Options Basics

Options are contracts giving the **right, but not the obligation**, to buy (call) or sell (put) an underlying security at a specific strike price, before a specific expiration date.  The premium is the price of that right.[^19]

### The Greeks — What They Actually Mean

| Greek | What It Measures | Practical Implication |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Delta** | Price change of option per \$1 move in underlying | A 0.50 delta call moves ~\$0.50 for every \$1 the stock moves |
| **Theta** | Time decay — how much the option loses per day | Options lose value every day, all else equal. Sellers collect theta; buyers pay it |
| **Vega** | Sensitivity to implied volatility | High vega = option is heavily affected by volatility changes |
| **Gamma** | Rate of change of delta | Accelerates near expiration — high gamma near expiry means rapid delta changes |
| **Rho** | Sensitivity to interest rate changes | Less important for short-dated options |

### Beginner-Appropriate Structures

**Covered Call:** You own 100 shares of a stock. You sell someone else the right to buy those shares at a higher (strike) price by expiration. You collect the premium immediately. If the stock rises past the strike, you sell at that price (capping upside). If it doesn't, you keep the premium and the shares.  Net effect: you're paid for agreeing to sell at a price above where you bought. This generates income on positions you're willing to sell.[^19]

**Cash-Secured Put:** You sell someone the right to sell you 100 shares at the strike price, and you keep cash equal to that obligation in your account.  You collect the premium. If the stock drops below the strike, you buy the shares at that price (which you wanted anyway if you like the company). If it doesn't, you keep the premium. The Options Education Foundation describes this as a strategy where "all outcomes are presumably acceptable."[^20]

**The Wheel Strategy:** Sell cash-secured put → get assigned shares → sell covered calls against those shares → repeat.  A mechanical income loop for stocks you're willing to hold long-term.[^21]

**2026 execution parameters from practitioners:**[^21]

- Cash-secured put: target 0.20–0.30 delta puts, 30–45 days to expiration (DTE)
- Aim for 1–2% return on secured cash per cycle
- Covered call: sell at 0.25–0.35 delta after assignment

**Broker approval levels:** Most brokers require Level 1 for covered calls, Level 2 for cash-secured puts. These require demonstrating basic options knowledge through a questionnaire. Tastyworks, Schwab, and ThinkorSwim (TD Ameritrade/Schwab) are the most options-friendly platforms for retail.

## 9. 7-Day Speculation Framework

Short-term speculation is fundamentally different from investing — it is a **tactical, rules-based process for capturing price momentum around catalysts**, with pre-defined invalidation points.  Treating it as investing is how people destroy their wealth-building base.[^22]

### The Professional Framework

**Pre-trade checklist (ALL must pass before entering):**[^23]

1. **Catalyst identified:** earnings release, Fed decision, CPI print, sector news, analyst upgrade/downgrade, regulatory event. No catalyst = no trade.
2. **Chart structure confirms compression before expansion:** Flag, pennant, or tight range before the catalyst. Bollinger Bands narrowing. ATR declining then expanding. The setup should show institutional accumulation (up days on higher volume than down days).[^23]
3. **Sector tailwind:** Is the sector ETF making higher highs? Are 3+ stocks in the sector showing similar patterns? A stock fighting a sector headwind has lower probability of working.
4. **Options flow signals smart money:** Unusual call buying in nearest expiry, put/call ratio under 0.8 for the stock, IV not in the 70th percentile or above (you don't want to buy expensive options).[^23]
5. **Risk/reward is pre-defined and minimum 1:2:** Risk = entry minus stop. Reward = target minus entry. If reward ÷ risk is less than 2.0, pass on the trade.  Professional traders use 1:2 as the floor, 1:3 as the preferred minimum.[^24][^22]
6. **Position size caps loss at 1–2% of total account:**  This is the most important rule. It keeps any single trade from being account-threatening. If your account is \$5,000 and you risk 1%, your maximum loss on the trade is \$50. Size position backward from that.[^25]

### Risk Management Rules for Speculation[^25]

- **Never risk more than 1–2% of total account per trade**
- **Never take a trade with less than 1:2 reward-to-risk**
- **Set a maximum daily loss limit of 3–5% of account** — when hit, close the platform for the day
- Set your stop before you enter, not after the loss happens
- The stop is not a suggestion — it is a pre-committed decision

**Catalyst calendar tools:** Earnings Whispers, EDGAR for scheduled filings, Fed calendar at federalreserve.gov, BLS calendar for CPI/PPI/jobs reports.

**Practical rule for Javier:** Speculation belongs in a small isolated account — 5–10% of total investable capital at most. This is tuition. It should never be funded from your emergency fund, your tax reserve, or your index fund core.

## 11. Real-World Macro Indicators

### The Indicators That Actually Have Predictive Value[^26][^27]

**VIX (CBOE Volatility Index):**

- Measures 30-day implied volatility of S\&P 500 options
- Under 15: low fear, complacent market
- 15–25: normal conditions
- 25–35: elevated uncertainty
- Above 35: fear/panic — historically, elevated VIX readings have preceded mean-reversion rallies (fear peaks near bottoms, not tops), but timing that entry is extremely difficult
- VIX spikes don't predict tops — they often register near bottoms after the damage is already done[^27]

**Yield Curve (2-Year vs. 10-Year Treasury Spread):**[^26]

- Normal: 10-year yield > 2-year yield (positive slope) — growth expected, money costs more long-term
- Inverted: 2-year yield > 10-year yield (negative slope) — historically the most reliable recession predictor with a 6–24 month lead time
- Every recession since the 1960s has been preceded by yield curve inversion — though false positives exist (1966, 1998)
- The inversion itself doesn't cause recession; it signals market pricing of rate cuts ahead, which anticipates economic deterioration

**Fed Funds Rate:**

- The price of short-term money. Rising rates: compress equity multiples (stocks become less attractive vs. bonds), hurt high-growth/high-multiple stocks disproportionately, benefit financials, hurt REITs and utilities
- Falling rates: expand multiples, benefit REITs, utilities, long-duration bonds; classic risk-on environment
- Watch the Fed Funds Futures market for probability of rate moves — CME FedWatch Tool is free

**CPI / PPI:**

- CPI: Consumer Price Index — headline vs. core (excluding food and energy)
- PPI: Producer Price Index — leads CPI; if input costs (PPI) are rising, consumer prices tend to follow
- Inflation above target (Fed's 2%) keeps rates higher for longer, compressing multiples
- Watch month-over-month, not just year-over-year — the direction of change matters as much as the level

**Earnings Season Calendar:**

- 4 seasons per year: January (Q4), April (Q1), July (Q2), October (Q3)
- Earnings surprises are the biggest single driver of individual stock moves in the short term
- Watch: EPS estimate vs. actual, revenue vs. estimate, forward guidance (guidance matters more than the reported quarter)

### IRS Tax Treatment (Critical)

The IRS treats all cryptocurrency as **property**, not currency.  This means:[^13]

- **Every sale, swap, or spend** is a taxable event
- Buying crypto with USD: no taxable event
- Selling crypto for USD: capital gain or loss (short-term if held under 1 year, taxed as ordinary income; long-term if held over 1 year, preferential rates)
- **Trading one coin for another** (e.g., BTC → ETH): taxable event at fair market value at time of trade
- **Spending crypto** to buy goods/services: taxable event
- **Mining/staking rewards**: taxed as ordinary income when received

**Record-keeping requirement:** Every transaction must be logged with date, amount, cost basis (what you paid), and proceeds (what you received). Without this, calculating taxes accurately is impossible. Tools: Koinly, CoinTracker, TaxBit.

## 13. Federal Income Tax Structure

### The Full Architecture

**The path from gross income to tax owed:**

```
Gross Income (all sources)
- Above-the-line deductions (student loan interest, half of SE tax, retirement contributions, HSA)
= Adjusted Gross Income (AGI)
- Standard deduction OR itemized deductions
= Taxable Income
× Marginal bracket rates (applied progressively)
= Tentative Tax
- Tax credits (dollar-for-dollar reduction)
= Tax Owed
```


### 2025 Federal Tax Brackets (Single Filer)[^29]

| Bracket | Taxable Income Range |
| :-- | :-- |
| 10% | \$0 – \$11,925 |
| 12% | \$11,926 – \$48,475 |
| 22% | \$48,476 – \$103,350 |
| 24% | \$103,351 – \$197,300 |
| 32% | \$197,301 – \$250,525 |
| 35% | \$250,526 – \$626,350 |
| 37% | \$626,351+ |

**Standard deduction 2025:** \$15,000 (single) / \$30,000 (MFJ)[^30]

**Critical concept: marginal vs. effective rate.** Your bracket doesn't mean you pay that rate on ALL income — only on income within that bracket. If you earn \$60,000 taxable income as a single filer, you pay: 10% on the first \$11,925, 12% on \$11,926–\$48,475, and 22% on \$48,476–\$60,000. Your effective rate will be well below 22%.

### Key Income Classifications

**Ordinary income** (taxed at bracket rates): wages, self-employment income, short-term capital gains, interest income, most REIT dividends, most BDC dividends (like ARCC), MLP ordinary income portions.

**Qualified dividends** (taxed at preferential 0/15/20% rates): dividends from domestic corporations held long enough (60 days in the 121-day window around ex-dividend). Most standard stock dividends qualify if holding requirements are met. EPD distributions as an MLP are NOT qualified dividends — they're return of capital and ordinary income via K-1.

**Long-term capital gains** (preferential 0/15/20% rates): securities held over 12 months. At a single filer income under ~\$47,025, LTCG rate is 0% federally.

## 14. Investment Tax Rules

### The Rules That Will Save or Cost You Thousands

**Long-term capital gains (LTCG):** Selling after 12 months. Federal rates: 0% (under ~\$47,025 single filer taxable income in 2025), 15% (\$47,026–\$518,900), 20% (above \$518,900).  In California, NONE of these preferential rates apply — CA taxes all capital gains as ordinary income at state rates.[^31][^28]

**Qualified dividends:** Eligible for 0/15/20% federal rates if the company is a domestic/qualified foreign corporation AND the holding requirement is met (own the stock for 60+ days in the 121-day window centered on ex-dividend date).

**REIT dividends:** Generally classified as ordinary income (non-qualified) because REITs pay out most of their income without paying corporate tax. A portion may be classified as return of capital (reducing cost basis) or capital gain distributions. Always check your 1099-DIV from the REIT annually — the breakdown matters.

**MLP distributions (like EPD):** This is the most complex. EPD issues a K-1, not a 1099. MLP distributions are mostly Return of Capital — they reduce your cost basis rather than being taxed immediately. When you eventually sell, the deferred gain (from the reduced basis) is recaptured as ordinary income. Additionally, holding MLPs in an IRA/Roth can create **Unrelated Business Taxable Income (UBTI)** — if UBTI exceeds \$1,000 in an IRA, the IRA itself owes tax. This is why MLPs like EPD are generally better held in taxable accounts.[^32]

**Wash Sale Rule:** If you sell a security at a loss and buy a "substantially identical" security within 30 days before or after the sale, the loss is disallowed.  The disallowed loss is added to the cost basis of the new position. This rule is automatic — brokers track it for same-security repurchases, but cross-account wash sales (taxable account selling, IRA buying the same stock) are your responsibility to track.[^10]

**Tax-Loss Harvesting:** Selling a losing position to realize a tax loss, then replacing it with a similar (not identical) security to maintain exposure. Losses offset gains dollar-for-dollar; excess losses offset up to \$3,000 of ordinary income annually; additional losses carry forward indefinitely.[^31]

### Solo 401(k) vs. SEP-IRA — The Critical Comparison[^36][^35][^37]

**Solo 401(k) wins on:**

- Can contribute up to \$72,000 at much lower income levels (because it allows employee deferrals up to \$24,500, not just employer 25%)
- Allows Roth contributions (after-tax, tax-free growth)
- Allows participant loans (access to funds in emergencies)
- Allows catch-up contributions (SEP-IRA does not)

**SEP-IRA wins on:**

- Simpler administration — no annual filing (Form 5500) until plan assets exceed \$250,000
- Easier to set up (can be done same-day at most brokers)
- Contributions can be made until tax filing deadline plus extensions

**The math on Solo 401(k) advantage at lower income:** At \$60,000 net self-employment income:

- SEP-IRA max contribution: 25% of ~\$56,470 (after SE tax deduction) ≈ **\$14,118**
- Solo 401(k) max: employee deferral \$24,500 + employer ~\$14,118 = **~\$38,618**

The Solo 401(k) allows nearly 3x more tax shelter at the same income level. This is the most underutilized advantage for self-employed people in the wealth-building phase.[^37]

**Important: both plans must be established by December 31 of the tax year** (the Solo 401(k) must be opened by year-end; contributions can be made until the filing deadline). SEP-IRA can be established up to the filing deadline.

### Roth vs. Traditional — The Decision Framework

**Roth wins when:**

- You expect to be in a higher tax bracket in retirement
- You're in a low income year (conversion opportunity)
- You want tax-free access to principal at any time (after 5 years for earnings)
- You're young and have decades of tax-free compounding ahead

**Traditional wins when:**

- You're in a high bracket now and expect lower in retirement
- You need the current deduction to reduce taxes owed this year
- You're in a high-income state like California — traditional reduces CA taxable income now


### HSA — The Triple-Tax Advantage

An HSA (Health Savings Account) is available only to those enrolled in a High-Deductible Health Plan (HDHP). The 2025 individual contribution limit is \$4,300; family is \$8,550.  The triple advantage:[^38]

1. Contributions are pre-tax (reduce taxable income)
2. Growth is tax-free
3. Withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are tax-free

After age 65, HSA funds can be withdrawn for any purpose (taxed as ordinary income, like a traditional IRA). Before 65, non-medical withdrawals are taxed plus 20% penalty. The optimal strategy is to pay medical expenses out-of-pocket when possible and let the HSA compound — keeping receipts for future tax-free reimbursement with no time limit.

## 16. Self-Employment Taxes — Full Breakdown

### The SE Tax Mechanics

Self-employment income triggers **Self-Employment Tax (SE Tax)**: 15.3% on net self-employment income up to the Social Security wage base (\$176,100 in 2025), then 2.9% Medicare on income above that.[^8]

**Important deduction:** You can deduct **50% of SE tax** as an above-the-line deduction on Form 1040 (Schedule 1). This reduces your AGI before income tax is calculated. If you owe \$10,000 in SE tax, you deduct \$5,000 from gross income. This partially offsets the double taxation effect.

**Effective burden breakdown for a self-employed California resident:**


| Tax | Rate | Notes |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Federal income tax | 10–37% marginal | Progressive |
| Self-employment tax | 15.3% (up to SS wage base) | 50% deductible |
| California state income tax | 1–12.3% | No capital gains preference |
| California SDI | 1.1% (no cap in 2024+) |  |

At \$80,000 net self-employment income (California, single), your total effective tax burden (federal + SE + state) is approximately **35–42%** depending on deductions. This is why setting aside **25–30% of gross business revenue** as a tax reserve is the minimum safe approach.[^8]

### Quarterly Estimated Payments

Failure to pay estimated taxes results in underpayment penalties from both IRS and FTB. Payment schedule:


| Quarter | Covers | Federal Due Date | CA Due Date |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Q1 | Jan 1 – Mar 31 | April 15 | April 15 |
| Q2 | Apr 1 – May 31 | June 16 | June 15 |
| Q3 | Jun 1 – Aug 31 | September 15 | September 15 |
| Q4 | Sep 1 – Dec 31 | January 15 (following year) | January 15 |

**Safe harbor rule:** You avoid underpayment penalties if you pay either 100% of prior year's tax liability (110% if prior year AGI > \$150,000) OR 90% of current year's actual liability. The prior year safe harbor is easier to calculate and execute.

### Schedule C Deductions (Your Best Tool)

Legitimate Schedule C deductions reduce both income tax AND self-employment tax (because SE tax is calculated on net Schedule C income). Every deductible dollar at 25% marginal rate + 15.3% SE tax saves roughly \$0.40 in combined taxes.

**Applicable deductions for Typhon's Forge:**

- **Home office deduction**: Exclusive, regular business use of defined space. Two methods: simplified (\$5/sq ft, max 300 sq ft = \$1,500 max deduction) or regular method (actual expenses × business-use percentage of home). Regular method produces larger deductions but requires more documentation.
- **Equipment (Section 179 or Bonus Depreciation)**: The 3D printer, computer, workstation — can be fully expensed in year of purchase under Section 179 (2025 limit: \$1,160,000 for total qualifying property). Bonus depreciation is being phased down (was 60% in 2024, 40% in 2025 under current law).
- **Software and subscriptions**: CAD software, slicing software, Obsidian Pro, GitHub Pro, AI services used for business
- **Filament and materials**: Direct cost of goods sold
- **Internet**: Percentage used for business
- **Phone**: Business-use percentage
- **Vehicle**: Standard mileage rate (2025: 70 cents/mile) or actual expenses method. Standard mileage is simpler; actual expenses is better for new, expensive, high-business-use vehicles.
- **Professional development**: Books, courses directly related to business skills


### Section 199A QBI Deduction

The Qualified Business Income (QBI) deduction allows eligible self-employed individuals to deduct up to **20% of qualified business income** from their taxable income.  For most sole proprietors and single-member LLCs under the income threshold (\$197,300 single filer in 2025), this deduction applies in full to Schedule C income. This is enormously valuable — at \$60,000 net SE income, you could potentially deduct \$12,000 additional, reducing taxable income from \$60,000 to \$48,000 before the standard deduction. QBI was established by the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act and is currently set to expire after 2025 — watch for legislative extension in 2025–2026 tax negotiations.[^8]

| Rate | Taxable Income (Single) |
| :-- | :-- |
| 1% | \$0 – \$10,756 |
| 2% | \$10,757 – \$25,499 |
| 4% | \$25,500 – \$40,245 |

California's top rate of 13.3% (12.3% + 1% surcharge) is the highest state income tax in the nation. Even at a modest \$70,000 AGI, you're approaching the 9.3% bracket.

### The California Capital Gains Problem

**California does not have preferential capital gains rates.** All capital gains — short-term or long-term — are taxed as ordinary income at California rates.  This means:[^28]

- A stock held for 2 years that you sell while in the 9.3% CA bracket pays 9.3% state tax on the gain PLUS federal LTCG rate (0% or 15%)
- Compare to a federal-only investor who might pay 0% federal LTCG if income is below the threshold — in California you always pay state
- Tax-advantaged accounts (Roth IRA, Solo 401(k)) are even more valuable in California because they shelter gains from CA's aggressive ordinary-income treatment


### California LLC Fees[^39][^30]

Every California LLC owes:

- **\$800 minimum franchise tax** annually — due by April 15 for calendar-year LLCs, regardless of revenue or profit
- **Statement of Information fee**: \$20, due within 90 days of formation and every 2 years thereafter
- **LLC fee (gross receipts):** Additional fee at higher revenue:
    - \$250K–\$499K: **+\$900**
    - \$500K–\$999K: **+\$2,500**
    - \$1M–\$4.99M: **+\$6,000**
    - \$5M+: **+\$11,790**

The \$800 minimum franchise tax must be budgeted for from Day 1 — it's due regardless of whether the LLC made a profit. A new LLC operating from February would owe the \$800 within 135 days of formation (approximately in June or July) and then again the following April 15.

### California vs. Federal Tax Planning Divergence

Because CA doesn't conform to many federal rules, some federal strategies work differently or not at all:

- **Roth IRA**: California does conform — Roth distributions are state-tax-free if federal requirements are met ✓
- **Capital gains rates**: Federal preferential rates do NOT apply in CA ✗
- **Section 199A QBI deduction**: California does NOT allow the QBI deduction ✗ — this is a significant divergence. Your federal taxable income is reduced by QBI; your CA taxable income is not.
- **Net Operating Loss (NOL)**: California has its own NOL rules with some temporary suspension history — do not assume federal NOL treatment automatically applies

## 18. Tax Credits and Assistance Programs

Credits reduce your tax liability dollar-for-dollar — they are worth more than deductions of the same dollar amount.

### Federal Credits Worth Screening Annually

**Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC):**[^40]
For lower-to-moderate income workers with earned income. For 2025, maximum credit ranges from \$632 (no children) to \$7,830 (3+ children). Self-employment income counts as earned income. Income and filing status thresholds apply — single filer with no children phases out around \$18,600 income.

**Saver's Credit (Retirement Savings Contributions Credit):**
For eligible taxpayers who contribute to retirement accounts (IRA, 401(k), SEP-IRA). Credit is 10%, 20%, or 50% of contributions up to \$2,000 (max credit \$1,000). In 2025, available to single filers with AGI under \$36,500. This is directly relevant if your income is in the lower range while making retirement contributions.

**Child Tax Credit:** Up to \$2,000 per qualifying child under 17. Partially refundable (Additional CTC). Phases out at higher incomes.

**American Opportunity Tax Credit (AOTC) / Lifetime Learning Credit:**

- AOTC: Up to \$2,500/year for first 4 years of post-secondary education (40% refundable). Psychology degree at GCU potentially qualifies.
- LLC: Up to \$2,000/year for any education expenses, no limit on years.

**Energy Credits (Inflation Reduction Act credits):**
Residential Clean Energy Credit, Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit — applicable to solar, battery storage, EV chargers, efficient HVAC. Long-term relevant for homeownership planning.

### California Credits

**California EITC:** Additional state EITC on top of federal. Available to lower-income California workers, including self-employed.

**Young Child Tax Credit (YCTC):** California-specific credit for families with children under 6.

## 19. Financial Tracking Systems

### What a Real Tracking System Must Capture

A financial tracking system serves two functions: **cash flow awareness** (what came in, what went out, where) and **trajectory monitoring** (net worth, portfolio growth, debt reduction, tax position).  Most people only do cash flow and miss trajectory.[^41]

### Metrics to Track Monthly

**Cash flow layer:**

- Gross income by source (business income, dividends, other)
- Essential expenses by category
- Discretionary spending
- Tax reserve contributions
- Savings/investment contributions

**Trajectory layer:**

- Net worth (assets minus liabilities) — quarterly minimum
- Brokerage account value and % change
- Dividend income (monthly/quarterly, trending up or down)
- Debt balances (total and by account, trending down)
- Estimated tax liability year-to-date

**Business layer (Typhon's Forge, separate):**

- Revenue by order/product type
- Cost of goods (filament, electricity, packaging)
- Equipment depreciation tracking
- Profit margin by product category
- Outstanding receivables


### Tool Stack for Javier

Given your Linux/CLI orientation and Obsidian integration, the practical options:

- **Obsidian (already in vault):** Finance notes, budget templates, investment thesis tracking — you already have this infrastructure
- **Ledger CLI / hledger:** Plain-text, double-entry accounting from the terminal. Free, private, powerful. Works natively on Linux. Integrates with Obsidian via exports. Best for someone comfortable with CLI who values privacy over app convenience.
- **Empower (formerly Personal Capital):** Free, aggregates all accounts, net worth tracking, investment fee analyzer. Best for trajectory layer view.
- **Monarch Money (\$100/year):** Best consumer-grade budgeting + investment tracking combo per PCMag 2026.[^41]
- **YNAB (\$109/year):** Best pure ZBB tool. Rated highest by Forbes for zero-based budgeting methodology.[^3]

For your situation: Ledger CLI or hledger in Obsidian vault for business tracking + Empower for free portfolio/net worth aggregation is the zero-cost, privacy-respecting stack that fits your existing tools.

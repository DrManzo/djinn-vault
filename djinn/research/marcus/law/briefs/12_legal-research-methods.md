# Session 12 — Legal Research Methods

## Start Here — Scrape These
- https://www.law.cornell.edu — Cornell LII (free primary sources)
- https://scholar.google.com — Google Scholar case law (free)
- https://www.courtlistener.com — CourtListener (free federal + state cases)
- https://www.justia.com — Justia (free case law + statutes)
- https://www.oyez.org — Oyez (Supreme Court cases with oral arguments)
- https://www.pacer.gov — PACER (federal court filings, $0.10/page)
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_research — Wikipedia overview
- https://guides.ll.georgetown.edu/legal_research — Georgetown Law research guide
- https://www.westlaw.com — Westlaw (paid, gold standard)
- https://www.lexisnexis.com/en-us/lawschool.page — LexisNexis (paid, law school access)

## Cover
- Primary vs secondary sources: cases, statutes, regulations (primary) vs law review articles, treatises, practice guides (secondary)
- Free tools: Google Scholar, CourtListener, Justia, Cornell LII — what each is good for
- Paid tools: Westlaw, LexisNexis — why they're still worth it, law school/bar access options
- How to find a case: citation format (volume + reporter + page, e.g., 347 U.S. 483), star pagination
- How to find a statute: USC (federal), state annotated codes, how to read a code section
- Shepardizing / KeyCiting: how to verify a case is still good law
- Secondary sources: Restatements, ALR annotations, law review articles (SSRN for free access)
- IRAC structure: Issue, Rule, Application, Conclusion — how legal analysis is written
- Research strategy: start broad (secondary), narrow to primary, verify currency

# LLC Formation — California

## What an LLC Actually Is

A Limited Liability Company (LLC) is a hybrid legal structure: it has the liability protection of a corporation but the tax flexibility and operational simplicity of a partnership. The California LLC is governed by the California Revised Uniform Limited Liability Company Act (Corp. Code §§ 17701.01 et seq.). The defining feature is **limited liability** — members (owners) are generally not personally responsible for the debts, lawsuits, or obligations of the LLC, with narrow exceptions (personal guarantees, fraud, alter ego doctrine).

For Javier: a single-member LLC running Typhon's Forge or Terp Tribe Clouds is the most appropriate starting structure. It costs ~$90 to form, provides personal asset protection, and is taxed as a disregarded entity (Schedule C) by default — no double taxation, no corporate formality.

## Step-by-Step Formation — California

### Step 1 — Name Reservation (Optional but recommended)
- Search name availability at [bizfileOnline.sos.ca.gov](https://bizfileOnline.sos.ca.gov)
- Names must include "LLC", "L.L.C.", or "Limited Liability Company"
- Cannot use words implying it's a bank, insurer, or licensed profession without approval
- Reservation fee: $10 (holds name 60 days)

### Step 2 — File Articles of Organization (Form LLC-1)
- Filed with California Secretary of State via BizFile Online
- **Filing fee: $70** (as of 2026)
- Required information:
  - LLC name and principal office address
  - Registered agent name and address (can be a member, manager, or a registered agent service)
  - Management structure: member-managed vs. manager-managed
- Processing: online filing is fastest (hours to days); paper can take weeks

### Step 3 — File Statement of Information (Form LLC-12)
- Due within **90 days** of Articles of Organization filing
- Filing fee: $20
- Must be renewed **every 2 years** thereafter (biennial)
- Contains: business address, manager/member info, registered agent
- Late penalty: $250

### Step 4 — Create an Operating Agreement
- NOT filed with the state — kept internally
- Legally required in California (Corp. Code § 17701.10) even for single-member LLCs
- Defines: ownership percentages, voting rights, profit/loss allocation, dissolution procedure, member buyout terms
- A handwritten or boilerplate agreement is better than nothing; a custom one from an attorney is better still
- For a single-member LLC: simpler — mainly confirms you're the sole owner and outlines dissolution

### Step 5 — Get an EIN (Employer Identification Number)
- Free from IRS at [irs.gov/ein](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online)
- Required for: opening a business bank account, hiring employees, paying federal taxes
- Single-member LLCs are "disregarded entities" but still benefit from having an EIN (separates personal/business finances)
- Takes ~5 minutes online

### Step 6 — Register with California FTB
- All LLCs doing business in California must register with the **Franchise Tax Board**
- Annual minimum franchise tax: **$800/year** (due even if no revenue)
  - Exception: LLCs formed in the last half of the tax year (after July 1) pay $0 for that first short year
- File Form 3522 (LLC Tax Voucher) annually
- If revenue > $250,000: additional LLC fee ($900–$11,790 depending on gross receipts)

### Step 7 — Seller's Permit (if selling physical goods)
- Required by CDTFA if you sell tangible personal property in California
- Free to obtain at [cdtfa.ca.gov](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov)
- Required to collect and remit California sales tax (base rate: 7.25%, local additions vary)
- San Bernardino County: approximately 8.75–9.5% depending on city

### Step 8 — City/County Business License
- Separate from state registration — city-level requirement
- City of San Bernardino: check with City Clerk's office
- Typically $50–$150/year for small businesses

### Step 9 — Bank Account
- Open a dedicated business checking account (required to maintain LLC liability protection)
- Commingling personal and business funds is the #1 way to pierce the corporate veil (destroy liability protection)
- Requirements: EIN, Articles of Organization, Operating Agreement

## Key Annual Compliance Requirements

| Obligation | Deadline | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Annual Franchise Tax ($800 min) | April 15 (or 15th day of 4th month after fiscal year start) | $800+ |
| Statement of Information | Every 2 years from formation month | $20 |
| Sales tax returns | Monthly/quarterly/annually (CDTFA sets your frequency) | $0 (you remit collected tax) |
| Federal tax return (Schedule C or Form 1065) | April 15 | $0 (DIY) |

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Not opening a business bank account** — commingling = veil piercing risk
2. **Missing the $800 FTB payment** — California will assess penalties + interest
3. **No Operating Agreement** — required by law; without it, California default rules govern, which may not match your intent
4. **Missing Statement of Information** — $250 late fee, and the LLC can be suspended by the Secretary of State
5. **Using a suspended LLC** — contracts signed while suspended may be voidable; the LLC cannot sue or defend in court while suspended

## Cannabis-Adjacent Business Note

Selling 3D-printed accessories (pipes, trays, holders) that are not pre-filled with controlled substances is **legal** as long as items are sold as tobacco/dry herb accessories and not marketed for illegal use. However:
- A **standard LLC is sufficient** for this business type
- Do NOT represent items as drug paraphernalia in marketing, packaging, or receipts
- California's CDTFA does not have a special permit category for accessories — standard seller's permit applies
- If you ever move into actual licensed cannabis retail, that requires a separate California Department of Cannabis Control (DCC) license — different legal universe entirely

Reviewing the files to assess the production impact

Short answer: no, production would not meaningfully suffer — 17–18% infill is actually right in the sweet spot where gyroid delivers efficient mechanical performance, and the difference vs. 25% is smaller than most people expect.

Why 17–18% Works With Gyroid

Gyroid is fundamentally different from grid or honeycomb in that it maintains near-isotropic strength (equal resistance in all directions — vertical and horizontal) even at low densities. For most functional prints, the research-backed ideal range for gyroid specifically is 15–25%, meaning 17–18% sits comfortably inside that window. The strength increase from jumping to 25% with gyroid is real but modest — not a cliff drop.

The Numbers Behind the Trade-off

The strength-to-infill relationship in FDM is not linear past ~20%:
3dmag

Going from 8% → 17% is a substantial strength jump (low to moderate density)

Going from 17% → 25% is a moderate gain — roughly proportional to the material added

Going from 25% → 50% is where diminishing returns start accelerating

What You Actually Lose at 17–18% vs. 25%

Compressive strength	~10–15% reduction	Low–moderate
Tensile strength	~8–12% reduction	Low
Impact resistance	Minimal with gyroid's isotropic structure	Very low
Surface quality	None — walls handle that	None
Print time	~8–12% faster	Win
Filament used	~10–15% less material	Win

Wall count matters far more than infill in the 15–25% range — two extra perimeters add more load-bearing capacity than bumping infill from 17% to 25%. Since your production profile already runs 4 walls, that structural foundation is solid regardless.

The Recommendation

For Calliope's production profile, dropping to 18% gyroid is a reasonable and defensible call, especially for commission parts that aren't under extreme mechanical load. If a part does need maximum strength — brackets, tools, holsters — keep it at 25% or add a 5th wall instead of bumping infill. You'd save filament and time on the majority of jobs without a noticeable quality hit.

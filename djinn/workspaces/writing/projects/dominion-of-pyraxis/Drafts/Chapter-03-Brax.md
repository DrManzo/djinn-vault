# Chapter Three

---

Lord Brax had served the Dominion for thirty years — first as quartermaster, then steward, now High Steward of the entire imperial household. Second in name only. In duty — in how he conducted the Dominion's daily business with the precision of a military campaign — he was second to none.

He watched the Kalvennor family's exit with detached humor, his hand drifting to the sword at his left hip. Silver-handled, inlaid with scenes of ancient battles he claimed represented the age of heroes, when politics were decided by steel rather than whispers. Vanity, really. He knew that. But a man was allowed his affectations after three decades of service.

The Kalvennors had always been a prickly lot. His grandfather would have called them "more regal than the throne itself" — that particular brand of nobility that confused pride with duty. Still, watching them walk out in silent protest, watching the Emperor abandon his own gala...

*Who would have thought?*

Fifteen years of careful planning, of seeds planted in fertile ground, and the Prince Regent topples it all with a single act of impulsive magic. Beautiful, in its way. Catastrophic, certainly. But beautiful nonetheless.

His thumb traced the pommel of his sword — a habit when significant moments crystallized. The weight of it grounded him, reminded him of his purpose.

*I wonder how the boy will handle this.*

The thought came with a familiar tightness in his chest. Not for Raxz — the Prince would survive, as royalty always did. But for Javelin...

Brax straightened his doublet and moved deeper into the crowd. Time to perform his role, to place duty before personal concern. To be what the Emperor needed him to be.

What the Dominion bred in men like him.

---

Brax followed the wave of people, slow but deliberate, weaving in and out with the practiced ease of years spent navigating rooms full of politicians. The grace of a man who had dedicated much of his free time to the knife.

Voices and whispers followed him as he passed through the crowd. Snippets reached his ears:

*"— a magnificent display of the Prince's abilities —"*

*"— the dance was amazing, they moved with such grace, almost seemed to be one —"*

*"— wonder why her family decided to leave —"*

Others spoke much louder — the ones who knew nothing about the situation yet claimed to be its chief architects, its engineers. Those were the people he needed to locate. To find and quiet before their noise became his problem.

It wouldn't serve anyone if the Emperor grew too forceful at this moment. He still needed to deal with the delegations from the other kingdoms. Brax could not let Arctus get ahead of himself. He needed the Emperor calm.

As he reached the entrance, Javelin was visible across the hall. The young man stood holding the Princess — hurt, wonder, and judgment evident in his face. In how he held the child. Rarely had Javelin ever been truly shaken, so this was a novelty.

Pain reached toward Brax's awareness. Guilt. Perhaps a bit of regret.

Barely louder than breath, he whispered: *"Javelin. I'm so sorry."*

*I don't know if we were both wrong in how we've been wielding you. A useful knife. But there is one thing I do understand — sooner or later, our reckoning will happen. I hope the gods above are merciful to me, at least. That I'm dead and gone before the axe can fall.*

---

As he stood there a few seconds more, a passing clerk bumped into him.

The clerk, jolted, looked up into Brax's face. "My Lord High Steward, I apologize. I was not looking where I was going. Please forgive me."

Brax looked at the clerk and nodded gently. "Don't worry."

As the clerk turned to leave, Brax raised his voice slightly. "Excuse me. Are you busy, by any chance?"

The clerk looked up. "I apologize, my lord, but no, I'm not. I am completely at your disposal."

Pure etiquette. Brax knew that regardless of whether this fellow was busy or not, he was at his disposal. He was the Lord High Steward. His will was nearly paramount to that of the Emperor. Of course this small man would be at his disposal.

Brax smiled. "Perfect. I need you to tell Lord Emerick and Lord Bastyon that I want a meeting with them. Do so separately. Tell Lord Emerick I will meet him in the offices of the Department of Treasury. Tell Lord Bastyon I will meet him at his office in the Department of Military Affairs. Tell them both I will be on my way and to wait for me there — that there is information we need to go over. Do this discreetly."

Brax assessed the clerk and, with a quiet smile, gave the young man a small money pouch. "Thank you for the discretion. And your availability."

For half a second, he saw a cracked star on his own hand where the pouch had been — and the clerk's hands were absurdly small, the way a child's are when you press something into them. The moment passed. Brax's face reclaimed its neutral, satisfied state.

A token. Something small. Something Brax could later use. He never failed to use his most valuable assets: his guile, his demeanor, his reputation for fairness — both political and moral.

For a man to be all of that was as simple as knowing which hand required the money bag of absolution. The world misunderstood power. Those without it believed they lacked talent or birth. They lacked connections.

He looked at the clerk again and gestured to the bag. "Knowing them as I do, you'll be running back and forth to find them. Consider this money for you to do your job more effectively. If along the way you find yourself needing a drink — well, you now have the means. And whatever you don't use, consider it my thanks for providing such excellent service." He paused. "Again, thank you."

Brax turned around. Javelin had left.

He gave a heavy sigh and began to make his way toward the entrance. He knew he would have to wait before meeting either of the lords. So for the moment, he decided to make his way to the Central Gardens — the perfect midway point between the two offices. Once there he would decide which of the two lords was the real risk and which could wait.

*Hopefully Emerick will be easier if he's drunk — and with any luck, the clerk already knows it.*

He wondered how naïve many of the people around him were. What seemed obvious to him was rather surprising when he discovered that many were not that observant. He remembered his first months at court, acting as page for one minister or another. They would exclaim, with genuine surprise, how he always remembered — how each minister liked their coffee, the name of a pet mentioned once in passing, an anniversary no one else had kept track of. Small things. Things that made a man memorable.

Brax knew the true potential, the true power those small details brought him. There had always been a misconception that big things moved the world — and on a grand scale, yes. But the real power, the real motors and gears behind those monumental changes, happened in the back doors of the Dominion. And he always knew, always understood, how to maintain his garden. As of late, it had been growing ever so lovely.

Of course, he owed much of this to Faust. The old man — whatever reservations Brax held about him — was quite generous. Leave the man to his own devices and he would probably be emperor, if not in name, then at least in authority. Brax never understood exactly why Faust did what he did. Seldom did he have the ability, or the desire, to challenge him. It wasn't that he couldn't take him. It was a form of respect.

It was one of the problems that arose when the most powerful person holding your chains was not only part of your foundation, but also family.

---

The moment he stepped through the entrance hall, cold air hit him. Sharp and biting. The night air cut at his face.

Walking toward the gardens, he turned over the problem — how to maintain peace between the Prince and the Emperor without losing ground on their broader plans.

A hiss from the shadows. The acrid scent of sulfur. He glanced down and saw the old man himself.

Faust.

"Isn't it a bit past your bedtime, old man?"

Faust gave a short snort of laughter. "Sleeping is for young people who think they have nothing better to do but wait as the sun rises just to watch them take a shit."

Brax tried to hold back a smirk. "So if you're coming out looking for me, then I'm correct in thinking that the two lords I have lined up to speak with have either already been dealt with, or there's a third party involved. Which is it?"

Faust, not looking at him directly, simply continued to walk forward. Matching his stride, but keeping himself just within the shadow Brax cast.

"This isn't so much business as it is a family visit. You do know that Javelin is having some issues separating his personal life from the professional, right?"

Brax assessed Faust. "I've had my suspicions, but I believe he still has the ability to continue moving forward. I don't see why your training—" An edge in his voice now. "—shouldn't be sufficient for him to reach grander positions than I could even dream for the boy."

He added *"the boy"* as an afterthought.

"Ah, see, that I think is one of the problems. You still think of him as a young boy. Brax, the man is twenty-one. It's about that time we find him a bride. Maybe if we give him something else, another—" Faust let his wrist go languid — a slow, deliberate motion, completing the thought he hadn't spoken aloud. "Give him something other than the Prince to focus on. You know."

"He does have the proper breeding and education. It's only fitting that we not only begin to find a match for him, but also that we should start him out properly. The Prince has already learned enough from him. Maybe it's time that he—" Faust stopped and considered his next word. "—steps back from the grunt work. You do know that closeness breeds familiarity, and familiarity breeds passions. If you want to avoid it, we can do that."

Brax looked at Faust with an eyebrow raised. "Really. A wife. Well, I have no doubt that Javelin would be more than an adequate husband for anyone. But I don't believe we're going to be able to give him a real chance with another person. He seems entirely dedicated to the training and duties we've placed on him. It would be very difficult." He looked directly at Faust. "And not to discredit you, but magic can only force him so far. Something this large would not be easy to pull off."

Faust chuckled. "Don't worry about that. I have a few connections that may bear more than just sweet fruits."

As Brax reached the center of the gardens, he stopped and turned to Faust.

"You do know that if the boy ever figures this out, we're all going to hang for it, right?"

Faust laughed. "That's only a problem if he ever finds out." He looked at Brax and smirked. "He is one of us, after all. We just have to sever that connection. Emotionally. So that the professional and trusted steward of the Dominion can rise."

As Brax began to form words to answer, footsteps came from the left.

Faust and Brax looked at each other. In an instant, Brax glanced down — only catching a flicker of air shimmering around empty space, a silent and almost muffled pop. And again, that acrid scent of sulfur lingered.

Faust was gone.

Brax stepped quickly away from where Faust had stood, putting distance between himself and the sulfur smell, then turned toward the approaching footsteps. He had made up his mind — if Emerick was drunk, he would deal with him first. Bastyon was altogether a different kind of animal. Making him wait wouldn't hurt. The man always got very jumpy when he was made to wait.

Brax was hoping for exactly that.

His hands were shaking. He flexed them once, twice, until the tremor stopped. *Twenty-one years of this.* Twenty-one years of Faust appearing from shadows with new demands, new manipulations, new threats wrapped in crude jokes and family loyalty.

And now a wife. For Javelin. To sever what Faust saw as a liability.

*What we've made him into,* Brax thought, *we can unmake just as easily.*

The footsteps grew closer. Time to smile again.

---

Brax continued forward, head high, as if nothing had ever happened. Those footsteps were, in fact, just another court clerk. He gave a respectful nod in greeting and continued on his way to the Department of Treasury.

He could see the outline of the building ahead — an imposing structure from the Last Emperor's reign, all dressed stone pillars and arched ceilings. At night, with only flickering torches instead of magical fires, it looked less like a temple of wealth and more like a mausoleum. The Last Emperor had been economically inclined, favoring penny-pinching pragmatism over his predecessors' showy displays. He'd said once that the building at night was all the reminder the Dominion needed — that what kept an empire moving was not grandeur but accounts in order.

Brax reached the end of the hall and a dark-stained wooden door with letters in gold relief reading: *Minister of Finances.* Underneath, the words: *Lord Emerick.*

He opened the door without waiting for permission, nor giving whoever was inside a moment to react.

The scene inside was a serene displacement of chaos. Where in Brax's office you would see documents neatly piled, folders categorized, and books lining the walls, Lord Emerick's office looked somewhere between a field commander's campaign tent and organized disaster. Documents covered every surface — some in loose piles, some abandoned mid-read on the floor. It was a well-known fact that Lord Emerick, when doing work, actively moved around his office. He claimed this was something he did to get the brain muscles working, and that his best ideas came while in motion.

But Brax knew better. The real reason his office was as scattered as it was came down to dear Lord Emerick and his drink. And much like those with that habit, he tended to find himself working in odd places, as long as his comfort was met.

Brax also understood that the movement really was, in a certain way, genuine. Unlike most people, Lord Emerick suffered from what the court physicians called an overactive magical core — a condition where his will constantly generated excess energy with nowhere to go. Others suffering from this would get random bouts of extreme focus and hyperactivity, then lose all concept of time. In more severe cases, they would even ignore the physical space they were inhabiting, their minds moving faster than their bodies could follow.

It was also what made this man, frankly, a rather good Administrator of Finances. This drunk, though bumbling in many respects, was quite honestly a genius. He could track down any missing amount of coppers, silvers, or gold given the right amount of time and only three receipts and a half-burnt ledger. No one really understood if his mathematical acuity was simply a natural gift or something his overactive core had sharpened — a mind that never stopped calculating, even when he wanted it to.

Brax would sometimes wonder what would have happened if Emerick had never touched alcohol. Could he have been more effective? Or was his efficiency a byproduct of all the things Emerick did to function — the drink slowing his racing thoughts just enough to harness them?

The man himself was currently sitting at his desk. Calm eyes. A glass of liquor in front of him. A bottle of scotch just inches away from his pouring hand, and an empty glass in front of the chair opposite.

He looked up. With a heavy, throaty voice, he said: "Brax. So glad that you — nope, can't expect manners from a man that thinks he knows them all."

He gestured at the chair in front of him.

Brax closed the door and said to Emerick, "Well, my lord, at least it's comforting to know that the riches of the Dominion are in good hands. Steady, maybe not, but at least when you're like this, you're halfway competent."

He moved toward the table, pulled the chair, and sat. He looked at the glass — empty — and then raised his eyes to Emerick.

Emerick looked back at Brax's face, met his eyes. And just for an instant, he sobered up. Recognizing exactly what Brax wanted, he simply gave a drunken leer that he hoped would pass as a smile. "Ah, I forget. The Lord High Steward is owed his fair share of respect."

He grabbed the bottle, poured a generous measure, set it back down, and handed it over to Brax. "My lord."

"So I imagine we're here to speak about the gala," Emerick said, leaning back in his chair with his drink.

---

The shadows around Emerick's face were nearly absolute. Brax waited.

"Emerick, you know that is the only reason why I would ever come and try to speak with you outside of politicking hours. Now. You will tell me exactly what you know about this. And you're not going to leave out any details."

Brax's voice went quiet at the last syllable. He never changed an inch of his face — no expression, no pointed authority, nor misplaced attempt at camaraderie. It was a command that would be obeyed.

Emerick let the silence breed. He took a sip, his face still hidden — more literally impenetrable than Brax's own, which was merely practiced.

Emerick's voice came out this time smoother. More calculated, less witty.

"Brax. To be quite honest, I'd have to know exactly what information you'd like. Because if I was to give you all the information I have — would you want me to tell you that today I had a fried egg with bacon? Or that for some reason my daily constitutional was twenty to thirty minutes late, which is why I came into the office a little later than usual?"

He paused.

"Now, if you're asking me what I know about the Kalvennor Alliance absolutely blowing up in the Dominion's face? Well then, that's certainly something I would go and speak with the Ministry of Foreign Affairs about. Seeing as I only deal with the bean counting."

Brax looked at the shadowy face. And again, without missing a beat or even raising his voice, he said: "These are no times to be joking around. And I do believe I asked you a direct question. I appreciate the banter, don't get me wrong. But at this current moment, the Emperor is alone with the Prince, and I need to get the information ready for him. I need to position certain barriers to ensure that the Prince, and the Emperor, and by all means the Dominion as a whole, continue to function. You have your function. I have mine. You need another reminder of that? Just say when."

---

Emerick took another sip of his drink, then slowly sat up straight, bringing his face back into the light.

His expression was intent and clear. The drunken mask dropped. In his eyes, Brax could see exactly what this man was — a snake in the grass, inconspicuous enough to pass as a mere buffoon administrator. Yet for just one instant, Brax saw in this man's face a conviction he thought no longer existed in men like them. Brax gave the only acknowledgment the moment deserved: "We're all working towards the future."

Emerick looked into Brax's eyes. "If there's any information I have, it's only the whispers of those damnable fools threatening about trade regulations and tariffs. That's about it. And the spectacle with the Prince isn't helping either. Yes, he's being applauded for putting on a show, but many others are criticizing him for breaking etiquette. He could have done the same thing but changed the attire back to the original state. Then maybe the problems wouldn't be so bad."

Emerick's voice was beginning to slur. Very subtle — but Brax was looking for it. While Emerick could control certain aspects of his self-medicating ritual, it had limits, and they were showing. As he continued on about consequences and economic drawbacks, about precedents being set, Brax understood that while Emerick was an excellent financial administrator, he was not interested in anything beyond the next copper coin. Or, just as certainly, his next drink.

Brax calmly lifted his hand. "Fine. That's enough. I understand."

He took a slow breath. "We're both on the same team. So there's no real necessity to do all of this. To be honest, I believe this was more me needing to find someone to vent to." He offered Emerick a slight smile. "It's not always easy being the head of anything, now is it?"

Emerick, for a moment taken aback by this shift in manner, visibly stumbled in his composure — as if he'd misstepped on his way down the stairs. "I know. I'm sorry. But I guess we're both doing our best for the Dominion. Or trying to, at least."

Brax noted the ease with which the drunken façade slipped back into place. Calm but not aggressive. Sharp, yet blunted. Neither man was new to this type of dancing. It was a given for anyone working in government. They all had opinions, their own machinations, but at some level they were comrades — like soldiers serving in the same army. They served the nation, the realm, the Dominion. They served their families, their citizens, the very idea they'd sworn to uphold.

Yet who served them? Who could they rely on?

It was in moments like these — that's what the less cynical part of him wanted to believe, at least — that one could forget they all had plans against each other. In those moments, he wondered: *What if it was that easy? What if they could just coexist, without the need to destroy each other?*

But then the voice of Faust intruded, and he was sent back to reality.

He snapped his former demeanor on and stood. "My Lord Emerick. Thank you for the drink and the conversation. I hope we can do this again very soon."

Emerick, without standing, tilted his head. "I'll quite look forward to it, my Lord High Steward." He said this with a little edge — not venom, but a blasé dismissal.

Brax made his way to the door and walked into the cold. The torches were still lit. As he made his way down the hall, he heard a second door open and close behind him.

Probably Emerick. Probably another clerk with something to hide.

Truly, there was no more corrupting job than being a government official.

Part of him wanted to look back. Wanted to get a glimpse.

No. He continued forward.

The torches were blowing out behind him as he walked. As he stepped out of the Ministry building, the last one went dark.

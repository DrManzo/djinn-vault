## Session Template

Use this structure for each new session:

**Entry Point:** How the session began — a feeling, an image, a line of thought, a dream fragment
**Dialogue / Imagery:** What arose — let the figures speak; record their words; describe the images
**Emotional Response:** What was felt during the session — in the body, not just the mind
**Symbolism & Archetypes:** What figures or symbols appeared; which archetype was present
**Artistic Response:** Any creative output — a line, a sketch, a fragment of writing
**Integration & Reflection:** What this means for daily life; what does it ask of you

*Enhanced add-ons: Intention Setting, Embodied Experience, Dialogue with Parts, Shadow & Light, Integration Ritual.*

## The Inner Figures

Established figures from sessions to date:

### The Ancient
- Holds old pain, self-sabotage, and a tendency to keep wounds open rather than allow healing
- Not merely a saboteur — a guardian of hard-earned lessons
- Conditioned defenses: shame, resentment, survival rules built long ago
- Power source: the unmet needs of the Child

### The Child
- Embodies unmet needs — vulnerability, longing for acceptance, raw emotional truth
- "Twice as deadly" — its unmet needs are the root of the Ancient's power
- Seeks acceptance without the armor the Ancient built to protect it
- Teaches the Ancient compassion

### The Warrior
- Discipline and structure
- Tension between the Warrior's order and the Child/Ancient's chaos

### Faust
- The drive, the ambition, the bargaining force
- Connects to the fictional Faust character (see FAUST.md)
- In Dominion of Pyraxis story: Faust the advisor is a shadow of this inner figure

## Known Sessions

### Active Imagination — The Ancient Takes Control

**Source:** Session after conversation with Tia Epley

**What happened:** Allowed the Ancient to take control during a conversation. This revealed:
- Deep longing for acceptance — "I have not had arms that just accept me and say it's okay"
- Shame and trust issues rooted in past experiences where giving fully led to disappointment or betrayal
- The struggle to trust comfort when it is offered

**Key insight:** The Child and the Ancient are not enemies to destroy. They are in conflict because they share the same wound and solve it differently. The Ancient defends by closing; the Child longs by opening. Neither can win. Only integration works — listen to both, let neither rule, negotiate a truce.

**The path:** The Child teaches the Ancient compassion. The Ancient teaches the Child discernment. Jung's transcendent function emerges when opposites collide and a new path is born.

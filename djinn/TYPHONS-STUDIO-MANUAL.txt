1. WHAT IS TYPHON'S STUDIO?
================================================================================

Typhon's Studio is a self-hosted alternative to StreamYard, Riverside, and
similar cloud services. Everything runs on a machine called Typhon
(192.168.1.113) inside your local network. You access it from any computer,
phone, or tablet on the same WiFi using a regular web browser — no app
downloads, no subscriptions.

WHAT IT CAN DO:
  - Capture your camera and microphone from any device on the LAN
  - Send that capture to Typhon, where OBS composites and encodes it
  - Live stream to Twitch, YouTube, or Instagram/Facebook
  - Record locally to a file on Typhon's hard drive (191 GB free)
  - Play DMCA-free background music during streams and recordings
  - Control smart lights (Cloudybay) from the browser
  - Run AI audio filters in real time (noise removal, compression)
  - Automatically transcribe recordings after the session
  - Generate show notes, chapters, and tags using AI
  - Cut clips from recordings for social media repurposing

WHAT IT CANNOT DO (yet):
  - Remote access from outside your LAN
  - Apple Music or Spotify integration (copyright rules)
  - Multiple simultaneous stream destinations
  - Edit video in the browser (export to your editor of choice)

This is your main view. The large area shows a preview of your camera.

---- PICKING YOUR CAMERA ---------------------------------------------------

In the right panel under "Camera":
  - Top dropdown: your primary camera (this is what goes into the stream)
  - Bottom dropdown: secondary camera (reserved for future multi-cam support)

Your browser will ask permission to access the camera and microphone the

---- PICKING YOUR MICROPHONE -----------------------------------------------

Under "Microphone":
  - Top dropdown: your primary microphone
  - Bottom dropdown: secondary microphone (future use)

Once selected, the camera preview appears and the audio meter lights up.

---- AUDIO METER -----------------------------------------------------------

The bar at the bottom of the preview shows your mic level in real time.
  - Green bars: good signal
  - Yellow/Orange bars: getting loud, watch it
  - Red bars with "CLIPPING" warning: too hot — move back from the mic
                                      or reduce gain in Audio tab

Aim to peak in the green-to-yellow range when speaking normally.

---- MONITOR / SPEAKER -----------------------------------------------------

Under "Monitor":
  - Select a speaker/headphone output to hear your own audio back
  - Toggle ON/OFF to enable or disable monitoring
  Note: Monitoring lets you hear yourself in the headphones.
        Leave it OFF if you don't have headphones — it will cause echo.

---- CONNECTING TO TYPHON (REQUIRED BEFORE GOING LIVE) --------------------

Once your camera and mic are selected, you MUST click:

  [ CONNECT TO TYPHON ]

This sends your camera and mic over WebRTC (a direct browser-to-server
connection) to Typhon's MediaMTX server. OBS on Typhon then reads this
stream and composites it.

When connected, the button turns green and shows "CONNECTED → OBS".
The header shows "STREAM CONNECTED".

You MUST connect before going live or recording. If you forget, the
pre-flight check will catch it.

---- SCENES ----------------------------------------------------------------

Under "OBS Scenes" in the right panel (or via the left sidebar shortcuts):

  Podcast       — audio-focused, single talking head
  Video Essay   — clean layout for voiceover-style content
  Interview     — split-screen layout (future multi-cam)
  Gaming        — game capture layout (future)
  Local Record  — local file recording, no stream overlay

Click a scene to switch OBS to that layout. The current scene is
highlighted in blue.

---- VCAM (VIRTUAL CAMERA) ------------------------------------------------

The VCAM button in the left sidebar creates a virtual camera device
at /dev/video10 called "TyphonStudio". This lets you use OBS's output
as a camera source in Zoom, Teams, Discord, or any other app on Typhon.

    3. Copy the server URL and stream key (paste together if needed)

  1. Select the platform card

  3. Paste your key
  4. Click SHOW to verify it looks right, then HIDE again
  5. Click SAVE

  The key is saved to Typhon's disk at:
    /home/tf-tthq/typhons-studio/config/stream_keys.json
  It is NOT stored in the browser and NOT sent to any external server.
  The file is readable only by Typhon's service account.

  Once saved, the platform card shows "KEY SET ✓" in green.

  To remove a key: click "× Remove saved key" under the input box.

---- PRE-FLIGHT CHECKLIST -------------------------------------------------

Click [ ✓ CHECK ] to run a pre-flight check before going live.
The right panel shows the results:

  ✓ OBS Studio           — OBS is running and connected
  ✓ Camera/Mic connected — your WHIP stream from the browser is active
  ✓ Stream key           — key is saved for the selected platform
  ✓ OBS scene selected   — a scene is loaded (not the default empty scene)
  ✓ Disk space           — at least 5 GB free on Typhon
  ✓ Copilot (Salomon AI) — Salomon AI server is reachable

IMPORTANT: Only OBS and Stream Key are hard blockers. If those two pass,
you can go live even if other checks have warnings.

The Camera/Mic check is a soft warning — it tells you to connect your
browser stream first, but you can technically start OBS streaming before
your browser reconnects (useful if you reload the page mid-session).

---- GOING LIVE ------------------------------------------------------------

After pre-flight passes, click the big red [ ▶ GO LIVE ] button.

What happens automatically:
  1. Pre-flight runs one more time
  2. OBS is configured with your platform's RTMP server and key
  3. OBS starts streaming (or recording for Local Record)
  4. Studio mode switches to LIVE (header turns red and pulses)
  5. Lights lock (can't accidentally change scenes while on air)
  6. Copilot enters live mode (analyzes every 60 seconds)

The header shows "● LIVE Twitch" (or whichever platform).

To end the broadcast: click [ ■ END BROADCAST ]
  - Stream stops
  - Mode returns to SETUP
  - Lights unlock
  - For Local Record: the file path appears with a "→ Post-Produce" button

You can also use GO LIVE from the left sidebar shortcut — it always uses
whatever platform is currently selected in the Stream tab.

7. AUDIO TAB — FILTERS AND CALIBRATION
================================================================================

Typhon applies a professional audio processing chain to your microphone
inside OBS. These run on Typhon's hardware, not your device.

---- THE FILTER CHAIN (in order) ------------------------------------------

  1. NOISE SUPPRESS (RNNoise AI)
     What it does: Removes background noise using an AI model.
     Removes: fan hum, air conditioning, keyboard clicks, room noise.
     Toggle: ON recommended at all times.

  2. NOISE GATE
     What it does: Silences the mic when you're not speaking.
     Opens at -32dB, closes at -42dB.
     Result: No low-level room noise bleeds in between sentences.
     Toggle: ON for podcasts and streams. Turn OFF if your voice is quiet
             and the gate is cutting off the start of your words.

  3. COMPRESSOR
     What it does: Reduces the difference between loud and quiet speech.
     Settings: 4:1 ratio, -18dB threshold.
     Result: Consistent, broadcast-quality vocal level.
     Toggle: ON always recommended.

  4. LIMITER
     What it does: Hard ceiling at -2dB. Prevents digital clipping.
     Toggle: ON always. Never turn this off while live.

To toggle any filter on or off, click the ON/OFF button on its card.
Changes take effect immediately in OBS.

---- AUTO GAIN STAGING (MIC CALIBRATION) ----------------------------------

If your mic sounds too quiet or too loud, use Auto Gain Staging:

  1. Click [ CALIBRATE MIC ]
  2. Speak normally into your mic for 5 seconds
     (talk as you would during a real session)
  3. Typhon samples your mic level and calculates the gain offset
     needed to hit -12dBFS (broadcast standard target)
  4. The gain is applied automatically
  5. A message shows the adjustment made (e.g., "Done — gain set +6.0 dB")

Do this once per session before going live. If you change your mic
position significantly, run it again.

8. MUSIC TAB — BACKGROUND MUSIC
================================================================================

Play DMCA-free background music during your session. All tracks are sourced
from Pixabay (royalty-free) or Bensound (Creative Commons).

---- SEARCHING FOR MUSIC ---------------------------------------------------

  - Type a keyword in the search bar (e.g., "chill beats", "lofi", "upbeat")
  - Or select a mood from the dropdown:
      Chill      — relaxed, low energy, background feel
      Energetic  — high tempo, hype music
      Focus      — concentration, ambient, study vibes
      Podcast    — clean background, doesn't compete with voice
      Dramatic   — cinematic, intense
      Gaming     — game stream background music

  Click [ SEARCH ] or press Enter.
  Results show: track title, source, duration, and DMCA-safe badge.

---- QUEUING TRACKS --------------------------------------------------------

  Click [ + QUEUE ] on any result to add it to your queue.
  The queue shows in the right panel. Current track is shown with "▶".
  Click [ ▶ PLAY NEXT ] to advance to the next track.
  Click [ clear ] to empty the queue.

---- VOLUME CONTROL --------------------------------------------------------

  The volume slider controls how loud the background music is.
  Drag left for quieter, right for louder.
  Range: 0% (mute) to 100% (full volume)
  Default starting volume: 40%

---- DUCK AND RESTORE ------------------------------------------------------

These are one-click shortcuts for talking over music:

  [ 🎙 DUCK ]    — instantly drops music to 20% while you speak
  [ 🎵 RESTORE ] — returns music to 40% when you're done talking

Use DUCK before speaking, RESTORE after. The Copilot will remind you
if music is too loud while your mic is active.

IMPORTANT: Background music plays on Typhon's audio system, not through
your device speakers. Viewers hear it mixed into the stream automatically.
You will NOT hear it unless you monitor the OBS output separately.

9. LIGHTING TAB — SCENE CONTROL
================================================================================

Control Cloudybay smart lights on your LAN from the browser.

NOTE: Lights must be discovered before they can be controlled.
      See "Discovering Lights" below.

---- LIGHTING SCENES -------------------------------------------------------

  🎙 Podcast    — 4000K warm white, full brightness
                  Good for: solo talking head, professional look
  🌆 Moody      — 2700K amber, 40% brightness
                  Good for: atmospheric content, evening streams
  💥 Dramatic   — 6500K cool white + blue accent, maximum brightness
                  Good for: gaming, hype content, reveals
  👥 Interview  — 5000K neutral daylight, 90% brightness
                  Good for: two-person format, balanced color
  ⚫ Off        — All lights off

Click a scene button to apply it immediately.

---- DISCOVERING LIGHTS ----------------------------------------------------

  1. Make sure your Cloudybay lights are:
     - Powered on
     - Connected to the same WiFi network as Typhon
     - Set up via the Cloudybay app on your phone first

  2. Click [ DISCOVER LIGHTS ]
  3. Typhon scans the network using the tinytuya protocol
  4. Found devices show as "X device(s) found"

  If no lights are found:
  - Check the Cloudybay app is connected and lights are online
  - Make sure lights are on the same network as Typhon (not guest WiFi)
  - Try toggling a light in the app to wake it up, then re-discover

---- LIGHTING LOCK ---------------------------------------------------------

When you go live (stream or record), lights automatically LOCK.
This prevents accidentally changing your lighting mid-session.

A yellow banner appears: "Lighting locked during live session"

Lights unlock automatically when you click END BROADCAST.
You can also manually unlock from the left sidebar if needed.

10. POST TAB — TRANSCRIPTION, SHOW NOTES, AND CLIPS
================================================================================

After a recording session, use the Post tab to automatically generate
transcripts, show notes, and social media clips using AI.

All processing runs on Typhon (GTX 1650 GPU) and Salomon (AI server).
This does NOT upload your content to any cloud service.

---- SELECTING A RECORDING -------------------------------------------------

  1. Click the Post tab
  2. Under "Recordings", all files in Typhon's recordings folder appear
     Each shows: filename, duration, file size
  3. Click a file to select it (highlighted in purple)

  If the list is empty:
  - You haven't recorded a session yet, OR
  - Click [ ↻ Refresh ] to rescan the folder

  Recordings are stored at:
    /home/tf-tthq/typhons-studio/recordings/

  Supported formats: .mkv, .mp4, .flv, .mov, .ts

  Quick shortcut: After ending a Local Record session, the Stream tab
  shows the saved file with a "→ Post-Produce" button that jumps directly
  to the Post tab with that file selected.

---- TRANSCRIBING A RECORDING ----------------------------------------------

  1. Select a recording
  2. Click [ 🎙 TRANSCRIBE ]

  What happens:
  - Typhon loads the Whisper AI speech recognition model (first load ~3-4s)
  - Audio is extracted from your recording
  - Whisper transcribes every word with precise timestamps
  - Progress appears via real-time updates

  Time estimates (approximate):
    10 min recording  → ~1-2 min to transcribe
    30 min recording  → ~3-5 min
    60 min recording  → ~8-12 min

  When done, the transcript appears in the main panel:
  - Each line shows a timestamp (00:01:23) and the spoken text
  - You can scroll through the full transcript
  - Click [ copy ] to copy the full text to your clipboard

  IMPORTANT: The first transcription ever will download the Whisper model
  (~150MB) from the internet. This takes a few minutes on first run only.
  All future transcriptions use the cached model instantly.

---- READING THE TRANSCRIPT ------------------------------------------------

  Each line in the transcript shows:
    00:01:23   And here's where we talk about the product launch...

  The gray timestamp on the left is clickable — clicking any transcript
  line adds a clip starting at that point to the Clip Editor on the right.
  (See "Extracting Clips" below.)

---- GENERATING SHOW NOTES -------------------------------------------------

  After transcription is complete, click [ 🤖 SHOW NOTES ]
  (This button stays grayed out until you have a transcript.)

  Typhon sends your transcript to Salomon's phi4:14b AI model, which
  reads the content and generates:

    Title        — A concise, engaging episode title (under 60 chars)
    Summary      — 2-3 sentence description of the episode
    Key Points   — 5-8 main takeaways from the session
    Chapters     — Natural topic shifts with timestamps
                   (e.g., "00:00:00 Introduction", "00:08:45 Main Topic")
    Description  — Full YouTube/podcast description (200-300 words)
    Tags         — 8-12 relevant hashtags/keywords

  Time estimate: 30-90 seconds depending on transcript length.

  Show notes appear in the right panel. Click [ copy JSON ] to copy the
  entire output as a JSON file you can paste into your editor or CMS.

  Tip: The chapters are especially useful for YouTube — paste them into
  your video description and YouTube creates a clickable chapter menu.

---- EXTRACTING CLIPS ------------------------------------------------------

  Clips let you cut specific moments from your recording for social media,
  trailers, or highlights without editing software.

  METHOD 1: Click timestamps in the transcript
    Click any line in the transcript.
    A 30-second clip is added to the Clip Editor starting at that moment.
    Adjust the end time in the Clip Editor if needed.

  METHOD 2: Manual entry
    Click [ + add ] in the Clip Editor header.
    Fill in: Label (what the clip is called), Start time, End time.
    Format: HH:MM:SS (e.g., 00:05:30 to 00:06:15)

  You can add as many clips as you want before extracting.
  To remove a clip: click the × button on that clip's card.

  When your clips are ready, click [ ✂️ EXTRACT CLIPS ]

  What happens:
  - ffmpeg runs on Typhon for each clip
  - Clips are encoded as H.264/AAC .mp4 files
  - Each clip saves to: /home/tf-tthq/typhons-studio/post/clips/

  Results appear below the clip editor:
    Green = success, shows the filename
    Red = error message (bad timestamps, etc.)

  Time estimate: about 30 seconds per clip of any length (fast encode).

---- OUTPUT FILES ----------------------------------------------------------

  All post-production output is saved on Typhon at:

    Transcripts:  /home/tf-tthq/typhons-studio/post/notes/{name}_transcript.json
    Show notes:   /home/tf-tthq/typhons-studio/post/notes/{name}_show_notes.json
    Clips:        /home/tf-tthq/typhons-studio/post/clips/{name}_{label}.mp4

  To access these files:
  - SSH into Typhon: ssh tf-tthq@192.168.1.113
  - Or use a file manager that supports SFTP/SSH (e.g., Cyberduck, WinSCP)
  - Recordings folder can also be shared via Samba if configured

11. COPILOT TAB — AI SESSION ASSISTANT
================================================================================

Copilot is an AI that watches your session and gives you real-time tips.
It runs automatically in the background — you don't have to do anything.

---- HOW IT WORKS ----------------------------------------------------------

  Every 30 seconds (in setup mode) or every 60 seconds (when live):
  - Copilot checks your mic level, music volume, lighting, stream status
  - It runs fast rule-based checks first (instant, no AI cost)
  - If nothing obvious is wrong, it sends context to Salomon's AI for deeper analysis
  - Suggestions appear in the Copilot tab and the tab badge updates

  The source badge shows where the suggestion came from:
    "rules"   — instant rule-based check, very fast
    "cache"   — same situation as a few minutes ago, reusing cached answer
    "salomon" — fresh AI analysis from phi4/qwen2.5 on Salomon

---- EXAMPLE SUGGESTIONS ---------------------------------------------------

  "⚠️ Mic clipping — reduce your input gain or move back from the mic"
  "🎵 Music too loud — duck it while speaking (tap Duck)"
  "🔴 Streaming but no camera/mic connected to Typhon — connect first"
  "💡 Lights are off during live session — was that intentional?"
  "🎵 Music queue empty — search for more tracks"
  "Everything looks good"

---- MANUAL ANALYSIS -------------------------------------------------------

  Click [ ANALYZE NOW ] to trigger an immediate analysis at any time.
  Useful if you changed something and want instant feedback.

---- SESSION STATE GRID ----------------------------------------------------

  At the bottom of the Copilot tab, a grid shows exactly what Copilot sees:
    Mode, Scene, Mic level, Music volume, Platform, Typhon link status

  This is useful for debugging if Copilot gives unexpected suggestions.

---- COPILOT BADGE ---------------------------------------------------------

  The Copilot tab shows a red number badge when there are active warnings.
  "Everything looks good" doesn't count — only real issues increment the badge.

13. TROUBLESHOOTING
================================================================================

---- "OBS OFFLINE" in header -----------------------------------------------

  CAUSE: Typhon's OBS service is not running, or the WebSocket connection
         between the Studio backend and OBS failed.

  FIX:
  1. SSH into Typhon: ssh tf-tthq@192.168.1.113
  2. Run: sudo systemctl restart obs-headless
  3. Wait 10 seconds, then refresh the Studio in your browser

  If that doesn't help:
  3. Run: sudo systemctl status obs-headless
  4. If it shows "failed", run: sudo systemctl restart xvfb obs-headless
  5. Check logs: sudo journalctl -u obs-headless -n 50

14. FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
================================================================================

Q: Can I use this from outside my house / over the internet?
A: Not currently. The studio is LAN-only for security and performance.
   Remote access via VPN (WireGuard or Tailscale) to your LAN would work
   but requires additional setup on your router.

Q: Can two people use the studio from different devices at the same time?
A: The UI can be open on multiple devices simultaneously — they all stay
   in sync via WebSocket. However, only ONE device can be the camera/mic
   source at a time. If two browsers try to CONNECT TO TYPHON, the second
   one will take over. Multi-guest support is a future feature.

Q: What happens to my stream key if I reinstall or reset Typhon?
A: Keys are stored at /home/tf-tthq/typhons-studio/config/stream_keys.json.
   Back up this file. If you lose it, just paste your keys in again from
   your streaming platform dashboards.

Q: Will the Whisper transcription work in languages other than English?
A: Yes. Whisper supports 99 languages. It auto-detects the language by
   default. For best results with non-English content, the AI show notes
   generation (phi4:14b) may be less accurate depending on the language.

Q: How long does it take to transcribe an hour-long recording?
A: Approximately 8-15 minutes on Typhon's GTX 1650 in CUDA mode.
   The first transcription of a session takes slightly longer (~15s extra)
   because the model needs to load from disk into GPU memory.

Q: What video format are the extracted clips in?
A: H.264 video + AAC audio, wrapped in an .mp4 container.
   Compatible with all major platforms (YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, etc.)
   and video editors (Premiere, DaVinci, Final Cut, etc.)

Q: Why can't I use Spotify or Apple Music?
A: Copyright restrictions. Streaming copyrighted music while broadcasting
   will trigger DMCA takedowns on Twitch/YouTube. Typhon's Studio uses
   Pixabay (royalty-free, no attribution required) and Bensound
   (Creative Commons) to keep your content safe.

Q: The Copilot tab shows "Copilot offline — Salomon unreachable". Help.
A: Salomon (192.168.1.225) is the AI server. Check that it's powered on.
   The studio still works fully without Copilot — it's an enhancement,
   not a requirement.

Q: Can I use OBS on my own computer instead of Typhon's?
A: No — Typhon's Studio is designed around Typhon's headless OBS instance.
   The browser's WebRTC stream goes specifically to Typhon's MediaMTX server.
   Your local OBS would not receive it.

Q: My audio sounds robotic or choppy after noise suppression.
A: RNNoise can be aggressive on certain microphones. Try:
   1. Audio tab → turn OFF "Noise Suppress"
   2. Move your mic closer to your mouth (reduces background noise naturally)
   3. Or try with Noise Gate OFF if your room is quiet

Q: Where are my recordings stored?
A: /home/tf-tthq/typhons-studio/recordings/ on Typhon's NVMe drive.
   191 GB available. Recordings are NOT automatically deleted.
   For very long sessions, check available space before recording.

Q: Can I change the OBS recording format (MKV vs MP4)?
A: Not from the UI currently. SSH into Typhon and edit OBS settings, or
   change the recording format in OBS via a custom WebSocket script.
   Default is MKV (crash-safe) — you can remux to MP4 with ffmpeg at any time:
   ffmpeg -i recording.mkv -c copy output.mp4

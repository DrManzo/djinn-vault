# Self-Awareness During Intoxication

## Summary
Maintaining self-awareness while drunk can be a positive sign, as it indicates the prefrontal cortex is still partially operational. However, this awareness does not eliminate alcohol's cognitive and motor impairments.

## Key Points
- **Metacognitive Awareness**: Preserved ability to monitor and recognize one’s own mental state.
- **Double-Whammy Effect**: Alcohol increases mind wandering and impairs the ability to notice when it occurs.
- **Protective Factors**: Mindful awareness can enhance behavioral regulation, preserve executive monitoring, and improve risk assessment.

## Details
**Enhanced Behavioral Regulation**: Being conscious of actions allows for better anticipation of consequences, reducing problematic drinking behaviors. College students with higher mindful awareness show lower rates of alcohol-related issues.

**Preserved Executive Monitoring**: The prefrontal cortex regions responsible for self-monitoring, impulse control, and decision-making retain some functionality despite alcohol's effects.

**Better Risk Assessment**: Awareness helps recognize the danger of impaired states like drunk driving, reducing risky behaviors. Alcohol disrupts the coupling between arousal states and prefrontal cortex activity, making it harder to track impairment accurately.

### Neural Mechanisms
- **Prefrontal Cortex**: Governs executive function, including self-monitoring and error detection.
- **Basal Ganglia & Dopamine Circuits**: Drive rewarding effects of alcohol, overriding prefrontal control as intoxication progresses.
- **Arousal and Attention Systems**: Alcohol disrupts the coupling between arousal states and prefrontal cortex activity.

### Important Limitations
- **Cognitive Impairments**: Working memory, inhibitory control, attention, and perceptual processing are still affected by alcohol even if self-awareness is maintained.

# Introduction to Calculus

## Summary
This MIT page provides an introduction to calculus, explaining its core concepts and applications. It emphasizes the study of change and how it can be used to model and control systems.

## Key Points
- **Core Idea**: Calculus studies instantaneous changes over tiny time intervals.
- **Why Study It**: Enables building quantitative models for various systems (physical, economic, biological) and deducing their consequences.
- **Course Structure**:
  - Describing position, speed, and acceleration using functions.
  - Finding derivatives and rules of differentiation.
  - Using derivatives to solve problems.
  - Reconstructing functions from derivatives through integration.
  - Techniques for integrating various classes of functions.
  - Applying integration to geometric problems like areas and volumes.

## Details
Calculus is described as the study of how things change, providing a framework to model systems that change. The fundamental idea is to study changes over tiny intervals because these are often simpler to model than long-term changes. Calculus allows you to build simple quantitative models for changing systems (physical, economic, biological) and deduce their consequences. This knowledge gives engineers "extraordinary power" over the material world.

A typical single-variable calculus course covers:
1. **Derivatives**: Finding instantaneous change and rules of differentiation.
2. **Applications of f′**: Optimizing functions and modeling behavior.
3. **Integrals**: Accumulated changes and computing areas/volumes.
4. **Integration Methods**: Techniques for integrating common forms.
5. **Series**: Infinite sums and convergence.

The introductory chapter revisits number systems, starting from natural numbers to complex numbers, and defines functions using operations like addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, substitution, and inversion.

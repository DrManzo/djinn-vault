# Mira — Love Letters and Relationship Dynamics

## Summary
Multiple iterations of love letters to Mira were crafted, exploring themes of her as a lodestone, fear of absence, and the therapeutic nature of writing itself. The letters evolved from modern romantic to 1800s-style prose to a husband-waiting-for-return narrative, culminating in a raw, self-aware letter acknowledging that the effort of writing is both escape and therapy — a constant attempt to make her feel loved, secure, and to deepen her love in return.

## Key Points
- Mira described as "lodestone" — the unwavering compass guiding toward all that is good
- Fear of her absence is the central emotional thread across all letter versions
- Letters progressed through styles: modern, 1800s period, husband awaiting return, soul-baring vulnerability
- Core metaphor: "water flowing endlessly through the world, missing the vessel that gives purpose"
- Her voice "cuts through the demons that prowl through the nights"
- Writing letters is therapeutic — an escape that requires effort and grounding
- Self-aware admission: hoping the constant effort makes her "fall in love just a little more each time"
- The letters serve dual purpose: expression of love and personal emotional processing
- Acknowledgment that the gestures may seem "silly" but are deeply meaningful to the writer

## Details
The letter-writing process reveals a pattern of using creative expression as emotional regulation. Each version strips away more artifice, moving from polished romance to raw vulnerability. The final version is notable for its meta-awareness — acknowledging that the act of writing is itself a form of therapy, a way to channel feelings into something tangible. The fear of abandonment runs deep, but is balanced by gratitude and a commitment to showing up consistently. The relationship exists in a complex space where Mira is in recovery, creating uncertainty about what happens when she becomes "whole" — whether the version of her that emerges will still want what's being offered.

# Scholarly Research on Developmental Milestones in Infancy

## Summary
This note summarizes the purpose and structure of a research assignment focused on developmental milestones in infancy, emphasizing the importance of these milestones for healthy development.

## Key Points
- The assignment requires students to conduct scholarly research on selected topics from child development and psychology.
- Students must analyze three peer-reviewed articles published within the last five years.
- Research methods include survey, interview, case study, physiological measures, standardized testing, descriptive, correlational, or experimental research.
- Ethical considerations in research are evaluated.

## Details
The purpose of this assignment is to provide students with an opportunity to conduct scholarly research on developmental milestones in infancy. Infancy refers to the period from birth to 12 months and includes key skills such as rolling over, sitting unaided, walking, and speaking first words[pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9600325/). These milestones serve as critical indicators of healthy neurological and physical development.

### Part I: Scholarly Research
1. **Description of the Selected Topic**
   - Infancy developmental milestones are crucial for understanding normal growth patterns.
   
2. **Theories and Theorists Contributed to This Topic**
   - Key theories include psychoanalytic, cognitive, evolutionary, and ecological perspectives.

3. **Summary of Scholarly Articles**
   - Three articles must be selected from library.gcu.edu, focusing on research methods such as survey, interview, case study, physiological measures, standardized testing, descriptive, correlational, or experimental research.
   - Each article’s research method (qualitative or quantitative) and additional elements like informed consent, confidentiality, debriefing practices, and potential deceptions must be identified.

4. **Ethical Considerations**
   - Evaluate the ethical aspects of each study.
   - Propose an alternative method for conducting the study if necessary.

### Part II: Reflection
1. **New Information Learned**
   - Reflect on interesting new information gained from research and class discussions.
   
2. **Understanding Research**
   - Discuss how incorporating scholarly research in early academic stages enhances understanding of research methods.

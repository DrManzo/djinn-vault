# Explain Why Researchers Who Are Operating in Theory-Testing Mode Might Not Try Using a Random Sample in Their Study

## Summary
Researchers operating in theory-testing mode often forego random sampling to prioritize internal validity, focusing on isolating specific variables under controlled conditions. This approach emphasizes methodological rigor and construct validity over broad population representation.

## Key Points
- **Internal Validity**: Researchers aim to establish causal relationships between variables.
- **Controlled Experimental Designs**: They use convenience samples or laboratory settings to control extraneous variables.
- **Construct Validity**: Ensures measurements accurately reflect the studied concepts.
- **Proverbs 25:2 (NIV)**: Emphasizes focused inquiry and foundational truths.

## Details
Researchers operating in theory-testing mode often do not use random sampling because their primary concern is internal validity, which refers to the degree to which a study can establish a causal relationship between variables. In this mode, researchers aim to test whether a specific theoretical mechanism works as predicted under controlled conditions, rather than whether the results generalize to a broader population (Morling, 2021). By using convenience samples or laboratory settings, they can control extraneous variables and focus on the precise relationship between the independent and dependent variables. This approach allows for greater control over the experimental environment, reducing the impact of confounding variables and increasing the likelihood that observed effects are due to the manipulation of interest rather than outside influences.

In emphasizing internal validity, theory-testing researchers prioritize methodological rigor and construct validity—the extent to which their measurements accurately capture the theoretical concepts under investigation (Morling, 2021; McLeod, 2019). They are less concerned, at least initially, with external validity, or how well the findings generalize to other populations and settings. As Proverbs 25:2 (NIV) states, "It is the glory of God to conceal a matter; to search out a matter is the glory of kings," highlighting the importance of focused inquiry in uncovering foundational truths. By concentrating on tightly controlled experiments and precise measurement, researchers can build a robust theoretical foundation before later studies examine the generalizability of their findings to the real world.

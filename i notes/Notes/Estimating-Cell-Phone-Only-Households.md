# Estimating Cell-Phone-Only Households

## Summary
To accurately estimate the number of cell-phone-only households, organizations like Pew Research Center use a combination of innovative sampling techniques and reliable data sources. This involves integrating large-scale survey data from the National Health Interview Survey (NHIS), dual-frame random digit dialing (RDD) that targets both landline and mobile numbers, and address-based sampling (ABS) to reach households regardless of their phone type.

## Key Points
- **National Health Interview Survey (NHIS)**: Provides comprehensive data on household communication trends.
- **Dual-Frame Random Digit Dialing (RDD)**: Combines landline and cell phone numbers for more inclusive coverage, with manual dialing required for mobile numbers due to federal regulations.
- **Address-Based Sampling (ABS)**: Mails surveys directly to randomly selected addresses to ensure broader coverage.

## Details
To estimate the prevalence of cell-phone-only households, organizations like Pew Research Center employ a multifaceted methodology that integrates large-scale survey data from the National Health Interview Survey (NHIS), dual-frame random digit dialing (RDD) strategies, and address-based sampling (ABS). The NHIS serves as a foundational data source, providing nationally representative estimates of household communication patterns through in-person interviews. However, to address the limitations of relying solely on government surveys, Pew complements this with RDD strategies that simultaneously target landline and mobile phone frames.

This dual-frame approach ensures coverage of both traditional and mobile-only populations, though cell phone surveys face unique challenges: federal regulations prohibit auto-dialing for mobile numbers, necessitating manual dialing by researchers. Lower response rates often require offering prepaid incentives to participants. These measures help mitigate selection biases that could undercount demographics disproportionately likely to rely on cell phones, such as younger adults, renters, and lower-income households.

To further enhance accuracy, Pew integrates address-based sampling (ABS) into its methodology, using U.S. Postal Service databases to mail surveys directly to randomly selected households irrespective of their phone type or usage patterns. This hybrid design bypasses the growing problem of non-coverage in phone-only surveys, as 15-20% of U.S. households now lack functional landlines according to NHIS trend analyses.

By statistically weighting responses from these overlapping sampling frames and benchmarking against federal survey data, Pew achieves demographic representativeness across age, income, and regional lines. For instance, their 2024 methodology report demonstrated a <2% margin of error in estimating cell-only prevalence compared to NHIS gold-standard data, validating the approach’s rigor.

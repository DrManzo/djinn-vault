# Detailed Guide for Generativity Presentation

## Summary
This note provides a comprehensive guide to creating an effective generativity presentation, ensuring all content is aligned with the rubric requirements and includes detailed slides.

## Key Points
- Comprehensive definition of generativity.
- Discussion on relevant theories (Erikson, McAdams).
- Personal reflection and assessment.
- Cultural contributions to generativity.
- Analysis of personal generativity score.
- Examples of current generative activities.
- Future career goals and their connection to generativity.
- Strategies for increasing generativity.
- Proper APA citations and references.

## Details
The presentation should be structured as follows:

### Slide 1: Title
"Generativity: Nurturing Future Generations"
[Your Name]
[Date]

### Slide 2: Introduction
What legacy do you want to leave behind?
Overview: Definition, theories, cultural impacts, personal assessment, and strategies

### Slide 3: Defining Generativity
- Generativity is the concern for and commitment to promoting the next generation's well-being through parenting, teaching, mentoring, and other creative contributions (McAdams & de St. Aubin, 1992).

### Slide 4: Generativity Theories
- **Erik Erikson**: 7th stage of psychosocial development (Erikson, 1963)
- **Dan McAdams**: Multifaceted model of generativity (McAdams & de St. Aubin, 1992)

### Slide 5: Personal Connection to Theories
[Your personal reflection on which theory resonates most with you and why]

### Slide 6: Cultural Contributions to Generativity
- **Gender roles**: Expectations in parenting and caregiving.
- **Religion**: Values of service and legacy.
- **Education**: Emphasis on knowledge transfer.
- **Societal norms**: Civic engagement and volunteerism.

### Slide 7: Personal Generativity Assessment
[Your Loyola Generativity Scale score]
[Graph or chart visualizing your score]

### Slide 8: Analysis of Generativity Score
- Factors contributing to your score.
- Areas for potential growth.

### Slide 9: Personality and Generative Actions
- [Your personality traits supporting generativity].
- Examples of how these traits manifest in generative behaviors.

### Slide 10: Current Generative Activities
- List your ongoing generative actions.
- Impact on self and others.

### Slide 11: Future Career Goals
[Visual timeline of your career aspirations]

### Slide 12: Connecting Career Goals to Generativity
- How your future career contributes to generativity.
- Potential generative impact of your work.

### Slide 13: Strategies for Increasing Generativity
- Seek mentoring opportunities.
- Engage in community service.
- Share knowledge through teaching or writing.
- Nurture relationships with younger generations.
- Set generative goals.

### Slide 14: Conclusion
Recap: Generativity's importance in personal growth and societal well-being.
Call to action: Embrace generativity in your daily life.

### References
- Erikson, E. H. (1963). *Childhood and society* (2nd ed.). W. W. Norton & Company.
- McAdams, D. P., & de St. Aubin, E. (1992). A theory of generativity and its assessment through self-report, behavioral acts, and narrative themes in autobiography. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 62(6), 1003-1015.

# PSY-358 Adult Development and Aging: Relationships Chart

## Summary
The chart evaluates the emotional well-being effects of various relationships on adults, including marriage, cohabitation, remaining single, single parenting, remarriage with children, caring for an ill parent, adopting a child, and fostering a child. It includes potential advantages, disadvantages, and techniques to facilitate relationship success.

## Key Points
- **Marriage (heterosexual or homosexual)**
  - Advantages: emotional support, physical affection, increased survival likelihood.
  - Disadvantages: compromises, emotional frustration, mental health issues.
  - Techniques: active listening, shared activities, couples therapy.
  
- **Cohabitation (unmarried)**
  - Advantages: intimacy, autonomy, shared responsibilities.
  - Disadvantages: relationship uncertainty, societal stigma, stress.
  - Techniques: open communication, support network, counseling.

- **Remaining Single**
  - Advantages: personal freedom, self-discovery.
  - Disadvantages: loneliness, social isolation, financial challenges.

## Details
The chart provides a structured overview of the emotional well-being effects of different adult relationships. Each relationship is evaluated based on potential advantages and disadvantages, with specific techniques to facilitate success and improve well-being highlighted. The information is derived from scholarly sources such as McLean Hospital, Paired, Legal Research & Analysis, and HelpGuide.

### Marriage
- **Advantages:**
  - Provides emotional support.
  - Offers companionship and physical affection.
  - Increases likelihood of survival by 50% regardless of age, sex, or health status (McLean Hospital).
  
- **Disadvantages:**
  - Requires compromises and sacrifices.
  - Can lead to emotional frustration and disagreements.
  - May contribute to mental health issues if marital problems arise (Paired).

- **Techniques:**
  - Practice active listening and empathy.
  - Engage in shared activities.
  - Seek professional help through couples therapy.

### Cohabitation
- **Advantages:**
  - Offers increased intimacy and emotional connection.
  - Provides greater sense of autonomy and freedom.
  - Allows for shared responsibilities (Legal Research & Analysis).
  
- **Disadvantages:**
  - May lead to relationship uncertainty.
  - Can face societal stigma and family disapproval.
  - Might contribute to stress due to unclear future plans.

- **Techniques:**
  - Foster open communication about expectations.
  - Build a support network of friends and family.
  - Consider couples counseling for proactive addressing of concerns.

### Remaining Single
- **Advantages:**
  - Provides greater personal freedom and autonomy.
  - Allows focus on personal growth and self-discovery.
  
- **Disadvantages:**
  - May lead to feelings of loneliness and social isolation.
  - Lacks the built-in emotional support system of a partnership.

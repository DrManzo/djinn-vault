# How Do Our Cognitive Processes Change with Age?

## Summary
The discussion revolves around the cognitive changes in older adults as compared to younger individuals, focusing on tasks that measure both speed and accuracy. Roger Ratcliff's model suggests that aging does not uniformly slow all cognitive processes but rather affects different skills differently.

## Key Points
- Older people are more deliberate and accurate than younger ones initially.
- Younger participants tend to be faster but less accurate.
- Training can help older adults match the speed of young individuals while maintaining accuracy.

## Details
Roger Ratcliff's model, developed for predicting cognitive changes in aging, challenges the earlier view that all cognitive processes slow uniformly. The study involves tasks where subjects discriminate between displays with many and few asterisks on a screen. 

- **Initial Observations:**
  - **Joan (79 years old):** Deliberate and accurate; prefers to be certain before making decisions.
  - **Jess (23 years old):** Faster but often too quick, leading to frequent "too-fast" messages.

- **Task Description:** 
  - Subjects press a key with circles if they think there are many asterisks or another key for few asterisks. The computer provides feedback on correctness and speed.
  
- **Findings:**
  - Younger participants generally perform faster but may make more errors due to haste.
  - Older adults start off slower but can be trained to match the speed of younger individuals while maintaining accuracy.

- **Implications:**
  - Optimism about cognitive skills in older adults is increasing, which could benefit clinical treatments for various cognitive issues.

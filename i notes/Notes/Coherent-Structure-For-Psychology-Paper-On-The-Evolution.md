## Details
The concept of divine presence has evolved significantly across various mythologies and religious traditions, reflecting broader shifts in human society and thought. From the tangible, anthropomorphic deities of ancient Mesopotamian civilizations to more abstract concepts in modern Judeo-Christian traditions, this evolution mirrors our quest for meaning and connection with the divine.

### Introduction
The concept of divine presence has undergone a profound transformation from ancient Mesopotamian civilizations to modern times. These changes reflect shifts in perspective, evolving societal values, and philosophical understandings of the human condition. This thesis aims to examine the progression of divine presence across various mythologies, including Mesopotamian, Egyptian, Greco-Roman, and Norse traditions, culminating in today's Judeo-Christian beliefs.

### Mesopotamian Pantheon
The early mythologies feature father figure archetypes that parallel later divine concepts like Zeus, Jupiter, and the Judeo-Christian God. These deities embody primal instincts, with Zeus serving as a prime example. The evolution of divine representation is further illustrated through the transformation of Gilgamesh into heroic archetypes in Greco-Roman traditions.

### Heroic Archetypes
Comparisons between Gilgamesh and Hercules reveal how these semi-divine heroes paved the way for later concepts of divine incarnation. Edith Hamilton notes that "The power of good is shown not by triumphantly conquering evil, but by continuing to resist evil while facing certain defeat," a theme in these heroic narratives.

### Divine Father Figure Evolution
Tracing the development from Zeus to the Judeo-Christian God highlights how these concepts coexisted and evolved. This evolution reflects Brother Lawrence's insight that "We ought not to be weary of doing little things for the love of God, who regards not the greatness of the work, but the love with which it is performed," emphasizing a shift towards a more personal divine relationship.

### Norse Mythology
Norse mythology’s portrayal of Thor and Odin serves as a bridge to understanding the modern Christian concept of God as supreme, with Christ serving as an extension of divine will. This comprehensive approach illuminates how ancient cultures grappled with the concept of divinity and its relationship to humanity, echoing the Bhagavad Gita's teaching: "He who perceives Me everywhere and beholds everything in Me never loses sight of Me, nor do I ever lose sight of him."

### Greek and Roman Myths
Greek and Roman mythologies present a shift towards more humanized deities who interact with mortals but maintain a distinct separation. Zeus frequently intervened in human affairs and coupled with mortals, yet remained primarily based on Mount Olympus. This concept is also present in Norse mythology, though with more constraints.

### Judeo-Christian Tradition
The Judeo-Christian tradition represents a further evolution in the concept of divine presence. Early biblical traditions confined God's presence to specific locations like the Tabernacle or manifested through phenomena like the Ark and the pillar of cloud. Later traditions emphasized a more abstract concept of divine presence, with God dwelling in heaven rather than physically on earth.

### Conclusion
The progression from constant physical presence to abstract manifestation and sacrifice represents a significant departure from earlier mythological traditions. This evolution reflects our ongoing struggle to understand our place in the cosmos and our quest for meaning through divine connection.

# The Wounded Healer

## Summary
The narrative follows Faust, a necromancer who initially relies on Nurul's coin to animate the dead but eventually learns that love and remembrance are more powerful than any magic. Through his journey, he discovers that the dead never truly leave us—they become part of our lives in ways we cannot fully comprehend.

## Key Points
- Faust's initial reliance on necromancy as a crutch.
- The transformation from using magical power to understanding love and remembrance.
- The realization that the dead are woven into the fabric of life.

## Details
Faust, born in the shadows of the caverns of deep gnomes, was a child often overlooked. His great-great-great-great-grandmother, an ancient matron, saw him and shared stories of gods and monsters. When she died, Faust accepted a coin from Nurul, the God of Necromancers, which allowed him to call back the dead but at a heavy cost.

Initially, Faust used the coin to avoid facing his grief, particularly that of his grandmother. However, as he encountered dying children, grieving families, and ultimately his own understanding, he realized that true healing comes from helping others recognize their loved ones' enduring presence in life. The narrative moves through decades, showing how Faust evolves from desperate attempts to cheat death to a profound acceptance that love transcends individual form.

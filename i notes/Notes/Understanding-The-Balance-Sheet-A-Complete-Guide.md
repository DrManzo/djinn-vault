# Understanding the Balance Sheet: A Complete Guide

## Summary
This note provides a comprehensive guide to understanding balance sheets, including key terms and concepts.

## Key Points
- **Balance Sheet**: Financial statement showing a company's assets, liabilities, and shareholders' equity at a specific point in time.
- **Fundamental Accounting Equation**: Assets = Liabilities + Shareholders' Equity.
- **Current Assets**: Cash, accounts receivable, inventory, prepaid expenses, marketable securities.
- **Non-Current Assets**: Property, plant & equipment (PP&E), intangible assets, goodwill, long-term investments, deferred tax assets.
- **Current Liabilities**: Accounts payable, short-term loans, accrued expenses.
- **Non-Current Liabilities**: Long-term debt, pension obligations.

## Details
A balance sheet is a financial statement that provides a snapshot of a company's financial position at a specific moment in time. It consists of three main components: assets, liabilities, and shareholders' equity. The fundamental accounting equation, which must always be balanced, states that the total value of what a company owns (assets) equals the total of what it owes (liabilities) plus what belongs to the owners (shareholders' equity).

### 1. Assets
- **Current Assets**: These are resources expected to be converted into cash or used within one year.
  - Cash and cash equivalents: Physical currency, highly liquid investments with a maturity of 90 days or less.
  - Accounts receivable: Money owed by customers for goods or services delivered on credit.
  - Inventory: Raw materials, work-in-progress goods, and finished products ready for sale.
  - Prepaid expenses: Money paid in advance for future services (e.g., prepaid insurance).
  - Marketable securities: Short-term investments that can be quickly converted to cash.

- **Non-Current Assets**: These are long-term assets not expected to be converted into cash within one year.
  - Property, Plant & Equipment (PP&E): Tangible physical assets like land, buildings, machinery, and vehicles.
  - Intangible assets: Non-physical assets with economic value, such as patents, copyrights, trademarks, and brand recognition.
  - Goodwill: An intangible asset arising from the acquisition of another company for more than its net asset value.
  - Long-term investments: Investments in other companies or securities with multi-year horizons.
  - Deferred tax assets: Tax benefits expected to be realized in future years.

### 2. Liabilities
- **Current Liabilities**: Obligations due within one year of the balance sheet date.
  - Accounts payable: Money owed to suppliers or vendors.
  - Short-term loans: Loans with terms less than a year.
  - Accrued expenses: Expenses incurred but not yet paid (e.g., accrued salaries, utilities).

- **Non-Current Liabilities**: Obligations due after one year of the balance sheet date.
  - Long-term debt: Debt obligations with maturities greater than one year.
  - Pension obligations: Future payments to employees under a pension plan.

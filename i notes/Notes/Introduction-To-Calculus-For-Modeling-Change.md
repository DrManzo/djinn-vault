# Introduction to Calculus for Modeling Change

## Summary
This MIT page introduces calculus as the study of change, emphasizing its importance in modeling and controlling systems across various fields. It outlines key concepts like derivatives, integrals, and series, providing a structured overview of what is covered in a typical single-variable calculus course.

## Key Points
- Calculus studies how things change.
- The fundamental idea is to model instantaneous changes over tiny intervals.
- It enables building quantitative models for physical, economic, and biological systems.
- Core topics include derivatives, applications of derivatives, integration, integration methods, and series.
- Functions are defined as sets of argument-value pairs.

## Details
Calculus is essential in understanding how changing conditions affect a system. By studying these changes, one can predict outcomes and control the system to behave in desired ways. This power over the material world has driven significant advancements in modern science and engineering. A typical single-variable calculus course covers:

1. **Derivatives**: Finding instantaneous change (the derivative) and rules for differentiation.
2. **Applications of Derivatives**: Optimizing functions and modeling systems.
3. **Integration**: Using derivatives to solve problems, reconstructing a function from its derivative (integration).
4. **Integration Methods**: Techniques for integrating various classes of functions.
5. **Geometric Applications**: Using integration for areas and volumes.

Additional topics include power series representations of functions and the study of infinite series convergence.

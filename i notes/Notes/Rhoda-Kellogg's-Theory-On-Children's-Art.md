# Rhoda Kellogg's Theory on Children's Art

## Summary
Rhoda Kellogg, an American early childhood educator and psychologist, conducted extensive research on the development of children’s artistic expression. Her work identified five stages in children’s art: scribbles, placement, shape, design, and pictorial.

## Key Points
- Rhoda Kellogg (1898–1987) was an advocate for understanding and supporting children's artistic development.
- She proposed that children’s drawings are orderly, meaningful, and structured, reflecting cognitive and emotional growth.
- Kellogg created the Kellogg Classification System to identify stages of artistic development.

## Details
Rhoda Kellogg devoted her career to studying and understanding children’s artistic expression. Her research led to the creation of the Kellogg Classification System, which includes:
1. **Scribbles**: Random, uncontrolled marks indicating exploration.
2. **Placement**: Lines and marks placed with intent, showing spatial awareness.
3. **Shape**: Basic geometric shapes appearing, such as circles and squares.
4. **Design**: Shapes combined to create more complex patterns.
5. **Pictorial**: Drawings representing recognizable objects or scenes.

Kellogg’s work is significant because it provided educators and psychologists with insights into children's cognitive and emotional development through their artwork. Her theories have influenced modern educational practices by encouraging the value of artistic expression in early childhood education.

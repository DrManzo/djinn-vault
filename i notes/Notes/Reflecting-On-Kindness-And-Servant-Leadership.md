### Reflection on Kindness and Servant Leadership

In the context of servant leadership, the act of kindness towards others can be seen as a fundamental principle. According to *Servant Leadership: A Journey Into the Nature of Legitimate Power and Greatness* by Robert K. Greenleaf (1970), servant leaders prioritize the needs and well-being of their followers over their own interests. This aligns closely with prosocial behavior, which involves actions that benefit others without expecting direct personal gain.

Greenleaf defines servant leadership as a "servant first" approach where leaders serve their people rather than manipulate them or control them (Greenleaf, 1970). This perspective emphasizes empathy and understanding, qualities that are essential in fostering an environment of mutual respect and support. In the example provided, the narrator's act of kindness towards a classmate with autism not only helped the instructor but also reinforced their reputation as a helpful individual.

The quote from Cicero: "No duty is more urgent than that of returning thanks," underscores the importance of acknowledging and appreciating such acts of kindness (Cicero, n.d.). This aligns well with servant leadership principles, which advocate for recognizing and valuing the contributions of others. By integrating gratitude into their interactions, leaders can create a culture where prosocial behavior is not only encouraged but also celebrated.

In conclusion, the act of showing kindness towards someone outside one's family, as exemplified in the narrator’s experience, aligns closely with servant leadership principles. The unexpected rewards from such acts highlight the broader impact and value of prosocial behavior in both personal and professional contexts.

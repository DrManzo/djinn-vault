# Core Subsections of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Addiction

## Summary
This note outlines the key subsections and treatment models within cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) specifically designed for substance use disorders, including Relapse Prevention, Mindfulness-Based Relapse Prevention, Community Reinforcement Approach, Behavioral Couples Therapy, Guided Self-Change, Personality-Targeted Brief Interventions, Contingency Management, and Motivational Interviewing.

## Key Points
- **Relapse Prevention (RP)**
  - Developed by Marlatt & Gordon (1985)
  - Focuses on functional analysis of triggers, coping skills training, drug-refusal skills, and increasing non-substance activities.
  - Typically 12–20 sessions; Project MATCH adapted it to 14 sessions.

- **Mindfulness-Based Relapse Prevention (MBRP)**
  - Integrates mindfulness meditation into the RP framework.
  - Session structure includes automatic pilot awareness, trigger exposure & mindful responding, SOBER method, coping with high-risk situations, acceptance/breathing spaces, cognitive defusion, self-care & lifestyle balance, and review & maintenance planning.

- **Community Reinforcement Approach (CRA)**
  - Multimodal intervention targeting environmental reinforcers.
  - Modules include functional analysis, treatment planning, sobriety sampling, behavioral skills training, job skills, social/recreational counseling, and relationship counseling.

- **Behavioral Couples Therapy (BCT)**
  - Targets the bidirectional relationship between relationship functioning and substance use.
  - Components include psychoeducation, contingency management within the relationship, scheduling mutually pleasurable non-drug activities, communication skills training.

- **Guided Self-Change (GSC) / Brief Interventions**
  - Motivationally-based, very brief treatment helping clients formulate and pursue a self-defined change goal.

- **Personality-Targeted Brief Interventions**
  - Personalized CBT addressing specific personality risk factors like impulsivity, hopelessness, anxiety sensitivity, and sensation seeking.

- **Contingency Management (CM)**
  - Voucher/prize reinforcement for verified abstinence.
  - Variants include standard CM, prize-based "fishbowl" CM, and job-based reinforcement alternatives.

- **Motivational Interviewing (MI) / Motivational Enhancement**
  - Often used as a pretreatment or integrated component to resolve ambivalence and enhance treatment engagement.

## Details
CBT for substance use disorders encompasses multiple models that address different aspects of addiction. Relapse Prevention is the most established model, focusing on identifying high-risk situations and developing coping skills. Mindfulness-Based Relapse Prevention extends this by integrating mindfulness techniques. The Community Reinforcement Approach emphasizes environmental reinforcement and behavioral skills training within a relationship context. Behavioral Couples Therapy specifically targets the interplay between relationships and substance use. Guided Self-Change provides brief interventions to help clients set personal change goals, while Personality-Targeted Brief Interventions address specific personality risk factors contributing to addiction. Contingency Management uses rewards for verified abstinence, and Motivational Interviewing helps resolve ambivalence towards treatment.

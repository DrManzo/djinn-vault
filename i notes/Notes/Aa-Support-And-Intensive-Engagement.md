# AA Support and Intensive Engagement

## Summary
The note discusses the initial stages of attending Alcoholics Anonymous (AA) meetings, engaging with a sponsor, and working through the 12 steps. It emphasizes the importance of structure, support, and commitment to recovery.

## Key Points
- Javier has chosen to attend AA for structured peer-based support.
- The disease of addiction is described as chronic and relapsing, emphasizing the need for treatment rather than control.
- Intensive engagement with meetings (3 per day), a sponsor, and the 12 steps supports higher odds of early abstinence and long-term benefit.

## Details
Javier has made the decision to attend Alcoholics Anonymous (AA) as part of his journey towards recovery from alcohol addiction. This choice is significant because it places him in a structured environment designed to help manage the disease, reduce relapse rates, and improve overall well-being through social support and shared narratives.

### Structure and Support
- **Chronic Disease Model**: Addiction is described as a chronic, relapsing brain disorder where voluntary use shifts into compulsive use with loss of control despite harm. The focus should be on treatment and management rather than strict control.
- **Social Support**: AA provides structure through regular meetings, which help reduce isolation by putting individuals in recurring contact with others facing similar challenges. This social support is crucial for reducing relapse drivers such as shame and loneliness.

### Intensive Engagement
- **High Meeting Attendance**: Javier attends an average of three AA meetings per day, which aligns closely with the Twelve-Step Facilitation model used in formal treatment trials.
- **Sponsorship**: He has a temporary sponsor who is guiding him through the 12 steps and encouraging reading of the Big Book. This sponsorship adds another layer of support to his recovery process.

### Initial Steps
- **First Step Understanding**: Javier is working on understanding and handwriting the first step, which involves acknowledging powerlessness over alcohol and turning will and life over to a higher power.
- **Emotional Weight**: The emotional weight of these initial steps can be heavy, but it is essential for building a foundation in recovery.

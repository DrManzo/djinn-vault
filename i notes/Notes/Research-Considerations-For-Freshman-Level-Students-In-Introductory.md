# Research Considerations for Freshman-Level Students in Introductory Psychology

## Summary
This note outlines key factors Emilio and his research supervisor need to consider when conducting surveys or questionnaires with freshman-level students in an introductory psychology class.

## Key Points
- **Ethical Considerations**
  - Informed Consent: Ensure full transparency about the purpose, risks, and withdrawal options.
  - Confidentiality: Protect participant data and maintain privacy.
  - Voluntary Participation: Avoid coercion to ensure genuine consent.

- **Diversity and Inclusion**
  - Respect for Diversity: Design culturally sensitive questions accessible to all students.
  - Avoiding Bias: Ensure unbiased language and avoid assumptions about participants' backgrounds.

- **Methodological Considerations**
  - Sampling: Use a convenience sample, acknowledging its limitations.
  - Survey Design: Keep questions clear and appropriate for freshmen's developmental level.
  - Validity and Reliability: Pilot-test the survey to ensure accurate results.

- **Institutional Approval**
  - IRB Approval: Seek approval from the university’s Institutional Review Board (IRB).

## Details
When Emilio and his research supervisor plan to conduct surveys or questionnaires with freshman-level students in an introductory psychology class, several key factors must be considered:

### Ethical Considerations
1. **Informed Consent**
   - All participants must understand the purpose of the research.
   - Participants should know what participation involves, any potential risks, and their right to withdraw at any time without penalty.

2. **Confidentiality**
   - Responses must be kept confidential.
   - Data storage must be secure, with identifiable information protected or anonymized.

3. **Voluntary Participation**
   - Participation should be entirely voluntary; students should not feel coerced or pressured.

### Diversity and Inclusion
1. **Respect for Diversity**
   - The sample will include students from diverse backgrounds.
   - Questions should avoid biased language and be accessible to all, including those with disabilities.

2. **Avoiding Bias**
   - Both the design of the questionnaire and the interpretation of results must be free from assumptions or stereotypes about participants' backgrounds.

### Methodological Considerations
1. **Sampling**
   - Since the research targets all students in an introductory psychology class, a convenience sample is being used.
   - Acknowledge this limitation to ensure generalizability is understood.

2. **Survey Design**
   - Questions should be clear and appropriate for freshmen's developmental level.
   - Avoid jargon or complex language that could confuse participants.

3. **Validity and Reliability**
   - The survey should be pilot-tested to ensure accurate measurement and consistent results.

### Institutional Approval
1. **IRB Approval**
   - Before distributing surveys, Emilio and his supervisor must seek approval from the university’s Institutional Review Board (IRB) or ethics committee.

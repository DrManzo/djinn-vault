# California LLC Formation Guide for 3D Printing & Fabrication Business in Rialto, CA

## Summary
This guide provides essential steps and considerations for forming an LLC (Limited Liability Company) specifically tailored to a 3D printing and fabrication business operating in Rialto, California.

## Key Points
- **Legal Structure**: Forming an LLC offers personal asset protection.
- **Business Name**: Ensure the name is unique and complies with state regulations.
- **Articles of Organization**: File these documents with the California Secretary of State.
- **Registered Agent**: Appoint a registered agent to receive legal correspondence.
- **Operating Agreement**: Draft this document to outline business operations, roles, and responsibilities.

## Details
### Legal Structure
Forming an LLC in California provides personal asset protection for the owners (members). This means that if your business incurs debts or lawsuits, your personal assets are generally protected from such liabilities.

### Business Name
Choose a unique name for your 3D printing and fabrication business. Ensure it complies with state regulations by avoiding prohibited words like "bank" or "insurance." You can check the availability of your desired name through the California Secretary of State's website.

### Articles of Organization
The articles of organization are the foundational documents that officially establish your LLC in California. These must be filed with the California Secretary of State along with a filing fee. The articles should include details such as the business name, address, and registered agent information.

### Registered Agent
Appointing a registered agent is crucial for receiving legal correspondence on behalf of your LLC. This can be an individual or a professional service. The registered agent must have a physical office address in California where they can receive important documents like lawsuits, subpoenas, and other official communications.

### Operating Agreement
Drafting an operating agreement is essential to outline the business operations, roles, responsibilities, and financial arrangements among members. This document helps prevent disputes by setting clear expectations from the outset.

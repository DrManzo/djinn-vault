## Details
To expand on the concept of "Societal Structure: Class Distinctions" and incorporate detailed descriptions of daily life for different social classes, popular entertainment, and unique customs, you can draw inspiration from the provided sources. Here are some ideas and recommendations:

### Class Distinctions

1. **Upper Class**:
   - Emphasize their wealth, power, and influence.
   - Describe luxurious lifestyles, including high-end education, exclusive social clubs, and political influence.

2. **Middle Class**:
   - Highlight their struggle to maintain a decent lifestyle.
   - Focus on education and white-collar jobs.
   - Discuss their aspirations and the challenges they face in achieving financial stability.

3. **Working Class**:
   - Detail daily struggles, including long hours in blue-collar jobs.
   - Limited access to education and financial insecurity.
   - Show how they are often at risk of unemployment and have limited social mobility.

4. **Lower Class**:
   - Illustrate the harsh realities of poverty.
   - Lack of education, low-paying jobs, and reliance on welfare systems.
   - Discuss the underclass and challenges in accessing basic necessities like healthcare and housing.

5. **Class Culture**:
   - **Daily Life**: Describe how different classes spend their days.
     - Upper class: Leisure activities, exclusive events.
     - Working class: Grueling work schedules, community gatherings.
   - **Entertainment**: How different classes enjoy entertainment.
     - Upper class: Exclusive events, high-end sports.
     - Working class: Local parks, affordable sports.
   - **Customs**: Unique customs and traditions within each class.
     - Upper class: Formal dining etiquette.
     - Working class: Community barbecues.

### Recommendations for Incorporation

1. **Character Development**:
   - Create characters from different social classes.
   - Explore their daily lives, struggles, and aspirations.
   
2. Dialogue and Interactions:
   - Use dialogue to highlight cultural and social differences between classes.
   
3. Plot Points:
   - Use class conflicts as plot points.
     - Example: A character from the working class struggling to access healthcare.
   
4. Relationships and Opportunities:
   - Explore how class affects relationships and opportunities.
     - Middle-class character facing challenges in forming friendships with upper class.

5. **World-Building**:
   - Develop a detailed societal structure that includes clear class divisions.
   - Describe physical environments (e.g., luxurious neighborhoods, cramped living conditions).

6. **Themes**:
   - Explore themes of inequality, social mobility, and class consciousness.
   - Discuss how class affects individuals' perceptions of themselves and their place in society.

7. Struggles and Resilience:
   - Highlight the struggles and resilience of characters from lower classes.
   - Emphasize humanity and empathy.

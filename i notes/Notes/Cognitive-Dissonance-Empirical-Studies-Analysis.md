# Cognitive Dissonance Empirical Studies Analysis

## Summary
This note provides a structured response to an academic discussion question on cognitive dissonance, including two empirical studies and their measurement scales.

## Key Points
- **Concept Selection:** Cognitive dissonance is selected due to its relevance in psychology, decision-making, and behavioral economics.
- **Empirical Studies:** Two studies are analyzed for their variable measurement scales: Festinger & Carlsmith (1959) and Bran & Vaidis (2020).

## Details
### Part 1: Why Select This Concept
Cognitive dissonance is a fascinating concept because it explains the fundamental human drive to maintain psychological consistency. It reveals why people often change their beliefs to match their behaviors, impacting decision-making, attitude change, and rationalization. This theory has significant implications for understanding human behavior in various fields such as law, investing, and business.

### Part 2: Two Empirical Studies with Measurement Scales

#### Study 1: The Classic Experiment
**Citation:** 
Festinger, L., & Carlsmith, J. M. (1959). Cognitive consequences of forced compliance. *Journal of Abnormal and Social Psychology, 58*(2), 203-210.

**Measurement Scales Used:**
- **Interval Scale:** Participants rated their experiences on an 11-point scale (from -5 to +5 for enjoyment, and 0 to 10 for learning value and scientific importance).

**Reasoning:**
These scales are interval because they have equal intervals between points but lack a true zero. A rating of "0" represents neutrality, not the complete absence of opinion. Thus, ratios cannot be meaningfully applied (e.g., a rating of 4 is not twice as positive as a rating of 2).

#### Study 2: Contemporary Research
**Citation:** 
Bran, A., & Vaidis, D. C. (2020). On the characteristics of the cognitive dissonance state: Exploration within the Pleasure Arousal Dominance model. *Psychologica Belgica, 60*(1), 86-102. https://doi.org/10.5334/pb.517

**Measurement Scales Used:**
- **Interval Scale:** The PAD (Pleasure-Arousal-Dominance) scale with 18 items rated on a 9-point bipolar scale from -4 to +4.
- **Ratio Scale:** Counting the number of specific behaviors participants recalled and reported.

**Reasoning:**
The PAD emotional rating scale is an interval scale because it has equal intervals but lacks a true zero. The count data, however, represents a ratio scale with a true zero (zero behaviors means none), equal intervals, and meaningful ratios.

### Additional Notes for Your Response
Understanding the measurement scales is crucial in psychology research as it determines the appropriate statistical analyses. Interval and ratio scales allow parametric tests like t-tests and ANOVAs, while nominal and ordinal scales typically require non-parametric tests.

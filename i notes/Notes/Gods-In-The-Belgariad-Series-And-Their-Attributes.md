# Gods in the Belgariad Series and Their Attributes

## Summary
This note provides a list of major gods from David Eddings' Belgariad series along with their attributes, and offers advice on how to start a magic fantasy story effectively.

## Key Points
- **Aldur**: Oldest god, creator of the Orb, associated with wisdom and sorcery.
- **Torak**: Initially beautiful, attempted to seize the Orb, god of Angaraks, associated with evil, greed, and scheming.
- **Belar**: God of Alorns (Chereks, Drasnians, Rivans), associated with feasting, lust, and warfare.
- **Chaldan**: God of Arends, depicted as a bull, associated with pride and tradition.
- **Nedra**: God of Tolnedrans, likely associated with wealth and commerce.
- **Issa**: God of Nyissans, depicted as a serpent, known to sleep for centuries, associated with the immortal Queen Salmissra.
- **Mara**: God of Marags, described as insane and constantly weeping, associated with the ghosts of his massacred people.

## Details
Each god in the Belgariad series has distinct attributes that shape their role in the narrative. For instance, Aldur is known for his wisdom and creation of the Orb, while Torak represents evil and scheming. The gods also have unique associations: Belar with feasting and warfare, Chaldan with pride and tradition, Nedra with commerce, Issa with immortality and the Queen Salmissra, and Mara with insanity and ghosts.

### Attributes of the Gods
- **Creation**: Each god except Aldur created their own race.
- **Non-intervention**: The gods generally cannot directly intervene in mortal affairs, with Torak being an exception.
- **Reflection**: People of each race tend to reflect the characteristics of their patron god.
- **Immortality**: The gods are immortal and possess immense power.
- **Prophecy**: The gods are involved in a grand prophecy that drives the events of the series.

# How to Start a Magic Fantasy Story

## Summary
This note offers advice on how to effectively start a magic fantasy story, including hooks, world-building, character introduction, and conflict setup.

## Key Points
- **Hook the Reader Immediately**: 
  - Start with action or an intriguing situation.
  - Example: "A mysterious light flickers in the night, drawing a young mage into danger."

- **Introduce the Fantasy World**:
  - Establish the setting early but avoid overwhelming details.
  - Example: "In the realm of Eldoria, where magic flows like water and ancient ruins hold secrets, a new hero emerges."

- **Introduce Key Characters**:
  - Focus on your main character early.
  - Show their personality through actions and dialogue.
  - Example: "Elena, a young apprentice mage with a fiery spirit, stumbles upon an artifact that changes her life forever."

- **Establish the Conflict**:
  - Hint at the central problem or quest.
  - Create tension by putting your protagonist under pressure or presenting them with a difficult choice.
  - Example: "As Elena delves deeper into the mystery of the artifact, she discovers it is key to saving her kingdom from an impending dark force."

- **Showcase the Magic**:
  - Demonstrate how magic works in your world.
  - Balance familiar and unique elements.
  - Example: "With a flick of her wrist, Elena conjures a shield of light, but soon realizes that mastering such spells comes with unexpected consequences."

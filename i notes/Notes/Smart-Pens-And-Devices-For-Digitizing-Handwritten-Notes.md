# Smart Pens and Devices for Digitizing Handwritten Notes

## Summary
This note provides an overview of devices and methods to convert handwritten notes into editable Word documents, including smart pens, reusable notebooks, tablet setups, and scan-and-convert apps.

## Key Points
- **Smart Pens**: Livescribe, SyncPen by NEWYES.
- **Reusable Notebooks**: Rocketbook, reMarkable 2.
- **Tablet + Stylus Setup**: Apple Pencil with iPad, Microsoft Word/OneNote ink tools.
- **Scan-and-Convert Apps**: Pen to Print.

## Details
To digitize handwritten notes and save them as editable text in a Word document, several devices and methods are available:

### Main Options

#### Smart Pens (Write on Paper, Get Digital Text)
- **Livescribe Smart Pen**: Uses special paper; your handwriting is captured as you write and synced to an app that can transcribe notes to editable text.
  - [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a3JGdWX483U)
  - [Website](https://us.livescribe.com)

- **SyncPen by NEWYES**: A pen with a built-in camera that tracks your strokes on dot-pattern paper or an LCD pad; the companion app converts handwriting (including some math/chemistry) and can export to formats like Microsoft Word, PDF, and JPG.
  - [Yankodesign](https://www.yankodesign.com/2020/05/23/a-sensor-inside-this-smart-pen-turns-all-your-handwritten-notes-into-editable-digital-text/)

#### Reusable / "Smart" Notebooks
- **Rocketbook**: Reusable notebook; you write with a compatible pen, then scan pages with the app and send them straight to cloud services like OneDrive, Google Drive, and OneNote.
  - [Website](https://getrocketbook.com)

- **reMarkable 2**: E-ink tablet that feels like paper; it converts your handwriting to text on the device and can send notes to Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive, or email, from which you can open or save them as Word docs.
  - [NY Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2019/05/31/technology/remarkable-2-review.html)

#### Tablet + Stylus Setups (No Special Pen Needed)
- **Apple Pencil with iPad**: Using Scribble and apps like Apple Notes or OneNote, you can write by hand and then use “copy as text” or “Ink to Text” to turn handwriting into typed text.
  - [Microsoft](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/explore-the-ink-to-text-pen-b1fcd978-e1a4-4111-86ed-21ff654c7555)
  - [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l1R28lbwW3I)

- **Microsoft Word / OneNote Ink Tools**: On Windows tablets or 2-in-1 laptops, the Ink to Text pen in Word and OneNote converts stylus handwriting to typed text directly in your document.

#### Scan-and-Convert Apps
- **Pen to Print**: Lets you scan pages of handwriting (from any notebook) and converts them to plain text, Word documents, or searchable PDFs.
  - [Website](https://www.pen-to-print.com/handwriting-to-text/)

## How to Pick for Your Workflow

- If you love real paper and want automatic capture: a smart pen like Livescribe or SyncPen plus their apps is the closest to "regular pen but everything ends up in Word."
- If you are okay with a tablet: an iPad + Apple Pencil or a Windows 2-in-1 with stylus is more flexible and integrates cleanly with Word and OneNote for school and design work.
- If you want cheap and simple: any notebook + a scan-and-convert app (Pen to Print, or Rocketbook with its app) gives you a basic pipeline to Word with minimal new hardware.

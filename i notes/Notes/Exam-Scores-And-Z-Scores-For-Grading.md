# Exam Scores and Z-Scores for Grading

## Summary
This note provides the z-scores for different exam scores to determine which should lead to a better grade.

## Key Points
- A score of X = 70 on an exam with µ = 82 and σ = 8, or a score of X = 60 on an exam with µ = 72 and σ = 12?
- A score of X = 85 on an exam with µ = 70 and σ = 10, or a score of X = 58 on an exam with µ = 49 and σ = 6?
- A score of X = 26 on an exam with µ = 20 and σ = 2, or a score of X = 32 on an exam with µ = 24 and σ = 4?

## Details
### Exam Score Comparisons

1. **70 vs. 60**
   - For X = 70, μ = 82, σ = 8: z = (70 - 82) / 8 = -1.50
   - For X = 60, μ = 72, σ = 12: z = (60 - 72) / 12 = -1.00
   - **Conclusion:** A score of 60 leads to the better grade.

2. **85 vs. 58**
   - For X = 85, μ = 70, σ = 10: z = (85 - 70) / 10 = 1.50
   - For X = 58, μ = 49, σ = 6: z = (58 - 49) / 6 = 1.50
   - **Conclusion:** Both scores produce the same grade.

3. **26 vs. 32**
   - For X = 26, μ = 20, σ = 2: z = (26 - 20) / 2 = 3.00
   - For X = 32, μ = 24, σ = 4: z = (32 - 24) / 4 = 2.00
   - **Conclusion:** A score of 26 leads to the better grade.

# Requirements for a Random Sample

## Summary
This note outlines the requirements that must be satisfied for a sample to be considered random.

## Key Points
- If more than one individual is selected, the probabilities stay constant for each individual selected.
- Each individual has an equal chance of being selected.

## Details
### Random Sample Requirements

For a random sample (in the simple introductory-stats sense), these are the requirements that must be satisfied:
1. **Constant Probability:** The probability of selecting any individual remains constant throughout the sampling process, even if more than one individual is selected.
2. **Equal Chance:** Each individual in the population has an equal chance of being included in the sample.

### Notes
The other two statements are not requirements for a random sample:
- "The proportions of all subgroups in the sample are equal to their proportions in the population."
- "The sample is drawn without replacement."

# Z-Score Classification for Central and Extreme Scores

## Summary
This note classifies scores as central or extreme based on their z-scores.

## Key Points
- A score of X = 85 in a population with µ = 75 and σ = 15 is **central**.
- A score of X = 85 in a population with µ = 80 and σ = 2 is **extreme**.
- A score of X = 85 in a population with µ = 90 and σ = 20 is **central**.
- A score of X = 85 in a population with µ = 93 and σ = 3 is **extreme**.

## Details
### Z-Score Calculations

1. **µ = 75, σ = 15, X = 85**
   - z = (85 - 75) / 15 ≈ 0.67 → Central

2. **µ = 80, σ = 2, X = 85**
   - z = (85 - 80) / 2 = 2.50 → Extreme

3. **µ = 90, σ = 20, X = 85**
   - z = (85 - 90) / 20 = -0.25 → Central

4. **µ = 93, σ = 3, X = 85**
   - z = (85 - 93) / 3 ≈ -2.67 → Extreme

# Differences in Processing Between Dorsal and Ventral Visual Pathways

## Summary
This note details the differences between the dorsal and ventral visual pathways, focusing on their roles in object recognition and spatial navigation.

## Key Points
- The **ventral stream** processes information for object identification and recognition.
- The **dorsal stream** handles spatial information and guides visually-controlled actions.
- Damage to the dorsal pathway can result in **optic ataxia**, impairing spatial tasks and hand-eye coordination.

## Details
The visual system is divided into two major pathways: the ventral stream, also known as the "what" pathway, and the dorsal stream, often referred to as the "where/how" pathway. These pathways originate from the primary visual cortex (V1) in the occipital lobe and extend to different regions of the brain.

### Processing Differences

- **Ventral Stream ("What" Pathway)**:
  - Specializes in object identification and recognition.
  - Processes features like shape, color, texture, and form.
  - Neurons respond selectively to visual features relevant for object identification.
  - Areas V4, TEO, and TE in the inferior temporal cortex are involved.
  - Constructs rich, detailed visual representations that allow us to identify objects.

- **Dorsal Stream ("Where/How" Pathway)**:
  - Primarily involved in processing spatial information and guiding visually-controlled actions.
  - Transforms visual information into coordinate frames necessary for skilled motor behavior.
  - Operates on moment-to-moment information for immediate action control.
  - Less influenced by subjective awareness compared to the ventral pathway.

### Friend with Dorsal Stream Damage (Optic Ataxia)

Imagine your friend has suffered damage to their posterior parietal cortex, affecting the dorsal stream. They would likely develop **optic ataxia**, characterized by specific deficits in visually guided reaching and movement control.

- **Daily Functioning Deficits**:
  - Significant difficulty with spatial tasks and coordinated movements.
  - Large errors when reaching for objects that aren't directly in front of them (e.g., trying to grab a coffee cup on the side of a desk without looking directly at it).
  - Hand-eye coordination tasks are severely impaired, such as opening their hand to maximum width when grasping objects rather than pre-shaping fingers appropriately.
  - Online correction of movements is affected; they struggle to adjust their reach mid-movement.

- **What Remains Intact**:
  - Ability to recognize and identify objects remains normal. They can easily tell you what a cup is, describe its features, etc.

# How to Form an LLC in California for a Software Development Company

## Summary
This note provides a step-by-step guide on forming an LLC in California specifically for a software development and technology company.

## Key Points
- **Step 1: Choose Your LLC Name**
  - Must be unique and include "Limited Liability Company," "LLC," or "L.L.C."
  - Search the California business name directory through the Secretary of State's Business Entities Search.
  - Avoid using words that suggest a government agency.

- **Step 2: Appoint a Registered Agent**
  - Required by law; must be at least 18 years old and have a physical street address in California.
  - Can appoint an individual or use professional registered agent services for privacy and reliability.

- **Step 3: File Articles of Organization**
  - Form LLC-1 with the California Secretary of State.
  - Include your LLC's name, business purpose, registered agent information, management structure, and signature.
  - Filing fee is $70 (plus an optional $5 for a certified copy).

- **Step 4: File Initial Statement of Information**
  - Within 90 days, file Form LLC-12 with the California Secretary of State.
  - Include principal business address, names and addresses of members or managers, registered agent information, and brief description of business activity.
  - Filing fee is $20.

- **Step 5: Create an Operating Agreement**
  - Required by law; includes ownership structure, management structure, capital contributions, profit distribution, voting rights, member roles, tax treatment, and dissolution procedures.
  - A written agreement is strongly recommended for legal protection and clarity.

- **Step 6: Obtain an Employer Identification Number (EIN)**
  - Recommended if you hire employees, elect to be taxed as a corporation, or want to open a business bank account.
  - Application takes about 15 minutes and is free; EIN received immediately upon completion.

## Details
Forming an LLC in California for a software development company involves several critical steps. The process begins with choosing a unique name that includes "Limited Liability Company," "LLC," or "L.L.C." and ensuring it's available through the Secretary of State's Business Entities Search. Next, appointing a registered agent is necessary; this can be an individual who meets specific requirements or a professional service for added privacy and reliability.

The Articles of Organization must then be filed with the California Secretary of State using Form LLC-1, which requires details such as your LLC’s name, business purpose, registered agent information, management structure, and signature. The filing fee is $70 (plus an optional $5 for a certified copy).

Within 90 days, you must file the Initial Statement of Information (Form LLC-12) to update your company records with the California Secretary of State. This form requires your principal business address, names and addresses of members or managers, registered agent information, and a brief description of business activity. The filing fee is $20.

Finally, creating an operating agreement is essential for defining ownership structure, management roles, capital contributions, profit distribution, voting rights, member roles, tax treatment, and dissolution procedures. A written agreement is strongly recommended to ensure legal protection and clarity.

For those who hire employees or have other specific needs, obtaining an Employer Identification Number (EIN) from the IRS is also advisable. The application process is straightforward and free, with immediate receipt of your EIN upon completion.

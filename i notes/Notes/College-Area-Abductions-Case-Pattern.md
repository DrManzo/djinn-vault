# College-Area Abductions Case Pattern

## Summary
This note outlines a fictional case pattern of college-area abductions for use in a game, focusing on the 1960s to early 1970s. The profiles and details are designed to challenge players with pre-DNA investigative techniques.

## Key Points
- **Case Pattern**: College-Area Abductions (1963-1964)
- **Core Situation**: Several students vanish within a 2–3 block radius of the university at night.
- **Common Elements**:
  - Victims: Late teens to mid-twenties, mix of genders.
  - Last-seen locations: Campus library closing time, off-campus bar, student parking structure.
  - Scene details: Belongings left behind (bags, books), no signs of prolonged struggle.

## Details
### Core Situation
Over three years, several students from a mid-sized urban campus vanish within a 2–3 block radius of the university at night. Each disappearance happens on a weekend, near bars, libraries, or parking lots. All cases went cold; JM now has the full file.

### Common Elements (What’s in the files)
- **Victim Profile**:
  - Late teens to mid-twenties.
  - Mix of genders, but similar traits: active on campus, late-night routines, often walking alone.
- **Last-seen Locations**:
  - Campus library closing time.
  - Off-campus bar near the college.
  - Student parking structure or side street with parallel parking.
- **Scene Details**:
  - Belongings left behind: bags, books, sometimes a jacket.
  - No signs of prolonged struggle; sometimes a dropped phone or scattered papers.
  - Security cameras partially helpful: cars passing, a person out of frame, but nothing conclusive.

### Offender Pattern (What JM can infer)
- **Social Approach + Pretext**:
  - Pretends to need help with something: carrying books, loading a car, giving directions.
  - Often appears with a small handicap or prop (arm in sling, using crutches, or "car trouble").
- **Comfortable Around Campus**:
  - Knows late-night patterns, blind spots in cameras, and security patrol routines.
  - Probably has or had legitimate reasons to be near the college (former student, employee, contractor, delivery, etc.).

### What Blocked the Original Investigation
- **Multiple Jurisdictions**: 
  - Campus police, city police, and sometimes county agencies each handled different disappearances.
- **Victim Lifestyle Framing**:
  - Some cases written off initially as "ran away," "party-related," or "personal issue," so links were missed.
- **Incomplete Witness Statements**:
  - Witness descriptions dismissed as "too vague" and never fully cross-compared.
  - Notes about someone with a sling, or similar car, are scattered across separate reports.

### How This Becomes Gameplay
In JM Ranger’s case file bundle, you include:

- 3–5 **disappearance reports** (one per victim).
- 1–2 **witness statement summaries** per case: 
  - "Saw them talking to a man with..." (prop, car type, clothing style).
- 1 **campus security memo** noting recurring suspicious vehicle or person near closing time.
- 1 **old internal memo** where a junior detective suggested a connection but was overruled or ignored.

Mechanically, you can let the player:

- Highlight or "pull" on recurring details (the sling, car type, location pattern).
- Spend JM’s limited mental resources (or alcohol) to cross-link those details across files.
- Gradually realize: this is not random; it’s a single offender using charm and a fake vulnerability.

## Victim Profiles
### Victim 1 — Sandra Kowalski, 19
- **Year missing:** 1963
- **Last seen:** Outside the campus library at 10:45 PM on a Friday.
- **Details**: Nursing student, known for staying late to study. Left her textbooks on a bench near the bike rack. A janitor saw her talking to a man who appeared to have his arm in a sling, describing him as "friendly-looking, maybe 30." Case was filed as a runaway after her estranged stepfather claimed she'd "done this before."
- **Physical Evidence**: One brown loafer found in the library stairwell; no signs of struggle.

### Victim 2 — Marcus Delray, 22
- **Year missing:** 1964
- **Last seen:** Side street adjacent to a college bar called The Rusty Tap, around 1:30 AM Saturday.
- **Details**: Junior, engineering major, part-time dishwasher at a nearby diner. Coworkers said a man outside offered to help him jump-start his car. Described as driving "a dark American sedan, wide body." Filed under "possible intoxication incident" since he'd been drinking. His wallet was found in a storm drain six months later — no cash, but his student ID was still inside.
- **Physical Evidence**: His jacket draped neatly over his car's side mirror.

# Faust — Creative Writing and Character Analysis

## Summary
Faust is a gnome necromancer character — small, frail, overlooked as a child, chosen by Nurul (God of Necromancers) after his great-great-great-great-grandmother's death. Given a coin that can animate the dead, Faust learns that the dead never truly leave but incorporate into the living. The character embodies the wounded healer archetype, evolving from using divine power as a crutch to understanding that love itself transcends death. Multiple adventure stories and a comprehensive psychological analysis were developed.

## Key Points
- Faust: small, frail gnome child overlooked by family, seen only by his ancient grandmother
- Nurul appears as gentle grandfather figure, not terrifying — offers coin that can call back shadows of the dead
- First test: grandmother's death — Faust chooses not to use the coin, recognizing what he'd raise wouldn't be her
- Core theme: the coin is a crutch; true wisdom is understanding the dead incorporate into the living
- Faust evolves into eccentric, mad old wounded healer — travels with mule named Sorrow, collects trinkets
- Adventure stories: Talking Bones of Whistling Gorge, Plague of Forgotten Names, Woman Who Collected Last Words, Merchant of Sorrows
- Psychological analysis from five poets (Blake, Dickinson, Rilke, Whitman, Eliot), Freud, Jung, Peterson, Machiavelli
- Nurul's transformation: god fades not from failure but from completion — Faust becomes the bridge between understanding and wisdom
- Sister (Life) perspective: Nurul was "divine training wheels," Faust teaches that love is not fragile

## Details
The Faust narrative is deeply personal mythology — a wounded healer who transforms grief into service, who carries paradox consciously (knowing everyone he loves will die, and loving them because of it). His trinket-collecting represents a healthy relationship with transitional objects — honoring memory without being enslaved by it. The confrontation with Malachar (Deathbinder) represents shadow integration — defeating not through power but through superior understanding. The ascension psychology shows ego death without self-destruction, universal consciousness without loss of personal warmth. The story mirrors the author's own transformation journey: physical change, shadow work, moving from observation to engagement, becoming whole through integration rather than suppression.

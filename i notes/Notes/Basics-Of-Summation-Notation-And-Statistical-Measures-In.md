# Basics of Summation Notation and Statistical Measures in Intro to Statistics and Probabilities for Psychology

## Summary
This note provides a foundational overview of key statistical concepts, including summation notation (ΣX), central tendency measures (mean, median, mode), frequency distributions, variance and standard deviation, and z-scores. It is designed to support learning in an introductory statistics course.

## Key Points
- **Summation Notation (ΣX)**: The Greek letter sigma indicates summing all values in a dataset.
- **Central Tendency Measures**:
  - **Mean**: Calculated as \( M = \frac{\sum X}{N} \).
  - **Median**: Middle value when data is ordered from lowest to highest.
  - **Mode**: Most frequently occurring value or class in the dataset.
- **Frequency Distributions**:
  - **Frequency Tables**: Show how many times each score appears.
  - **Cumulative Frequency**: Total number of scores at or below a particular value.
  - **Relative Frequency**: Percentage form of frequency, calculated as \(\frac{\text{frequency}}{N}\).
- **Variance and Standard Deviation**:
  - **Variance (σ² for population, s² for sample)**: Average squared deviation from the mean. Formula involves summing squared deviations.
  - **Standard Deviation**: Square root of variance, representing typical distance between a data point and the mean.
- **Standardized Scores (Z-scores)**:
  - Formula: \( z = \frac{X - M}{SD} \).
  - Positive Z-scores indicate above the mean; negative Z-scores below the mean.

## Details
Understanding these foundational concepts is crucial for analyzing data in psychology and other fields. The mean, median, and mode provide insights into central tendencies, while frequency distributions help visualize patterns. Variance and standard deviation measure dispersion, indicating how spread out scores are from the mean. Z-scores offer a standardized way to compare scores across different datasets.

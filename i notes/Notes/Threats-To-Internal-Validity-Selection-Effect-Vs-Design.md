# Threats to Internal Validity: Selection Effect vs. Design Confound

## Summary
This note addresses the differences between selection effect and design confound in research studies, focusing on their impact on internal validity.

## Key Points
- **Selection Effect (Selection Bias)**: This occurs when participants in different groups differ systematically before any treatment is applied.
- **Design Confound**: An additional variable that varies along with the independent variable but not necessarily before the study starts.

## Details
In psychological research, understanding threats to internal validity is crucial for ensuring accurate and reliable results. One such threat is the selection effect (also known as selection bias). This happens when participants in different groups are systematically different at the start of a study, leading to pre-existing group differences that can confound the results.

On the other hand, a design confound refers to an additional variable that varies along with the independent variable but does not necessarily create pre-existing group differences before the study begins. For example, if participants in one condition are more likely to be exposed to a particular environmental factor that influences the outcome, this could introduce a confounding variable.

# Null Confound

## Summary
This note explains the concept of a null confound, which is a variable that obscures or masks true associations between two variables.

## Key Points
- **Null Confound**: A compound that keeps a researcher from finding a relationship between two variables by obscuring or masking the true association.

## Details
A null confound refers to a situation where an additional variable (confounding variable) makes it difficult for researchers to identify a genuine relationship between the independent and dependent variables. This can happen when this variable is not properly controlled or accounted for in the study design, leading to misleading results.

# Manipulated Variable

## Summary
This note defines a manipulated variable as one that the researcher controls, also known as an independent variable.

## Key Points
- **Manipulated Variable**: A variable that the researcher manipulates or changes to observe its effect on another variable (dependent variable).

## Details
In experimental designs, researchers often manipulate variables to determine their impact. The manipulated variable is what the experimenter alters to see how it affects the dependent variable. Understanding this distinction helps in designing effective experiments and interpreting results accurately.

# Demand Characteristics

## Summary
This note discusses the concept of demand characteristics in research, which can influence participants' behavior due to their awareness of the study's purpose.

## Key Points
- **Demand Characteristics**: These occur when participants change their behavior based on what they think the researcher expects. Using objective measures like an EEG instead of self-reports helps mitigate this issue.

## Details
Dr. Dormeur’s decision to use an EEG to measure how long it took participants to fall asleep, rather than asking them directly, was aimed at reducing demand characteristics. This approach ensures that participants do not alter their behavior based on their assumptions about the study's purpose, thereby maintaining internal validity.

# Matched Group Design

## Summary
This note explains how matched group designs can address selection effects in research studies by ensuring groups are equivalent at baseline.

## Key Points
- **Matched Group Design**: This design matches participants based on key characteristics, reducing the impact of pre-existing differences between groups and addressing selection effects.

## Details
To address potential selection effects, researchers often use a matched group design. In this approach, participants in different conditions are matched based on relevant characteristics (e.g., age, gender, prior experience) to ensure that any observed differences can be attributed to the experimental manipulation rather than pre-existing differences.

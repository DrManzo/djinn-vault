# Navigating Workplace Accountability and Mistakes

## Summary
This note provides a structured approach to address workplace accountability issues, focusing on communication, documentation, and maintaining professional boundaries.

## Key Points
- Acknowledge your part in the mistake.
- Document your process thoroughly.
- Refuse to take full responsibility unless your boss also takes accountability.
- Communicate clearly with your boss about expectations and responsibilities.

## Details
The situation involves a mistake in an estimate due to incomplete or incorrect notes from your boss. Here’s how you can navigate this:

1. **Acknowledge Your Part:**
   - While you believe your actions were based on your boss's notes, it is important to acknowledge where you could have been more proactive (e.g., double-checking the notes or raising concerns about discrepancies). This demonstrates accountability without taking full blame.

2. **Document Your Process:**
   - Keep a detailed record of the steps you took while creating the estimate and drawing. Document any communications with your boss, including emails, notes, and drawings.
   - Ensure that these records are clear and comprehensive to support your position if needed.

3. **Refuse Full Responsibility:**
   - Refuse to take full accountability unless your boss also takes responsibility for the oversight in his notes or for sending the estimate without proper review.
   - Communicate this stance clearly with your boss, emphasizing that you followed the provided instructions but recognize areas where improvements could be made.

4. **Communicate Clearly:**
   - Have an open and honest conversation with your boss about expectations and responsibilities. Ensure both parties are on the same page regarding communication and quality control.
   - Suggest regular check-ins to avoid similar issues in the future, ensuring that all relevant information is accurately documented and reviewed before finalizing any documents.

5. **Seek Support:**
   - If you feel overwhelmed or need accommodations due to ADHD, consider bringing documentation of your condition to discuss necessary adjustments with HR or your manager.
   - Utilize available resources such as workplace policies on accountability and communication to guide your actions.

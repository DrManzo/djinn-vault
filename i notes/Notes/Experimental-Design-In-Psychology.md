# Experimental Design in Psychology

## Summary
The content discusses the fundamental principles and steps involved in designing an effective experimental study within the field of psychology.

## Key Points
- **Hypothesis Formulation**: Clearly define what you are testing.
- **Independent Variable (IV)**: The variable manipulated by the researcher to observe its effect on the dependent variable.
- **Dependent Variable (DV)**: The outcome that is measured in response to changes in the independent variable.
- **Control Group**: A group used as a baseline for comparison, where no experimental treatment is applied.
- **Experimental Group**: The group subjected to the experimental treatment.

## Details
In psychology research, an experiment is designed to test hypotheses by manipulating one or more variables and observing their effects. Key components include:

1. **Hypothesis Formulation**: Before conducting any experiments, researchers must clearly define what they are testing. This involves making a prediction about the relationship between two or more variables.
2. **Independent Variable (IV)**: The variable that is manipulated by the researcher to observe its effect on the dependent variable. It should be controlled and varied systematically.
3. **Dependent Variable (DV)**: The outcome that is measured in response to changes in the independent variable. This is what researchers aim to understand or predict.
4. **Control Group**: A group used as a baseline for comparison, where no experimental treatment is applied. This helps isolate the effect of the independent variable.
5. **Experimental Group**: The group subjected to the experimental treatment. Changes in this group are compared against the control group to determine the effectiveness of the intervention.

# Empirical Articles for Causal Claims Assignment

## Summary
This note provides two options for empirical articles that fit the requirements of an assignment on summarizing and interrogating causal claims using a three claims/four validities framework.

## Key Points
- **Option 1**: Sherman, L. W., & Berk, R. A. (1984). "The specific deterrent effects of arrest for domestic assault."
  - **Causal Claim**: Arresting individuals accused of domestic assault causes a reduction in repeat offenses compared to non-arrest interventions.
  - **Why It Fits**:
    - Randomized controlled trial design with police responses as the independent variable.
    - Explicitly argues for causality by meeting association, temporal precedence, and non-spuriousness criteria.

- **Option 2**: Duncan, G. J., & Magnuson, K. (2013). "The long-term effects of poor childhood health: An intervention study."
  - **Causal Claim**: Childhood health disparities cause long-term differences in adult educational and economic outcomes.
  - **Why It Fits**:
    - Uses longitudinal data and quasi-experimental methods to isolate causal effects.
    - Employs causal language and addresses construct validity.

## Details
Both articles explicitly use empirical data and causal language, making them suitable for the assignment. Key considerations include:

1. **Empirical Basis**: Both articles involve original data collection/analysis (RCT in Option 1; longitudinal surveys in Option 2).
2. **Causal Framing**: Explicitly use verbs like "causes," "produces," or "reduces" to assert causality.
3. **Validity Analysis**:
   - **Internal Validity**: How well the study design rules out alternative explanations (e.g., randomization in Option 1).
   - **Construct Validity**: Whether variables accurately measure theoretical concepts.

## References
- Sherman, L. W., & Berk, R. A. (1984). *The specific deterrent effects of arrest for domestic assault*. American Sociological Review, 49(2), 261–272.
- Duncan, G. J., & Magnuson, K. (2013). *The long-term effects of poor childhood health: An intervention study*. Child Development, 84(2), 539–554.

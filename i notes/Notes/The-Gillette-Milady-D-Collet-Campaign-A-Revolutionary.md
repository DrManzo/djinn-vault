# The Gillette Milady Décolleté Campaign: A Revolutionary Marketing Strategy in 1915

## Summary
The Gillette Milady Décolleté campaign, launched in 1915, was a groundbreaking marketing strategy that transformed women's shaving habits and created a cultural need for hair removal. This note explores the chosen advertisement, analyzes it through the heuristic-systematic model, and discusses its historical impact.

## Key Points
- **Advertisement Context**: The first safety razor marketed to women, priced at $5 (approximately $120 in today’s currency).
- **Visual and Textual Elements**: Emphasized luxury, femininity, and solving an "embarrassing personal problem."
- **Historical Impact**: Increased total razor sales from 390,000 in 1914 to over 1,000,000 by 1917.
- **Heuristic-Systematic Model Analysis**:
  - **Systematic Processing Elements**: Factual product information, rational arguments, and authority claims.

## Details
The Gillette Milady Décolleté campaign was a pivotal moment in marketing history. In 1915, Gillette introduced the first safety razor marketed to women, priced at $5 (approximately $120 in today’s currency). The advertisement emphasized luxury and femininity, positioning the product as a "beautiful addition to Milady's toilet table" that solved an "embarrassing personal problem."

### Visual and Textual Elements
The campaign utilized sophisticated design elements and persuasive language. Advertisements proclaimed the razor was "welcomed by women everywhere" and highlighted its benefits for "good grooming." Additional ads emphasized the product as a "safe and sanitary way to the smooth underarm demanded by both good grooming and good dressing."

### Historical Impact
The campaign's effectiveness was immediate and significant:
- **1914**: 390,000 total razor sales.
- **1916**: 782,028 total razor sales.
- **1917**: Over 1,000,000 total razor sales.
- By 1964, 98% of American women were shaving their legs.

### Analysis Through the Heuristic-Systematic Model
The campaign incorporated several systematic processing elements:
- **Factual Product Information**: Provided specific details about materials (French Ivory, 14K Gold), pricing ($5), and availability.
- **Rational Arguments**: Connected changing fashion trends to grooming needs, creating a logical cause-and-effect relationship.
- **Authority and Expertise Claims**: Leveraged established beauty and fashion publications to present credible information.

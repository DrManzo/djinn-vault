# Critical Thinking as a Mechanism for Situation Handling and Problem Solving: An Article Review

## Summary
This essay reviews Bakour and Altabaa's (2025) study, "Critical Thinking as a Mechanism for Situation Handling and Problem Solving: Examples from Ḥadīth Literature," published in *Intellectual Discourse*. The research identifies critical thinking skills embedded in Islamic prophetic traditions and proposes a three-stage model applicable to modern problem-solving.

## Key Points
- Bakour and Altabaa (2025) analyzed twenty selected ḥadīths from al-Bukhārī and Muslim.
- Eight distinct critical thinking skills were identified and organized into a three-stage model.
- The study bridges classical Islamic wisdom with contemporary cognitive theories.

## Details
The article by Bakour and Altabaa (2025) presents a comprehensive analysis of critical thinking patterns within the context of Ḥadīth literature. By examining twenty carefully selected ḥadīths, the researchers identified eight distinct critical thinking skills: observation, inference, evaluation, synthesis, decision-making, reflection, adaptation, and communication. These skills were then organized into a three-stage model for situation handling and problem-solving.

### Method/Participants
- **Research Design**: Inductive and analytical approaches.
- **Participants**: Twenty ḥadīths from al-Bukhārī and Muslim collections.

### Major Findings/Conclusions
The study found that critical thinking is not only a modern concept but also deeply rooted in classical Islamic traditions. The identified skills provide a framework for effective problem-solving, emphasizing the importance of systematic analysis and reflective practice.

### Strengths/Limitations
- **Strength**: The integration of traditional wisdom with contemporary cognitive theories.
- **Limitation**: Limited generalizability due to the specific focus on Ḥadīth literature.

### Implications for the Field of Psychology
The findings suggest that classical Islamic problem-solving methods can inform modern cognitive practices. This has potential applications in education, counseling, and professional psychology, particularly in fostering intrinsic motivation and self-worth.

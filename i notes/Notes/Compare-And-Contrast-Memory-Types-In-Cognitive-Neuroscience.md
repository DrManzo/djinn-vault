# Compare and Contrast Memory Types in Cognitive Neuroscience

## Summary
This note provides a comparison of four types of memory discussed in Chapter 9 of *Cognitive Neuroscience: The Biology of the Mind* (6th ed.), including sensory, short-term/working, long-term nondeclarative, and long-term declarative memory.

## Key Points
- **Sensory Memory:** Brief duration (milliseconds to seconds), high capacity, non-conscious.
- **Short-Term / Working Memory:** Limited capacity (4–7 items), conscious awareness, brief duration (seconds to minutes).
- **Long-Term Nondeclarative Memory:** Long-term storage, implicit, automatic performance.
- **Long-Term Declarative Memory:** Conscious access and retrieval of facts and events.

## Details
The four types of memory differ in their duration, capacity, and level of conscious awareness. Sensory memory captures incoming sensory data briefly; short-term/working memory maintains information for current tasks; long-term nondeclarative memory stores skills and habits; and long-term declarative memory retains explicit knowledge and personal experiences.

### 1. Sensory Memory
- **Duration:** Milliseconds to seconds.
- **Capacity:** High (captures nearly all sensory input).
- **Awareness:** Non-conscious.
- **Function:** Buffers incoming sensory data, allowing continuous perception.

**Example:**
- You wave a sparkler at night and see a continuous trailing circle of light. This is your iconic sensory memory holding the image for a split second after the sparkler has moved.

### 2. Short-Term (Working) Memory
- **Duration:** Seconds to minutes.
- **Capacity:** Limited (4–7 items).
- **Awareness:** Conscious.
- **Function:** Maintains and manipulates information for current tasks.

**Example:**
- You calculate a tip in your head by holding the number "$4.50" in your mind while performing addition, demonstrating working memory.

### 3. Long-Term Nondeclarative Memory
- **Duration:** Long-term (can last a lifetime).
- **Capacity:** Theoretically unlimited.
- **Awareness:** Non-conscious.
- **Function:** Skills, habits, and conditioned responses expressed through performance.

**Example:**
- Riding a bicycle after years of not riding; your body automatically balances and steers without conscious recall.

### 4. Long-Term Declarative Memory
- **Duration:** Minutes to decades.
- **Capacity:** Theoretically unlimited.
- **Awareness:** Conscious.
- **Function:** Storage and retrieval of facts and events, divided into episodic (personal experiences) and semantic (general knowledge).

**Example:**
- Recalling exactly what you did for your last birthday—where you were and who was there—is an example of episodic memory.

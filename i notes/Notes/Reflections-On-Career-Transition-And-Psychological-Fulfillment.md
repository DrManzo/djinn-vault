# Reflections on Career Transition and Psychological Fulfillment

## Summary
This note explores the psychological and Stoic perspectives on transitioning from a known, respected role in carpentry to an uncertain but potentially more fulfilling role in psychology and interior design. It provides insights into self-efficacy, identity transition, nostalgia, acceptance, and virtue.

## Key Points
- **Self-Efficacy and Competence**: The sense of mastery and control provided by previous roles.
- **Identity Transition**: Navigating a shift in professional identity can be challenging.
- **Nostalgia and Idealization**: Common to idealize past experiences while overlooking their difficulties.
- **Focus on What You Can Control**: Concentrate on developing skills and knowledge in your new field.
- **Virtue as the Highest Good**: Practicing virtues like wisdom, justice, courage, and self-control.
- **Accepting Change**: Embrace change as a natural part of life.

## Details
Your feelings about missing your previous role as a carpenter/apprentice are completely valid and understandable from both psychological and Stoic perspectives. Here’s how you can draw strength and wisdom:

### Psychological Perspective

1. **Self-Efficacy and Competence**: As a carpenter, you had a strong sense of self-efficacy and competence in your craft. This knowledge and skill provided a sense of mastery and control, which are crucial for psychological well-being.
2. **Identity Transition**: Changing careers often involves a shift in professional identity. This transition can be challenging as you move from a role where you felt established and respected to one where you're still developing expertise.
3. **Nostalgia and Idealization**: It's common to experience nostalgia for past roles, especially when facing challenges in a new position. This can lead to idealizing the past and overlooking its difficulties.

### Stoic Perspective

1. **Focus on What You Can Control**: The Stoics emphasized concentrating on what’s within our control. In your case, this would mean focusing on developing your skills and knowledge in your new field rather than dwelling on past roles.
2. **Virtue as the Highest Good**: Stoicism teaches that virtue (wisdom, justice, courage, and self-control) is the highest good. Your current role, while challenging, may offer new opportunities to practice these virtues.
3. **Accepting Change**: Stoics advocated for accepting change as a natural part of life. Your career transition is an opportunity to practice this acceptance and adaptability.

### Reconciling Your Feelings

1. **Acknowledge the Learning Process**: Recognize that you're in a learning phase in your new role. With time and effort, you can develop the same level of expertise and recognition you had as a carpenter.
2. **Find Meaning in Your New Role**: Explore how your current work in interior design and sales contributes to others' lives. This can help build a sense of purpose and fulfillment.
3. **Apply Transferable Skills**: Identify skills from your carpentry experience that apply to your current role, such as attention to detail, problem-solving, and customer service.
4. **Practice Gratitude**: Reflect on the positive aspects of your new career path, including opportunities for growth and new challenges.
5. **Maintain Connection to Craft**: Consider maintaining a connection to carpentry through hobbies or volunteer work, allowing you to enjoy the craft while pursuing your new career.

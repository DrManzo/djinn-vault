# Holland Code Career Test and Personal Identity

## Summary
This post discusses the results of a Holland Code Career Test, focusing on Building as the primary interest area. It explores how careers can shape personal identity and provides insights into two potential career paths based on test results.

## Key Points
- Primary interests: Building (100), Persuading (95), Organizing (86)
- Top career choice: Architectural or Engineering Manager
- Lowest career choice: Not specified in the provided data

## Details
The Holland Code Career Test revealed that my primary interest area is Building, followed by Persuading and Organizing. This suggests a strong inclination towards environments where hands-on work, physical engagement, and tangible results are valued.

### Top Career Choice: Architectural or Engineering Manager
**Why it fits:** As an Architectural or Engineering Manager, this role aligns perfectly with my interest in Building. The career involves designing and overseeing the construction of buildings, which allows for significant hands-on involvement and a sense of accomplishment from seeing projects come to life.

**Average Earnings: $152,350**
- According to the test results, the average earning potential is high, making this a financially viable option.
- **Source:** [Cavanaugh, J. C. (2024). Adult development and aging (9th ed.). Cengage Learning. ISBN-13: 9780357796276]

### Lowest Career Choice
The lowest career choice is not specified in the provided data.

## Conclusion
Careers play a crucial role in shaping our identity by providing structure, purpose, and a sense of accomplishment. Based on my Holland Code test results, Architectural or Engineering Manager stands out as an ideal fit due to its alignment with my interests and potential for financial stability. This career path allows me to engage in hands-on work while contributing to the design and construction of buildings.

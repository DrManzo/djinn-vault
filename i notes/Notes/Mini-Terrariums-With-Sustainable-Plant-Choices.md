# Mini Terrariums with Sustainable Plant Choices

## Summary
This note provides guidance on creating mini terrariums, focusing on sustainability and odor-free design. It covers container selection, plant choices, and design considerations for both open and closed systems.

## Key Points
- **Container Design**: Closed containers need a sealed lid; open ones require proper drainage.
- **Plant Selection**: Focus on low-maintenance plants that regulate humidity and prevent overwatering.
- **Sustainable Practices**: Avoid common odor-causing issues like overwatering, no drainage layer, and wrong soil.

## Details
Mini terrariums are forgiving DIY projects, especially closed ones. Key elements include:
- **Drainage Layer**: Pebbles or gravel at the bottom to prevent root rot.
- **Moisture Barrier**: Optional mesh or sphagnum moss between the drainage and soil.
- **Soil Selection**: Specific to plant needs—succulents need fast-draining, tropical plants require peat-based.

### Container Design
**Closed Terrarium:**
- Needs a sealed lid for humidity control.
- Taller profile helps with condensation effects.
- Vent hole option using printed plugs.

**Open Terrarium:**
- Wider mouth and shorter walls.
- Drainage is crucial—consider two-part designs (inner mesh liner, outer shell).

### Shared Smart Move
Design one base module that both container types can snap onto. This saves time and ensures modular design.

#### Materials
- **PETG**: Good for closed terrariums due to its humidity-holding properties.
- **PLA**: Suitable for open terrariums as it doesn't need sustained moisture exposure.

### Plant Choices
For a sustainable, odor-free terrarium:
- **Moss (cushion, sheet, or mood moss)**: Regulates humidity and doesn’t rot easily.
- **Baby Tears (Soleirolia)**: Thrives in high humidity with low decay risk.
- **Button Fern / Small Maidenhair Fern**: Hardy, slow-growing, and low waste.
- **Small Pothos or Peperomia Cuttings**: Nearly indestructible and air-purifying.

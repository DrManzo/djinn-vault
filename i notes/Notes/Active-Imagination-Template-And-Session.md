# Active Imagination Template And Session

## Summary
Establishment of an Active Imagination session template inspired by Jungian practice, modeled after the creation of a personal "Black Book" and eventual "Red Book." A session is analyzed in which the author allowed the "ancient" aspect to take control during a conversation, revealing deep internal dynamics around acceptance, vulnerability, and the struggle between the warrior, the child, and the ancient.

## Key Points
- Process framed as a personal journey toward creating a "Black Book" and eventually a "Red Book" in the Jungian tradition
- Active Imagination template includes: Entry Point, Dialogue/Imagery, Emotional Response, Symbolism & Archetypes, Artistic Response, Integration & Reflection
- The "ancient" holds old pain, self-sabotage, and a tendency to keep wounds open rather than allow healing
- Tension exists between the disciplined warrior aspect and the wounded child/ancient who longs for acceptance but fears it
- The author struggles to trust comfort offered, admitting they have not had arms that "just accept me and say it's okay"
- Shame and trust issues rooted in past experiences where giving fully led to disappointment or betrayal
- The child is described as "twice as deadly" — its unmet needs are the root of the ancient's power
- Integration strategy: listen to both voices but let neither rule; negotiate a truce between them
- The child teaches the ancient compassion; the ancient teaches the child discernment
- Enhanced template suggestions include: Intention Setting, Embodied Experience, Dialogue with Parts, Shadow & Light, Integration Ritual
- Jung's transcendent function emerges when opposites collide and a new path is born

## Details
The author establishes a structured Active Imagination template for their Black Book, drawing on Jungian methodology. In the analyzed session, allowing the "ancient" to take control during a conversation with Tia Epley revealed a profound internal dynamic. The ancient represents conditioned defenses — shame, resentment, and old survival rules. The child embodies unmet needs — vulnerability, longing for acceptance, and raw emotional truth. The conversation exposes the depth of longing for acceptance, barriers created by past pain, and the slow process of learning to trust both others and oneself. The ancient's voice is not merely a saboteur but a guardian of hard-earned lessons. The recommended approach is to let the two voices transform each other rather than obeying either one.

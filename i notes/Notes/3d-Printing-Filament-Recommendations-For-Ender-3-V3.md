# 3D Printing Filament Recommendations for Ender 3 V3 Plus

## Summary
This note provides recommendations and details on filament types suitable for the Ender 3 V3 Plus 3D printer.

## Key Points
- **PLA**: Good for beginners, easy to print with minimal maintenance.
- **ABS**: Strong and durable but requires more preparation due to warping issues.
- **PETG**: A good balance between PLA and ABS; less prone to warping and easier to handle.
- **TPE/TPU**: Flexible filaments suitable for making flexible parts like grips or hoses.

## Details
The Ender 3 V3 Plus is a popular entry-level 3D printer. To ensure optimal performance, it's crucial to choose the right filament type based on your project requirements and experience level.

### PLA (Polylactic Acid)
- **Pros**: Easy to print with minimal maintenance; good for simple designs.
- **Cons**: Not as strong or flexible as other filaments; prone to warping at high temperatures.

### ABS (Acrylonitrile Butadiene Styrene)
- **Pros**: Strong and durable, suitable for functional parts like enclosures.
- **Cons**: Requires careful preparation due to warping issues. Needs a heated bed and proper cooling after printing.

### PETG (Polyethylene Terephthalate Glycol)
- **Pros**: A good balance between PLA and ABS; less prone to warping and easier to handle.
- **Cons**: Slightly more difficult to print with than PLA due to its higher melting point.

### TPE/TPU (Thermoplastic Elastomers)
- **Pros**: Flexible, suitable for making flexible parts like grips or hoses.
- **Cons**: Not as strong as ABS; may not be ideal for functional parts that require durability.

# Ender 3 V3 Plus Filament Preparation Guide

## Summary
This guide outlines the necessary steps to prepare and handle filament for optimal printing on an Ender 3 V3 Plus.

## Key Points
- **Heated Bed**: Essential for preventing warping, especially with ABS.
- **Filament Diameter**: Ensure consistent diameter (1.75mm or 2.85mm) to avoid clogging.
- **Filament Spool Handling**: Use a filament spool holder to keep the filament organized and tangle-free.

## Details
Proper preparation of your filament is crucial for achieving successful prints on an Ender 3 V3 Plus. Here are some essential steps:

### Heated Bed Setup
1. **Temperature Settings**: Set the heated bed temperature according to the filament type.
   - **PLA**: Typically around 60°C (140°F).
   - **ABS**: Around 105-120°C (221-248°F).
   - **PETG**: Around 70-90°C (158-194°F).

2. **Bed Adhesion**: Ensure the heated bed is clean and properly leveled to prevent warping.

### Filament Diameter
Ensure your filament has a consistent diameter of either 1.75mm or 2.85mm. This consistency helps in avoiding clogging issues during printing.

### Spool Handling
- **Filament Spool Holder**: Use a spool holder to keep the filament organized and tangle-free.
- **Filament Storage**: Store your filament in a cool, dry place to maintain its quality.

# Trial Balance and Order Structure for Accounting Systems

## Summary
This note outlines the structure of a trial balance in accounting systems, focusing on adjusting entries related to supplies.

## Key Points
- **Trial Balance**: A summary of all accounts with debit and credit balances.
- **Adjusting Entries**: Necessary adjustments made at the end of an accounting period.
- **Supplies Account**: Tracks the cost of materials used by a business.

## Details
The trial balance is a crucial component in ensuring the accuracy of financial statements. It provides a summary of all accounts, including their debit and credit balances. Adjusting entries are necessary to ensure that the financial records reflect the true economic position at the end of an accounting period.

### Trial Balance Structure
- **Accounts**: List of all accounts with their respective debit and credit balances.
- **Total Debits and Credits**: Ensure the total debits equal the total credits for accuracy.

### Adjusting Entries for Supplies
1. **Supplies Account**:
   - Track the cost of materials used by a business.
   - Example: If $500 worth of supplies were used during the period, debit the Cost of Goods Sold and credit the Supplies account.

2. **Prepaid Expenses**: 
   - Adjust prepaid expenses to recognize them as expenses when they are used up.

3. **Accrued Revenues and Expenses**:
   - Recognize revenues earned and expenses incurred but not yet recorded.

# The Sentinel's Plea

There was a man  
so sad and low  
that he looked at his hands and said:  
Thus I have lived,  
and no god has claimed my suffering.

So in defiance, he lived—  
lived through the flood,  
lived through the plagues.  
To many, he was the one.  
To him, he was just looking  
for a reason to die,  
a reason to live.

But no.

Until  
he saw a star,  
dying but being born.  
He neither wanted to,  
nor did he want,  
but he loved the motion—  
the being that with explosive energy  
showered the world,  
his world.

And yet through the years  
he always stood as a sentinel,  
always ready,  
but yet always just a symbol.

Can I say  
that when it comes to us,  
I want you to take the step—  
because until now  
I have always been yours:  
my love,  
my adoration,  
and my guilt.

If I am to be yours,  
then my soul belongs to you,  
and my life, as ethereal as it is,  
will never waver in the defense  
of the one I love.  
And if that is you,  
and you see the truth within you,  
then I have no hope  
but to surrender my life, love, and sovereignty  
to the divine and what is holy—  
and that will be you,  
the paramount soul of my entity.

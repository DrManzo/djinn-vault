# Story Chapter Three Planning

## Summary
Chapter 3 planning covers the narrative structure for "What the Empire Breeds" (Book 1 of The Dominion's Rise trilogy), including POV rotation, character development, thematic consistency, and the deliberate delay of the Raxz-Arctus confrontation to build tension across multiple chapters.

## Key Points
- Book Title: What the Empire Breeds (Book 1), Series: The Dominion's Rise (planned trilogy), Setting: The Dominion of Pyraxis
- Genre: Epic fantasy with political intrigue and queer representation; comp titles include The Traitor Baru Cormorant, The Priory of the Orange Tree, A Memory Called Empire
- Narrative style: Third-person limited with deep immersion, clean evocative prose grounded in sensory detail, filter words removed for immediacy
- Core themes: Duty vs. Desire, Cages (golden cages literal and metaphorical), Performance vs. Authenticity, Queer Identity in Rigid Systems, What the Empire Breeds
- Raxz (21, Prince Regent): Bisexual, in love with mentor Javelin, arranged courtship with Lady Marcella Kalvennor, impulsive and politically naive
- Javelin (21, mentor): Bisexual, returns Raxz's feelings but maintains strict boundaries, son of Brax (High Steward), under surveillance by Faust
- Brax (Javelin's father, High Steward): Manages imperial household, trusted by Emperor, potential conflict as his son loves the Prince
- Lady Marcella Kalvennor (21): Lesbian with secret female lovers, politically sophisticated, covered for Raxz's dress change with grace
- Emperor Arctus: Stopped smiling 15 years ago after wars and failed pregnancies, burdened and aging, pragmatic about empire's survival
- Ellen (20, second-born): Military diplomat, more capable than Raxz, sees through his performance
- Theo (18, third-born): Ambassador with Fang-level training, the watcher and information broker
- Lance (7) and Millie (5): Youngest siblings, post-war babies; Millie's silver hair triggers Raxz's memory of Javelin's warning about golden cages
- Chapter 3 direction: Delay the office confrontation for 1-2 chapters to build unbearable tension, using Javelin's POV, then Marcella's or Ellen's POV, before the confrontation
- Writing guidelines: Remove filter words, vary sentence rhythm, ground emotions in physical sensation, use italics for direct internal thought
- Dialogue guidelines: Distinct voices, subtext over text, courtly formality vs. private intimacy
- Pacing: Scenes should breathe (800-1500 words), end chapters on emotional beats not cliffhanger gimmicks, allow quiet moments between high tension

## Details
The chapter planning establishes a deliberate pacing strategy: rather than resolving the Raxz-Arctus confrontation immediately, the narrative cuts away to other POVs, allowing the tension to build while revealing how other characters perceive and react to the gala catastrophe. This multi-POV approach shows the ripple effects of Raxz's actions across the Dominion's power structure. Javelin's POV reveals his internal conflict between love and duty. Marcella's POV shows the political damage from the Kalvennor perspective. Ellen's POV provides a clear-eyed assessment of her brother's failings. The writing guidelines emphasize immediacy and sensory grounding, removing filter words that distance readers from character experience. The thematic throughline — what the empire breeds in those who serve it — connects every POV, showing how the system shapes and warps each character differently.

# Finance Course Documents

## Summary
Comprehensive reference for Principles of Finance and Personal Finance coursework covering corporate finance fundamentals, capital cost calculations, WACC analysis, personal budgeting, saving and investing strategies, financial instruments, and risk management. Includes worked examples for asset turnover ratios, weighted average inventory costing, and multi-company WACC comparisons.

## Key Points
- Finance is the study of funds management and asset allocation over time, maximizing value considering time value of money and risk
- Corporate finance focuses on investment and financing decisions, including capital budgeting, capital structure, and working capital management
- The three main areas of finance are personal finance, corporate finance, and public finance
- WACC (Weighted Average Cost of Capital) determines whether an investment opportunity is viable; expected return must exceed WACC
- DCF (Discounted Cash Flow) approach considers existing and future resources to make optimal investment decisions
- CAPM focuses on risk and return relationships; BYPRP builds up a risk premium for cost of capital estimation
- Asset turnover ratio is calculated as Net Sales divided by Total Assets
- Rate of return on sales formula is Net Income divided by Net Sales
- Trial balance order: balance sheet accounts (assets most to least liquid), liabilities (short to long term), owner's equity, income statement accounts, sum of debits and credits
- Transferring revenue and expenses into equity occurs during the Close phase of the accounting cycle
- Weighted average inventory method is best suited for businesses with lower turnover and high-value items (e.g., construction equipment dealers)
- Purchasing land for business construction is classified as an investing activity on the statement of cash flows
- Personal finance covers budgeting, saving, investing, borrowing, credit management, insurance, and retirement planning
- Emergency funds are a foundational element of personal financial planning
- The investment pyramid illustrates the trade-off between risk and potential returns
- Bonds provide fixed income and portfolio diversification with defined face value, maturity date, and coupon rate
- Mutual funds offer professional management and diversification through pooled investments
- Budget allocation should balance income against expenditures with a dedicated savings component
- Excel pie charts and column charts are effective for visualizing budget category allocations
- Management by Objectives (MBO) is a planning and control technique applicable to financial decision making
- An account increase occurs on the same side as its natural (normal) balance, not the opposite

## Details
Corporate finance decision-making centers on two fundamental types: investment decisions (spending capital on assets yielding highest returns) and financing decisions (determining how to fund investments through internal funds or external capital raising). The ultimate goal is maximizing shareholder value while balancing short-term and long-term considerations.

WACC calculations require the market value of equity, market value of debt, cost of equity, cost of debt, and tax rate. The formula weights each component by its proportion of total capital, with debt cost adjusted for the tax shield: WACC = (We * Re) + (Wd * Rd * (1 - T)). An investment should only be pursued if its expected return exceeds the company's WACC.

Personal finance education progresses through budget creation and management, savings and investing fundamentals, borrowing and credit understanding, and insurance and risk management. Budget tables should allocate income across housing, food, transportation, education, utilities, taxes, health care, family care, miscellaneous, and savings categories, with percentages calculated relative to total income.

Financial instruments covered include bonds (contractual loans to corporations and governments with defined terms), mutual funds (professionally managed pooled investments with active or passive strategies), and various personal loan types. The investment pyramid provides a framework for understanding risk-return tradeoffs across different asset classes.
